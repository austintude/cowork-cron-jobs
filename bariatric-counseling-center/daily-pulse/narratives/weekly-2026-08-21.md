# Weekly briefing -- 2026-08-21

### The window in one sentence

The week of August 13--19 was defined almost entirely by a single anomalous traffic event on August 16, which drove 476 sessions on its own -- roughly 71% of the week's total volume. Strip that day out and the underlying baseline sits around 28--35 sessions per day, which is modestly above the 28-day median but unremarkable on its own.

---

### Volume + composition

The 7-day window produced 669 total sessions, 655 users, and 1,480 pageviews, with a reported per-day average of 95.6 sessions. That average is misleading. Remove August 16 and the daily average for the remaining six days is roughly 32 sessions -- still slightly above the site's 28-day median of 18--24, but not meaningfully so.

GSC data shows zero impressions and zero clicks across the entire window. This is a data gap, not a real-world signal -- GSC typically lags 2--3 days and may have a feed issue worth confirming. No organic search signal means we cannot evaluate whether rankings moved or whether any content is gaining traction in Google.

Channel composition for the week was heavily skewed Direct at 562 sessions (84% of total), against BCC's expected baseline of roughly 60%. Organic Search contributed 78 sessions (12%), which is below the expected 25% share. Referral, Organic Social, AI Assistant, and Email together added 29 sessions. The inflated Direct share is almost certainly an artifact of the August 16 spike -- untagged traffic from an email send, a social post, or a shared link will land in Direct by default. Identifying the source of that spike is the most pressing analytical question this week raises.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves comparison shows a 692% session increase from the first half (75 sessions across August 13--15) to the second half (594 sessions across August 16--19). On paper that looks like strong acceleration. In practice, it is almost entirely the August 16 event inflating the second-half number.

What is genuinely worth noting: even after August 16, sessions on August 17 (51) and August 19 (41) both ran well above the site's 28-day median. August 17 users were up 177% versus median, and August 19 new users were up 105%. This suggests some residual lift carried past the spike day -- possibly people who bookmarked the site or returned after a first visit. Whether that retention holds into next week is the key question. If the next 7-day window shows a baseline of 35--45 sessions per day rather than the previous 18--24, the August 16 event had lasting value. If it snaps back, it was a one-day visit with no stickiness.

---

### Launches + efficacy

All launches captured in this window are git commits -- daily pulse logs, SEO snapshots, and marketing pulse files. These are operational records, not public-facing content. None carry a URL, none have GSC impressions, and none have GA4 sessions attributed. This is expected and appropriate; they are infrastructure, not content assets.

The 30-day rollup adds important context. Over the last 30 days, 3 pages were launched and 113 pages were modified across 59 commits. The 30-day window shows 521,628 GSC impressions and 2,231 GSC clicks attributed to the site overall, and 1,282 GA4 sessions attributed to launch-tracked content. No pages are currently ranking in the top 10 in GSC. That combination -- meaningful impression volume with zero top-10 rankings -- suggests the site is appearing in search results but not in positions that reliably drive clicks. Pages are likely landing in positions 11--30, which typically generate impressions but minimal traffic.

Backlink totals show 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, and 5 .edu links. No new backlinks were added during this window specifically, but the existing .edu link presence is a real asset. Five .edu links for a specialty medical counseling practice in San Antonio is above average and worth protecting and building on.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Identify the August 16 traffic source before next week.** Check GA4 session source/medium for that day filtered to the Direct channel. Look for a shared URL, an untagged email blast, a social post, or a staff member who sent a link to a large group. If it was an email or social share that drove 400+ sessions, that is a channel worth tracking and repeating intentionally -- not leaving untagged.

2. **Resolve the GSC data gap.** Zero impressions for seven consecutive days is not a real signal -- it is a missing data signal. Confirm the GSC API connection is healthy and that the property is verified. If the feed is broken, ranking and impression data for the last week is invisible and cannot inform content decisions.

3. **Address the top-10 ranking gap.** The 30-day rollup shows 521K impressions but zero top-10 positions. Spend time this week in GSC's Performance report identifying which pages are ranking in positions 11--20. Those are the closest candidates for a ranking move. Look for pages with high impressions and low CTR -- that is the signal that a title or meta description revision could unlock traffic without new content.

4. **Map the 113 modified pages against GSC performance.** Over 30 days, 113 pages were modified. It is worth doing a one-time audit to see whether those modifications correlate with any impression or click changes. This does not need to be exhaustive -- spot-checking the top 20 modified pages against their GSC trendlines would answer whether the modification work is moving the needle.

5. **Protect and extend the .edu backlink base.** Five .edu links is a meaningful foundation for a practice this size. Identify which institutions those links come from, confirm they are still live and pointing to the correct pages, and identify one or two additional local .edu or health system partners (UTSA, UT Health San Antonio, Methodist Healthcare) worth approaching for a link or resource mention. One new .edu link in the next 30 days is a realistic and high-value target.
