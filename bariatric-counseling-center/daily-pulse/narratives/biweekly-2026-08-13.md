# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-13

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day window (July 29 -- August 11), the site generated 297 sessions at a per-day average of 21.2, with Organic Search unexpectedly leading all channels at 51% of volume -- a significant departure from the usual 60% Direct baseline. The window ended with a meaningful spike of activity on August 10, the same day 14 pages were updated in bulk, which suggests that content work is generating measurable same-day traffic lift even before GSC data reflects it.

---

### Volume + composition

Total volume for the window: 297 sessions, 266 users, 993 pageviews, and a per-day average of 21.2 sessions. GSC impressions and clicks are both showing zero for the entire window, which is almost certainly a data pipeline issue rather than a true signal -- the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 234,878 attributed GSC impressions and 1,064 clicks, so search visibility clearly exists. That zero-GSC situation needs to be confirmed as a reporting gap before drawing any conclusions about organic performance.

Channel composition this window was notably off-baseline. Organic Search came in at 151 sessions (51%), compared to the expected ~25%. Direct landed at 103 sessions (35%), well below its usual ~60% share. Referral contributed 32 sessions (11%), which is in line with baseline. Organic Social (8 sessions) and Email (2 sessions) were minimal. The Organic Search dominance is the most interesting signal here -- it likely reflects some combination of existing pages holding rankings and the bulk of content modifications from August 10 catching crawlers before those changes are fully reflected in GSC.

The low Direct share is worth noting. Direct traffic at BCC typically captures return visitors, branded searches, and untagged traffic from email or social sends. A drop from ~60% to 35% could mean fewer return visits this period, fewer untagged campaigns running, or simply that new organic visitors are proportionally diluting the Direct share. There is no evidence of a large untagged push (Email was only 2 sessions), so the more likely explanation is that organic is genuinely pulling in people who haven't been to the site before.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions dipped slightly from the first half to the second -- 157 in days 1-7 versus 140 in days 8-14, a -10.8% drop. On the surface that looks like a gentle decline, but pageviews tell the opposite story: 463 in the first half versus 530 in the second, a 14.5% increase. That divergence -- fewer sessions but more pageviews -- suggests the people arriving in the second half are reading more deeply per visit. The session duration anomalies reinforce this: August 5 (average session duration 408 seconds, +241% vs. 28-day median), August 9 (316 seconds, +159%), and August 10 (215 seconds, +73%) all flagged as critical upward outliers.

So while the headline sessions trend is slightly negative, the quality signal is pointing up. Visitors in the second half appear to be more engaged than those in the first. That pattern is more consistent with content discovery -- people finding pages through search, reading them thoroughly -- than with branded or direct return visits. The August 10 spike (29 sessions, 118 pageviews, average session duration +73%) landing on the same day as 14 page modifications is hard to call coincidence, though one day is not enough to confirm causation.

### Launches + efficacy

The dominant event in this window was the August 10 bulk update: 14 pages modified in a single day, covering the core service and landing-page layer of the site. Pages included /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/, /bariatric-surgery-psychological-evaluation-in-san-antonio-tx/, /testimonials/, /registered-dietitian-in-san-antonio-tx/, /bariatric-therapist-near-me/, /bariatric-counseling-near-me/, and several others spanning the holistic, nutritional, and therapist topic clusters. These are only 3 days old as of this briefing, so any ranking or traffic impact is still early-stage.

What the data does show at 3 days out is telling. /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ has 1,870 GSC impressions with zero clicks -- this page is appearing in search results but getting no taps, which is a title or snippet problem, not a visibility problem. /bariatric-surgery-psychological-evaluation-in-san-antonio-tx/ is in a similar position: 1,566 impressions, 6 clicks for a 0.4% CTR. /testimonials/ shows 1,436 impressions and zero clicks. These three pages together are generating real search exposure and leaving conversions on the table right now. /registered-dietitian-in-san-antonio-tx/ is the positive outlier: 90 impressions, 6 clicks, 6.7% CTR -- that snippet is working and is worth examining as a model for the others.

The git-commit launches in the window (daily and SEO pulse files) are operational tooling rather than public content, and appropriately show no GSC or GA4 impact. The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 4 new pages launched and 112 modifications across the period, with 51 commits. Backlinks stand at 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links total, and 5 .edu links -- a meaningful signal of domain credibility that should be protected and built on deliberately.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Rewrite the title tags and meta descriptions on the three high-impression/low-click pages.** /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ (1,870 impressions, 0 clicks), /bariatric-surgery-psychological-evaluation-in-san-antonio-tx/ (1,566 impressions, 0.4% CTR), and /testimonials/ (1,436 impressions, 0 clicks) are already visible in search. The problem is the snippet, not the ranking. Study /registered-dietitian-in-san-antonio-tx/ (6.7% CTR) and use whatever that page's title/description is doing differently as a starting template.

2. **Diagnose the GSC zero-data issue in the window before drawing any SEO conclusions.** The 14-day rollup shows 0 impressions and 0 clicks across all days, while the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 234,878 impressions attributed to launches. That gap suggests a connection or date-range issue in the reporting pipeline, not an actual drop in search visibility. Confirm which is accurate before any strategic decisions are made based on organic performance.

3. **Investigate the Organic Search channel surge.** This window ran 51% Organic Search versus a 25% baseline. That is a meaningful departure. Pull a GSC query report for July 29 -- August 11 to identify which queries and pages drove that lift. If specific pages or topic clusters are responsible, those are candidates for internal-link reinforcement and follow-on content.

4. **Audit internal links pointing to the August 10 modified pages.** These pages are showing GSC impressions quickly, which suggests they are already indexed and crawlable. The next bottleneck is likely internal link depth -- making sure the site's existing high-traffic pages and navigation structures are pointing toward the bariatric counseling, psychological evaluation, and therapist pages. A shallow internal link profile will limit how much ranking momentum these pages can build.

5. **Develop a backlink outreach plan targeting the .edu referral base.** Five .edu links across 4 referring domains is a solid foundation for a local counseling practice. Identify which institutions those links come from, determine whether any have related departments (social work, nutrition, psychology, public health) that could justify a second or third link, and draft a short outreach sequence. The model is already working -- this is about replicating it deliberately rather than waiting for it to happen organically.
