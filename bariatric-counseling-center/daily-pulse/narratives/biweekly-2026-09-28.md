# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-28

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending September 26, sessions trended upward 12.1% (first half vs. second half), with Organic Search and Direct splitting traffic nearly evenly -- an unusual and noteworthy departure from BCC's normal Direct-heavy baseline. The second half of the window was volatile but net positive, driven by strong sessions on September 22 and 25 and a batch of page modifications that shipped late in the period.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 422 total sessions, 378 users, and 1,408 pageviews across 14 days -- averaging 30.1 sessions and 27 users per day. Those are modest but stable numbers for BCC's current traffic scale. GSC impressions and clicks are reporting as zero for this window, which is almost certainly a data pipeline or property-connection issue rather than an actual traffic blackout -- the 30-day efficacy rollup shows over 1.4 million impressions attributed across active pages, confirming Google is absolutely still serving BCC content.

Channel composition is the most interesting story here. Organic Search drove 177 sessions (42%) and Direct drove 176 (42%) -- essentially tied. That is a significant structural shift from BCC's expected baseline of roughly 60% Direct and 25% Organic Search. Organic pulling even with Direct suggests content published in prior months is maturing in search rankings and beginning to send measurable traffic. Referral contributed 24 sessions (6%), Email 34 (8%), and Organic Social 6 (1%). The 19 Unassigned sessions likely represent improperly tagged links -- worth monitoring to see if that number grows, which would indicate a new untagged campaign or social post.

The elevated Email channel (8% vs. the ~5% baseline expectation) is worth noting. If a newsletter or outreach email went out in this window, that would explain it. If not, there may be a UTM tagging gap on an automated email sequence.

---

### Trend (first half vs. second half)

The window is accelerating, but unevenly. The first half (September 13--19) produced 199 sessions and 666 pageviews. The second half (September 20--26) produced 223 sessions and 742 pageviews -- a 12.1% session increase and an 11.4% pageview increase. That directional improvement is real and worth acknowledging.

The second half was not smooth, though. September 20 was a low point (24 sessions, only 45 pageviews -- a 54% drop below the 28-day median), while September 22 and 25 were two of the strongest days in the entire window (44 and 43 sessions respectively, with September 25 posting 181 pageviews, 76% above median). The session duration data from this period is also swinging wildly -- from a 91% drop below median on September 20 to a 381% spike above median on September 24. That kind of volatility in dwell time, paired with a low engagement rate on September 24, suggests a mixed audience: some visitors are finding content highly relevant and reading deeply, while others are bouncing quickly. The batch of page modifications that went live September 23--25 likely explains some of the September 25 lift as Googlebot and return visitors picked up refreshed content.

### Launches + efficacy

This window was modification-heavy, not launch-heavy. Four substantive page or post updates shipped between September 23--25, plus ongoing daily pulse commits. No net-new pages were launched. The 30-day rollup confirms only 4 pages launched in the broader 30-day window, with 56 page modifications and 88 git commits -- this has been a refinement cycle, not a content expansion cycle.

The standout performer in the window is "/healthy-living-2/why-hydration-is-essential-for-weight-loss-and-management/", modified September 24. It has accumulated 348,464 GSC impressions and 1,737 clicks (0.5% CTR) with 370 GA4 sessions -- by far the highest-performing individual asset in the top-launches list. That 0.5% CTR on nearly 350K impressions is the number to pay attention to: the page has massive search visibility but is converting that visibility to clicks at a below-average rate. Improving the title tag and meta description on this page is a high-leverage move -- even a 0.2% CTR improvement at that impression volume would yield roughly 700 additional monthly clicks.

"/what-is-a-weight-loss-counselor/" (modified September 25) is an early bright spot -- 1,090 impressions and 7 sessions in just 3 days, with a 1.5% CTR that is meaningfully better than the hydration page. That CTR suggests the title and snippet are working; let it mature before intervening. "/nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/" is the concern: 3,854 impressions in 3 days with zero clicks. High impression volume at launch means Google is showing it, but something about the title or snippet is not compelling searchers to act. "/bariatric-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/" has 8 impressions and 0 clicks -- too early to read. The September 23 updates to "/emotional-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/", "/overeating-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/", and the Testimonials and Dr. Sara Hamilton pages do not yet appear in the top-launches efficacy data, meaning their GSC footprint is still being established or is below threshold.

On backlinks: BCC currently shows 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links, with 5 of those being .edu links. No new backlinks appear to have been earned specifically in this 14-day window, but 5 .edu links is a meaningful asset. Whether those are actively sending referral traffic is worth checking against the Referral channel breakdown.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Fix the title and meta description on the hydration post.** "/healthy-living-2/why-hydration-is-essential-for-weight-loss-and-management/" is sitting on 348,464 impressions with a 0.5% CTR. This is the single highest-ROI optimization available right now. Test a title that speaks more directly to the emotional or practical outcome a searcher is looking for -- not just the topic. A CTR improvement here will show up in GA4 sessions within days of reindexing.

2. **Diagnose and rewrite the snippet for "/nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/".** Nearly 4,000 impressions and zero clicks in 3 days suggests the title or meta description reads like a generic service directory entry. Pull the exact queries driving those impressions in GSC and rewrite the snippet to match the language real searchers are using. This page likely has genuine local ranking potential and the current execution is leaving traffic on the table.

3. **Investigate the Organic Search surge -- confirm it is structural, not a fluke.** Organic pulling even with Direct is unusual for BCC. Before drawing strategic conclusions, check whether this reflects improved rankings on specific content or a temporary anomaly from a single high-traffic page (the hydration post is the likely candidate). If it is the hydration post alone, the channel split picture changes significantly.

4. **Audit the .edu backlinks to understand their referral contribution.** Five .edu links is a genuine asset, but it only matters if those pages are live, indexed, and linking from pages with real traffic. Pull the Referral channel source breakdown for this window and map it against those .edu domains. If one or more are actively sending visitors, they become case studies for a targeted outreach campaign to similar institutions.

5. **Plan the next content cluster before returning to modification mode.** This window was almost entirely refinement work -- 56 page modifications, 4 launches in 30 days. That is appropriate for a maturation phase, but organic growth at scale requires new content surface area. Based on the channel data showing Organic Search pulling even with Direct, there is an opening here: identify the next cluster (emotional eating and overeating-related topics already have pages in progress) and plan 3--5 new supporting posts to build topical depth around those service pages.
