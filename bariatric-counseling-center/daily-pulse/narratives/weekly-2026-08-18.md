# Weekly briefing -- 2026-08-18

### The window in one sentence

The seven days ending August 16 were dominated by a single massive traffic event on the final day -- 476 sessions on August 16 alone, representing 75% of the week's total volume, with an average session duration of just 22 seconds that signals bot activity or a misconfigured untagged link rather than genuine audience growth. Strip that day out and the underlying week was flat to slightly above baseline, running 20-37 sessions per day against a 28-day median of roughly 18-19.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 634 total sessions, 619 users, and 1,457 pageviews across seven days. The raw averages -- 90.6 sessions per day, 88.4 users per day -- are meaningless without the August 16 caveat. On the six days before that spike, daily sessions ranged from 17 to 37, which is modestly above the 28-day median but not remarkable. GSC impressions and clicks are reading zero across the entire window, which is almost certainly a data pipeline issue rather than a real signal -- the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 472,163 impressions attributed over the broader period, so Google Search Console is producing data somewhere. That zero block needs to be diagnosed before next week's briefings can make any credible SEO claims.

Channel composition was heavily Direct at 534 sessions (84%), well above BCC's baseline expectation of ~60%. Organic Search contributed 73 sessions (11.5%), near baseline. Referral was 16 sessions (2.5%), below the expected 10%. The Direct dominance is almost certainly explained by August 16 -- a sudden 476-session Direct flood with a 22-second average session duration is the signature of either a bot crawl, a shared link that auto-loaded in a preview pane (like a Slack or Teams link unfurl), or an untagged mass email that drove cold clicks with no real engagement. None of those are meaningful audience signals.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The system reports a 563.9% sessions trend from first half (83 sessions across August 10-12) to second half (551 sessions across August 13-16). That number is technically accurate and completely misleading -- the second half includes the August 16 anomaly that accounts for essentially all of its volume. If you hold August 16 out, the second half produced 75 sessions across three days, which is actually slightly below the first half's 83 sessions across three days.

The honest read: the underlying week was flat, running slightly above the 28-day median on most days but showing no clear directional momentum. August 10 and August 12 showed genuine above-median activity with real engagement (session durations were elevated on August 10 at 215 seconds), but August 11, 13, 14, and 15 were unremarkable. There is no acceleration trend to act on here -- the headline number is noise.

### Launches + efficacy

The launches ledger for this window is almost entirely git commits -- daily pulse files, SEO snapshots, and marketing pulses. These are operational records, not content that drives traffic or rankings, and none of them have GSC impressions or sessions attached. The genuinely notable activity in this window was the batch of page modifications on August 13: nine pages updated in a single day, including `/stop-the-cycle-of-binge-eating-you-dont-have-to-face-this-alone/`, `/binge-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/`, `/food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/`, `/compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/`, and several therapy and post-surgical support pages.

These August 13 modifications are only five days old as of this briefing, so it is too early to see ranking movement -- Google typically takes 1-3 weeks to re-crawl and reprocess modified pages. However, the 30-day efficacy rollup notes 3 net new pages launched and 125 modifications in the past month with 472,163 GSC impressions attributed -- that is a healthy impression volume and suggests the broader content library is indexed and visible. The problem is zero top-3 or top-10 rankings in the efficacy data, which means impressions are coming from positions 11 and beyond. That is the core SEO story right now: content is being seen but not clicked.

The backlinks picture is thin but notable -- 4 referring domains, 12 total inbound links, and 5 of those are .edu links. Five edu backlinks is genuinely strong for a counseling practice of this size. No new backlinks appear to have been earned specifically in this window, but that existing edu footprint is an asset worth building on.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Diagnose the August 16 traffic event before interpreting anything else.** Pull the session source/medium breakdown for that day specifically. If it is Direct with a 22-second average session, look for a bot signature in the raw event logs or check whether a mass email or social post went out with an untagged URL. Until this is explained, weekly volume numbers for this period are not useful for trending.

2. **Investigate and fix the GSC data gap.** Seven consecutive days of zero impressions in the daily feed while the 30-day rollup shows 472K impressions means the pipeline is broken for this window. Resolving this is a prerequisite for evaluating whether the August 13 page modifications are gaining search traction.

3. **Plan a ranking-position audit focused on the pages modified August 13.** The binge eating, food addiction, and compulsive overeating pages are all clinically specific and likely pulling impressions from positions 11-30. Two to three weeks from now (around September 1-3), pull their individual GSC position data and identify which ones moved. Those are the candidates for title tag and meta description refinement to improve CTR without rewriting the full page.

4. **Build an internal linking plan around the eating-disorder and food-relationship cluster.** The pages modified August 13 form a natural cluster -- `/binge-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/`, `/food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/`, `/compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/`, and the stop-the-cycle page should all be pointing to each other and to the post-bariatric support page. Cluster-linking is low effort and likely to lift the whole group, especially if any of them are in that 11-20 position range.

5. **Pursue a second .edu backlink using the existing ones as a template.** Five edu links is an above-average baseline for a San Antonio counseling practice. Identify which institutions those links came from, find the contact or resource page editor, and pitch a complementary resource from the eating-disorder cluster. One successful edu outreach pitch in the next two weeks would meaningfully strengthen domain authority for the whole site.
