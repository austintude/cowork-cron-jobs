# Weekly briefing -- 2026-09-21

### The window in one sentence

The week of September 13--19 was a holding pattern: 199 sessions and a nearly flat first-half-to-second-half trend (-3%) suggest the site is running at baseline, not growing. The one genuinely notable signal is that session duration spiked well above normal on four separate days, which points to something worth understanding before the September 18 cluster of page modifications has time to show results.

---

### Volume + composition

Over the seven-day window (September 13--19), the site recorded 199 sessions, 180 users, and 666 pageviews. That works out to roughly 28 sessions and 95 pageviews per day -- in line with recent baseline but not a breakout week. GSC impressions and clicks both read as zero for the entire window, which almost certainly reflects a data pipeline issue rather than a genuine absence of organic search activity. Do not interpret that as a content failure -- verify the GSC connection first.

Channel composition this week was unusual. Direct led with 83 sessions (42%), followed by Organic Search at 78 sessions (39%), Email at 23 sessions (12%), Referral at 9 sessions (5%), and Organic Social at 5 sessions (2%). BCC's expected baseline is roughly 60% Direct / 25% Organic Search / 10% Referral. Organic Search is running noticeably above its expected share while Direct is running below it. That swap could mean one of two things: a genuine improvement in organic rankings is pulling in search traffic, or untagged Direct traffic has shifted into sessions being credited to Organic because of how UTM parameters were (or weren't) applied to recent outbound sends. Given that Email came in at a measurable 12% this week, there may have been a send that drove some of that Direct traffic. Worth checking whether the email list send and the Direct channel volume lined up on the same day or two.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves split evenly and flatly. The first half (September 13--15) produced 101 sessions and 339 pageviews. The second half (September 16--19) produced 98 sessions and 327 pageviews. That is a -3% trend, which is effectively flat. There is no acceleration here, and there is no meaningful decline. The site held steady.

What is worth noting is the shape underneath that flatness. September 15 was the strongest day of the window -- 39 sessions and 138 pageviews, both flagged as major anomalies above the 28-day median -- followed by a sharp drop on September 16 to 21 sessions. The second half then leveled out in the mid-20s. The high on September 15 and the drop on September 16 partially cancel each other in the aggregate, which is why the halves look equal. Neither the spike nor the drop appears to have a clear content-driven explanation yet, since the bulk of page modifications happened on September 18, after both events.

---

### Launches + efficacy

The defining production event of this window was September 18, when 19 pages were modified in a single day -- the entire eating disorder and related-services cluster, plus the homepage and the /start/ intake page. That is a significant content push. At three days post-launch, it is too early to draw conclusions from GSC data, and given the GSC pipeline issue flagged above, impression counts would not be reliable regardless. What the data does show: the homepage (/) has 338,659 cumulative GSC impressions and 1,044 GA4 sessions in its 30-day attribution window, which reflects its baseline authority, not the September 18 modification. The ADHD and emotional eating page (/adhd-and-emotional-eating-in-san-antonio-tx/) showed 211 GSC impressions and a 2.8% CTR in just three days -- that is an early sign of relevance. The eating disorder therapist near me page (/eating-disorder-therapist-near-me/) pulled 1 session. Everything else in the cluster shows zero sessions and zero impressions so far, which is expected at this stage.

One page that stands out for a different reason is /video-lightbox-test/, a page publish (not a modification) that went live September 18. It has 2 GA4 sessions already, which means real people found it. If this is a test page that was not meant to be publicly indexed, it should be reviewed now -- either noindex it or remove it before Google picks it up. The /start/ page modification shows zero sessions, which is worth watching. That page is a direct intake path, so any prolonged flatness there after the modification should prompt a review of the changes made.

The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 1,019,175 GSC impressions and 5,241 clicks attributed across all tracked launches, with zero pages currently ranking in the top 3 or top 10 for tracked keywords. That ranking gap is the core challenge: the site is generating impressions at scale but not converting them to top-of-page positions.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit the September 18 cluster for internal linking gaps.** Nineteen pages were updated in one push, but unless they link to each other and to the homepage eating-disorder hub, Google will treat them as isolated pages. Spend next week mapping and filling the internal link structure across the full eating disorder cluster. Pages that share a topic cluster should be linking to each other and to one clear hub page.

2. **Prioritize GSC indexing requests for the September 18 pages.** Most of the modified pages show zero impressions at three days post-launch. Submit them through Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool to request indexing. Do this before the end of this week so the crawl window opens as early as possible -- results from that push will not be meaningful until mid-to-late October otherwise.

3. **Investigate the session duration spikes before attributing them to content quality.** Average session duration ran 89--260% above the 28-day median on four separate days (September 14, 15, 16, and 18). That pattern across multiple non-consecutive days suggests something structural -- possibly bot traffic, a single user with an open tab, or a specific page type (like a video embed) inflating time-on-site. Pull a sessions-by-page-type breakdown for those four days before treating these as wins.

4. **Resolve the GSC data gap before next week's briefing.** Zero GSC impressions for every day in a seven-day window is not a content signal -- it is a tracking signal. If the GSC API connection has lapsed or the property is not verified, this briefing and every future one will be missing the most important ranking data BCC has. This is the highest-priority technical task for the week.

5. **Review /start/ post-modification.** The intake page at /start/ -- titled "Ready for Help That Actually Works" -- shows zero sessions three days after being modified. If the September 18 changes affected its structure, CTA placement, or load speed, that could suppress conversions without triggering a traffic anomaly. A quick review of the page against its pre-modification version is worth doing before the next production push.
