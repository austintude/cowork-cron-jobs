# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-10

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending August 8, the site logged 300 sessions at an average of 21.4 per day, with Organic Search running well above its expected share and a notable August 5 engagement spike suggesting meaningful content interaction. The window ends on a softer note than it started, but the channel mix and pageview-per-session numbers in the second half point toward a more engaged audience even as raw volume dipped.

---

### Volume + composition

Total for the window: 300 sessions, 270 users, 968 pageviews, averaging 21.4 sessions and 19.3 users per day. Pageviews-per-session across the window works out to roughly 3.2, which is healthy for an informational counseling site -- people are not landing and leaving, they are browsing. GSC impressions and clicks show zero for the window, which is almost certainly a data pipeline issue (the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 212,104 impressions attributed to tracked launches, so the search presence is real). Do not read that zero as a ranking collapse; flag it with whoever manages the GSC integration.

The channel composition is the most important story in this window. Organic Search drove 162 of 300 sessions -- 54% -- which is substantially above the BCC baseline of ~25%. Direct came in at 99 sessions (33%), well below its usual 60% share. Referral added 30 sessions (10%), and Organic Social and Email together contributed 9. The simplest explanation for this inversion is that fewer untagged internal/email visits arrived during this period, making Organic Search's actual contribution visible rather than buried under a Direct ceiling. It could also mean search traffic genuinely accelerated -- the GLP-1 medication page and a wave of August 6 modifications both fit that story. Worth watching next week to see if the pattern holds.

There are no backlink additions to report in the window itself, but the standing total of 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, and 5 .edu links is a meaningful asset. Those .edu links in particular carry authority weight that compounds slowly.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions declined 11.3% from the first half (159) to the second half (141), which is a mild downward drift -- not an alarm, but not acceleration either. The first half was anchored by a 40-session day on July 30 that pulled the average up; strip that out and the two halves are closer to flat. Pageviews tell a different and more encouraging story: the first half generated 442 pageviews while the second half produced 526, a gain of roughly 19%. That means the second-half visitors were reading more per visit even as the headcount shrank -- sessions were fewer, but engagement was deeper.

August 1 and August 2 were genuinely soft (10 and 12 sessions respectively, flagged as CRITICAL and MAJOR anomalies against the 28-day median), and those days dragged down the second-half session count. The recovery was real: August 4 and 5 saw a pageview surge -- 110 and 162 respectively, with August 5 average session duration hitting 408 seconds, 240% above the 28-day median. Something on August 4-5 held people on the site for a long time. That is the signal to investigate, not the August 1-2 dip.

### Launches + efficacy

The August 6 update wave is the most significant production activity in this window. Fifteen items were modified in a single day: the homepage, the About page, the Bariatric Surgery Counseling and Psychological Evaluation service pages, the GLP-1 medications post, the Coordinate with Medical Providers page, and several blog posts covering emotional eating, self-sabotage, goal-setting, and movement. That is a broad sweep of core site infrastructure plus high-traffic content -- it reads like a sitewide content audit pass rather than isolated edits.

Of the two pages with enough data to evaluate, the GLP-1 medications page (/understanding-weight-loss-medications-wegovy-ozempic-zepbound-and-mounjaro/) is the one pulling real weight. Four days post-modification, it shows 31,992 GSC impressions, 94 clicks, and 42 GA4 sessions -- a 0.3% CTR on nearly 32,000 impressions. That CTR is low given the impression volume, which suggests the page is ranking but the title or meta description is not compelling searchers to click. There is meaningful upside here: improving the snippet on this page could yield a significant click increase without any ranking change. The /ourprogram/coordinate-with-medical-providers/ page shows 2,800 impressions and 3 sessions -- low CTR and low volume, still early, but consistent with a page that is indexing rather than ranking competitively. The remaining August 6 modifications (homepage, About, service pages, blog posts) have no GSC or GA4 data attributed yet at 4 days post-launch, which is normal -- expect the first meaningful signal in 10-21 days.

The commits logged in the window (daily pulse automations, August social cards, transparent logo, task board) are infrastructure and operational items -- they do not generate direct traffic or rankings. They are not underperforming; they are doing exactly what they are supposed to do.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Prioritize the GLP-1 medications page snippet.** The page at /understanding-weight-loss-medications-wegovy-ozempic-zepbound-and-mounjaro/ is generating nearly 32,000 impressions on a 4-day post-modification window. A 0.3% CTR at that impression volume suggests the title tag and meta description are not matching searcher intent well enough. Next week, pull the GSC query report for that URL, identify the top 5-10 queries driving impressions, and rewrite the title and description to speak more directly to those specific questions. Even moving to a 0.6% CTR would double clicks without touching rankings.

2. **Investigate the August 4-5 pageview and engagement spike.** Average session duration hit 408 seconds on August 5 with 162 pageviews -- both well outside normal range. That kind of depth usually means a specific page or content cluster held people. Pull a page-level breakdown for August 4 and 5 in GA4 to identify which URL(s) drove it. If it is a content page, that page deserves internal linking investment and possibly a follow-up or expansion.

3. **Audit the GSC pipeline for this reporting window.** The 14-day aggregate shows zero impressions and zero clicks despite the 30-day efficacy rollup confirming 212,000+ impressions are real. That is a data gap, not a ranking gap, but it makes trend analysis harder. Confirm whether the GSC data pull is using the correct date range and property, and whether there is a lag in the integration that is producing the zero values.

4. **Begin building internal links into the August 6 core pages.** The homepage, About page, and two service pages (/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ and /bariatric-surgery-psychological-evaluation-in-san-antonio-tx/) were all modified on August 6. Once they clear Google's processing cycle -- likely within the next 10-14 days -- internal links from high-impression content like the GLP-1 page and the emotional eating cluster will help pass authority toward these higher-conversion pages. Map those links now so they are ready to place.

5. **Explore a second .edu backlink outreach.** The existing 5 .edu links are a real structural advantage. Identify which institution(s) those links come from and whether any adjacent departments, faculty pages, or resource lists at the same institution could yield a second link. Warm outreach to an existing contact is significantly easier than cold outreach and fits the pattern that is already working.
