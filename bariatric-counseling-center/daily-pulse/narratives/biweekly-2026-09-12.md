# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-12

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day window (August 28 -- September 10), sessions accelerated 59.7% from the first week to the second, driven by a significant traffic event on September 8-9 that brought in more than double the site's typical daily visitors. GSC data is reporting zero across the entire window, which is the most pressing structural issue to resolve before this briefing can tell a complete story.

---

### Volume + composition

The site logged 335 sessions, 313 users, and 920 pageviews over 14 days -- averaging 23.9 sessions and 22.4 users per day. Those averages are pulled down by two near-zero days (August 31 and September 1, each showing just 1 session), which look like a weekend combined with a data collection gap rather than actual traffic loss. Stripping those two days out, the functional daily average runs closer to 27-28 sessions, which sits at or slightly above the 28-day median of 29.

Channel composition came in meaningfully different from BCC's expected baseline. Direct (141 sessions, 42%) and Organic Search (136 sessions, 41%) were nearly tied -- together accounting for 83% of all traffic. That Organic share is well above the expected ~25%, which is a positive signal suggesting content or rankings may be performing better than the site's small GSC-visible footprint implies. Email contributed 41 sessions (12%), higher than the typical ~5% other bucket and worth noting as a channel that is actually activating. Referral (7 sessions) and Organic Social (9 sessions) were both minimal. The near-absence of Referral traffic is consistent with only 4 referring domains in the backlink profile -- that ceiling is real and worth addressing.

The zero GSC impressions across all 14 days is almost certainly a data pipeline issue rather than a true signal. The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 921,486 attributed impressions and 4,624 clicks, and the homepage alone carries 305,732 impressions historically. Something is broken in how GSC data is flowing into this briefing window. That needs to be diagnosed before the next rollup, because right now the organic channel cannot be evaluated with any confidence.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is accelerating -- clearly. The first half (August 28 -- September 3) generated 129 sessions; the second half (September 4 -- September 10) generated 206, a 59.7% lift. Pageviews followed the same direction: 379 in the first half, 541 in the second. That is a meaningful directional signal, not noise.

The acceleration is heavily weighted toward September 8-9, which together produced 108 sessions -- more than the entire first five days of the window. September 8 was a CRITICAL anomaly day across every metric: sessions up 113.8%, users up 115.1%, new users up 136.4%, and pageviews up 123.2% vs. 28-day medians. Session duration on that day averaged 140 seconds, well above the median, which suggests people who arrived were actually reading. September 10 dropped sharply (7 sessions), but that pattern -- a big day followed by a hard drop -- often reflects a one-time referral or untagged send rather than organic momentum. The honest read: the second-half acceleration is real, but it depends heavily on what caused September 8-9. If that was an email send or social post, the underlying baseline has not materially changed. If it was organic, that would show up in GSC data -- which we currently cannot see.

---

### Launches + efficacy

The only page-level launch captured in this window is two modifications to the homepage ("/"), logged on September 10 and September 11. The homepage carries significant historical weight -- 305,732 GSC impressions, 1,555 clicks, and 922 GA4 sessions attributed to it in the 30-day rollup -- so changes there are not trivial. However, at 1-2 days post-modification, there is nothing to evaluate yet. Homepage changes typically need 2-3 weeks to show measurable GSC movement, and the current GSC data gap means even that signal would be invisible right now.

Everything else in the launches ledger is git commits: daily pulse logs, SEO pulse logs, and one tag-consolidation commit that was explicitly noted as "prepared for production, not shipped." The Food Friday ship-check on September 10 logged "no reply, nothing shipped." In short, this was a maintenance and documentation week from a content production standpoint -- no new pages, no new posts, no new cluster content. The 30-day efficacy rollup confirms this pattern: 5 pages launched and 21 pages modified over 30 days, with only 1 URL currently ranking in the top 10 and zero in the top 3. The content pipeline needs new material to give organic search something to work with.

The backlink profile sits at 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links total, with 5 edu links -- a notably strong edu ratio for a site this size. No new backlinks appear to have been earned in this window. That 4-domain ceiling likely explains why Referral traffic is nearly absent and why organic authority is not compounding faster. The edu links that exist are an asset worth building on.

---

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Diagnose the GSC data pipeline before next week's rollup.** Zero impressions across 14 consecutive days is not a real signal -- it is a broken integration. Without GSC data, the organic channel is opaque, launch efficacy cannot be measured, and ranking movement is invisible. This is the highest-priority infrastructure fix.

2. **Identify the source of the September 8-9 spike.** Pull GA4 session source/medium for those two days specifically. If it was an email send or untagged social post, that is a repeatable channel that is currently undertagged and undercounted. If it was organic, that is a ranking event worth identifying. Either answer changes what to prioritize next.

3. **Get a new piece of content into production this week.** The window had no new pages and a missed Food Friday. The 30-day window shows only 1 top-10 ranking with no content in the top 3 -- that will not improve without new material for Google to evaluate. Even one well-targeted post in a cluster the site has already started would compound faster than starting fresh.

4. **Build a second backlink from an edu or health-system source.** The site has 5 edu links, which is a real differentiator. Identify which institution those came from, find a comparable partner (a local university health program, a hospital system with a patient resources page, a dietitian program at UTSA or UT Health San Antonio), and make a direct outreach ask. One high-authority domain addition would meaningfully move the needle at the current scale.

5. **Revisit the homepage modifications and set a 2-week check-in.** The September 10-11 homepage changes are too fresh to evaluate now, but they should be on the calendar for a focused GSC + GA4 review around September 24-25. Define in advance what a "working" outcome looks like -- an impression increase, a CTR improvement, a ranking shift -- so the check-in has a clear pass/fail question.
