# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-08

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending August 6, the site ran at a modest 21.6 sessions per day (302 total) with Organic Search unexpectedly leading all channels at 52% of sessions -- well above the expected 25% baseline. The window ended on a strong note with August 4 and 5 delivering two of the best pageview days in the period, but the overall second-half dip suggests the July 30 spike was a one-time event rather than a sustained lift.

---

### Volume + composition

The 14-day window produced 302 sessions from 274 users, generating 1,827 pageviews. Per-day averages land at 21.6 sessions and 19.6 users -- a modest but functional baseline for a specialty practice site. GSC data is showing zero impressions and zero clicks across all 14 days, which is almost certainly a data pipeline issue rather than a real signal; the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 330,833 impressions and 1,536 clicks attributed to tracked launches, so the site is clearly appearing in search. That GSC gap needs to be resolved before you can trust any organic trend reporting.

Channel composition is the most interesting story here. Organic Search drove 158 of 302 sessions (52%), which is roughly double the expected 25% baseline. Direct came in at 100 sessions (33%), well below the expected 60% baseline. Referral contributed 30 sessions (10%), which is right on baseline. The inversion of Organic and Direct is notable -- it suggests either that a Direct-dominant audience (existing patients, staff) is visiting less, or that search-driven discovery is genuinely growing, or both. Organic Social at 12 sessions (4%) and Email at 1 session are both well under baseline, which is worth flagging if any email campaigns were intended to run during this window.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves tell a declining story on paper: 167 sessions in the first half versus 135 in the second, a drop of 19.2%. That sounds concerning, but the shape of the data matters. The first half was inflated by a single July 30 spike -- 40 sessions and 118 pageviews, both registering as CRITICAL anomalies at roughly double the 28-day median. Strip that day out and the two halves are essentially flat. The second half actually shows a recovery pattern: Aug 1--2 were genuinely soft (10 and 12 sessions respectively, both flagged CRITICAL/MAJOR down), but Aug 4 bounced to 27 sessions with 110 pageviews, and Aug 5 posted 162 pageviews -- the highest single-day pageview count in the entire window -- with average session duration spiking to 408 seconds, which is 240% above the 28-day median. That Aug 5 engagement signal is meaningful: whoever visited that day was reading deeply.

The honest read here is that the window is neither accelerating nor declining -- it is volatile around a stable floor. The real question is whether the August 6 batch of page modifications (19 pages and posts touched in a single day) starts moving the needle in the coming weeks.

---

### Launches + efficacy

The significant production event in this window was August 6, when 19 pages and posts were modified across the site -- including the homepage, /about/, the weight loss medications page, bariatric counseling and psych eval service pages, staff profiles, and several blog posts covering emotional eating, goal-setting, and self-sabotage. Because these modifications are only 2 days old as of this briefing, measuring their direct traffic impact is premature. What the 30-day efficacy rollup does show is that prior launches are accumulating real search visibility: 330,833 GSC impressions and 1,536 clicks across attributed pages, with 968 GA4 sessions tracked back to specific content.

The standout performer in the top launches is the weight loss medications page (/understanding-weight-loss-medications-wegovy-ozempic-zepbound-and-mounjaro/), which has accumulated 29,752 GSC impressions and 42 GA4 sessions -- a 0.3% CTR that is low given the impression volume and suggests the title or meta snippet may not be as compelling as the page itself. The homepage (/), while modified just 2 days ago, carries 106,049 historical impressions and 592 GA4 sessions, making it the single largest session driver in the dataset. The bariatric surgery counseling page (/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/) is worth flagging: 1,658 impressions and zero clicks. That is a CTR of 0.0% and is a clear candidate for title and meta description work. On backlinks, the site currently holds 4 referring domains generating 12 inbound links, including 5 from .edu sources -- that is a respectable foundation and the .edu links in particular carry authority weight worth building on.

---

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Investigate and fix the GSC data gap.** Fourteen straight days of zero impressions in the daily GSC feed is not a real signal -- the 30-day rollup proves the site is visible in search. This is likely a property configuration or API scope issue. Resolving it is prerequisite to making any reliable organic trend decisions going forward.

2. **Prioritize the CTR problem on the bariatric surgery counseling page.** Zero clicks on 1,658 impressions is an actionable signal, not a mystery. Rewrite the title tag and meta description for /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ with a clearer value proposition and a direct reference to San Antonio. Test within 2 weeks and watch GSC CTR.

3. **Investigate the weight loss medications page CTR gap.** At 29,752 impressions, that page is getting real exposure. A 0.3% CTR on a high-volume page leaves meaningful clicks on the table -- likely 60 to 90 additional monthly visits if CTR doubles. Look at what query terms are driving impressions versus what the current title tag promises, and consider a more specific rewrite.

4. **Build a content cluster check-in for the August 6 modifications.** Nineteen pages touched in a single day is a significant batch update. Set a calendar reminder to pull per-page GSC data in 14 days to see which of those modifications Google has recrawled and whether any show impression movement. This is pattern-tracking work, not a one-day task.

5. **Explore a backlink outreach play using the existing .edu foundation.** Five .edu links is a strong starting position for a specialty practice. Investigate which institutions those links come from and whether any have additional resource pages, research pages, or student health pages where BCC could earn a second placement. One well-placed .edu link likely outperforms several months of on-page optimization for domain authority.
