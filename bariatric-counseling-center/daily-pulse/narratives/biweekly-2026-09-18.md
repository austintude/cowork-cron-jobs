# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-18

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending September 16, sessions held essentially flat (3.5% lift, first half to second half), with Email traffic emerging as a meaningful channel at 14% of sessions -- well above its typical share -- and the only notable content launch being the new `/start/` page, which is too early to evaluate. The bigger story this window is what's missing: GSC data is completely absent, which means organic search health is invisible right now.

---

### Volume + composition

Total sessions came in at 468 over 14 days, with a per-day average of 33.4 sessions and 31.1 users. Total pageviews landed at 1,311. GSC impressions and clicks are reporting zero across every day in the window -- that is a data problem, not a traffic problem, and it needs to be resolved before organic search can be evaluated meaningfully.

Channel composition tells an interesting story. Direct led at 207 sessions (44%), which is below BCC's baseline of ~60% -- that gap is worth noting. Organic Search came in at 174 sessions (37%), which is actually above the expected ~25% baseline, suggesting the site has real search traffic even if GSC isn't confirming it. Email drove 67 sessions (14%), which is roughly 3x its typical ~5% share. That spike is the most noteworthy channel shift in the window. Whether it reflects a deliberate email send, a newsletter, or a drip sequence firing more actively than usual, it's worth identifying and repeating if it was intentional. Referral (11 sessions) and Organic Social (8 sessions) were both minimal.

The lower-than-baseline Direct share combined with the higher Organic and Email shares suggests the audience reaching the site in this window skewed toward people finding BCC through search or being prompted by email -- less passive, likely more intentional. That's a healthy composition for a practice trying to build awareness beyond its existing base.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The first half (Sept 3--9) produced 230 sessions; the second half (Sept 10--16) produced 238 sessions -- a 3.5% increase. That is effectively flat. There is no acceleration happening, but there's also no concerning decline. The window held steady.

What's more interesting than the flat overall trend is the volatility within it. Sept 10 was a near-collapse (7 sessions, flagged as a CRITICAL anomaly at -75.9% below median), immediately followed by Sept 11 being the strongest day in the window (64 sessions, +116.9% above median). Those two days together look like a traffic source went offline and then came back, or an email deployed on Sept 11 and drove a one-day burst. Session duration anomalies on Sept 10, 14, 15, and 16 -- all running 90--208% above median -- suggest that whoever did show up in the second half was highly engaged, spending significantly more time on-site than typical. That's a quality signal even when volume is modest.

### Launches + efficacy

The only substantive content launch in this window was `/start/` -- titled "Ready for Help That Actually Works" -- published September 15. At 3 days old, it has zero GSC impressions and zero attributed sessions, which is completely normal. Pages in the indexing-warmup phase typically show no GSC signal for the first one to two weeks. It's too early to evaluate. What matters is whether it gets indexed promptly and whether the page is internally linked well enough for Googlebot to find it without waiting on a sitemap crawl. Both of those are worth confirming now rather than in two weeks.

Beyond `/start/`, the launches ledger for this window is almost entirely automated pulse commits and operational housekeeping (stopping video tracking in git, daily/weekly pulse files). There were no other page publishes or page modifications detected. The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 663,512 GSC impressions and 3,424 clicks attributed to site content, with one keyword ranking in the top 3 and one in the top 10 -- but those numbers reflect work done prior to this window, and the GSC data blackout inside the window itself makes it impossible to say whether that momentum is holding, growing, or stalling.

No new backlinks were earned in this window specifically, though the site's existing profile (4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, 5 of which are .edu) is a meaningful asset. The .edu links in particular carry authority weight and suggest at least one institutional relationship worth cultivating further.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Diagnose and fix the GSC data blackout first.** Zero impressions across 14 consecutive days is not a ranking problem -- it's a reporting problem. Check that the GSC property is verified, that the GA4--GSC linkage is intact, and that the correct property (domain vs. URL-prefix) is selected. Without GSC, you cannot evaluate organic search health, keyword movement, or whether any content is gaining or losing ground in search.

2. **Identify what drove the Email channel spike.** Email at 14% of sessions (67 sessions) is roughly 3x its usual share. If a specific send or sequence drove that, document it -- the message, the list, the send time -- so the model can be repeated. If it was unintentional or automated, that's still worth knowing because it suggests an engaged list that responds.

3. **Confirm `/start/` is indexed and internally linked before the two-week mark.** Request indexing via GSC's URL Inspection tool if you haven't already, and audit whether at least 3--5 existing high-traffic pages link to `/start/` with natural anchor text. A page with no internal links and no GSC impressions at 14 days is a page that needs help, not patience.

4. **Investigate the Sept 10 traffic collapse.** A 76% drop in a single day followed by a 117% spike the next day suggests a specific traffic source went dark and then returned -- not organic volatility. Pull the Sept 10--11 channel breakdown and see whether a particular channel (likely Email or Direct) accounts for the swing. If an email list or notification system had a delivery failure, that's worth flagging operationally.

5. **Plan the next content launch with internal linking in mind from day one.** The `/start/` page and any pages that follow should have their internal linking strategy mapped before publish, not after. Given the 30-day rollup shows the site is capable of generating 663K impressions from existing content, new pages need to be wired into that ecosystem immediately to benefit from existing crawl equity.
