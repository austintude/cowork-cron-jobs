# Weekly briefing -- 2026-07-29

### Volume + composition

The window produced 136 sessions, 123 users, and 1,797 pageviews across 7 days, averaging 19.4 sessions and 17.6 users per day. GSC impressions and clicks returned zero across all 7 days -- that is a data gap worth flagging, not a signal that search traffic disappeared. Either GSC reporting is delayed or the property connection needs a check.

That is a notable departure from BCC's expected baseline of ~60% Direct / ~25% Organic Search. Organic Search running higher than Direct is actually a healthy sign -- it suggests the site is earning more non-branded search visits rather than relying on people who already know BCC. The Referral share at 18% is also elevated above the ~10% baseline, which is worth investigating to identify the source. It may be tied to the 12 total inbound links across 4 referring domains shown in the backlinks data.

The AI Assistant channel showing 2 sessions is small but worth noting as a data point to track over time. That channel will likely grow as more people use AI tools to find local healthcare providers.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is accelerating, clearly. The first half (July 21--23) produced 51 sessions and 694 pageviews. The second half (July 24--27) produced 85 sessions and 1,103 pageviews -- a 67% session increase and 59% pageview increase over the same number of comparable days. That is not a flat line.

The important caveat: the pageview spike on July 23--24 (592 and 952 pageviews respectively, against a 28-day median of ~52) is so large relative to session counts that it almost certainly reflects a bot crawl, a scraper, or a single high-engagement session with deep internal navigation -- not a wave of new humans reading content. Sessions on those same days were 17 and 28, which are normal. The genuine acceleration story is in the session trend on July 27 (25 sessions, flagged as +32% above median), which looks like real visitor growth rather than a data artifact. The underlying momentum is real; the pageview numbers are inflated and should not be used in external reporting as-is.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit the GSC data connection this week.** Seven consecutive days of zero impressions in the aggregate feed, against a 30-day rollup showing 368,754 impressions, means something broke in how daily GSC data is pulling. This needs to be resolved before the trend briefing can accurately assess whether the July 23 page modifications improved or hurt rankings. This is the highest-priority diagnostic item.

2. **Investigate the Referral traffic source.** Referral ran at 18% of sessions this week, nearly double BCC's ~10% baseline. With 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links in the backlinks data (including 5 .edu links), there is likely a specific source driving a meaningful portion of that traffic. Identify which domain is sending it -- if it is one of the .edu links, that is a relationship worth nurturing for a second link or a content collaboration.

3. **Build internal links to the new mental health checklist page from the eating disorders and bariatric surgery service pages.** The page launched into a content cluster that already exists on the site. Without internal links, Google has limited context for how this page fits the broader site architecture, and visitors browsing the service pages will never discover it. Three or four contextual links from existing pages with topical overlap should be enough to help.

4. **Schedule a content audit of the five pages modified July 23.** Those pages were touched but the window's data cannot yet confirm whether the changes improved engagement or search visibility. Once GSC data is flowing again, pull position and CTR for `/therapy-for-eating-disorders-in-san-antonio-tx/` and `/body-confidence-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/` specifically -- those are service pages with direct revenue intent and worth tracking closely for ranking movement over the next 2--3 weeks.

5. **Consider whether the July 23--24 pageview spike warrants a bot/scraper filter review in GA4.** Pageviews at 1,049% and 1,713% above median while sessions stayed flat is a classic crawl signature. If unfiltered bot traffic is inflating the pageview totals regularly, it distorts engagement metrics like pages-per-session and average session duration, making it harder to read real user behavior. This is worth 30 minutes in GA4's exclusion filters.
