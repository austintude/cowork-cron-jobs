### Volume + composition

The site logged 190 sessions, 169 users, and 606 pageviews across the 7-day window, averaging 27.1 sessions and 86.6 pageviews per day. Those numbers are unremarkable on their own -- they sit roughly at baseline -- but the pageview-per-session ratio of 3.2 is notably healthy and consistent with engaged browsing rather than quick bounces. GSC impressions and clicks both show zero across every day of the window, which is almost certainly a data pipeline or property-connection issue rather than a true reflection of organic visibility. Do not read that as "no search traffic" -- the 68 Organic Search sessions in GA4 confirm Google is sending people; GSC just isn't reporting on it right now.

Channel composition was: Direct 48%, Organic Search 36%, Email 11%, Referral 5%, Organic Social less than 1%. That Organic Search share (36%) running well above the 25% baseline is genuinely encouraging and suggests the eating-disorder cluster pages are continuing to build search presence even without GSC confirmation. Direct at 48% is below the usual 60% baseline, which likely reflects the Email channel pulling its own weight (20 sessions, clearly tagged) rather than a drop in branded visits. The Referral number (9 sessions) is low -- worth watching to see if it stays suppressed after the Sep 18 edits finished deploying.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions went from 89 in the first half (Sep 14--16, 3 days) to 101 in the second half (Sep 17--20, 4 days). Normalizing for day count, that's roughly 29.7/day in the first half versus 25.3/day in the second half -- so the raw 13.5% session-count lift is actually a slight per-day decline once you account for the extra day in the second half. Call this flat, not accelerating.

Pageviews tell a similar story: 308 in the first half, 298 in the second, with Sep 19 (62) and Sep 20 (45) both flagging as significant CRITICAL and MAJOR anomalies well below the 28-day medians. The Sep 20 session-duration anomaly -- a 91% drop to under 10 seconds -- is also a flag, suggesting either a bot session, a tagging problem, or a very short test visit. None of this is alarming at the weekly level, but the late-week softness means the window ended on a weaker note than it started, which is worth watching over the next 7 days to see if it persists.

### Launches + efficacy

The defining event of this window was a coordinated update to the eating-disorder cluster on Sep 18: 13 pages modified in a single day, covering the core treatment pages (/eating-disorder-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/, /eating-disorder-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/, /eating-disorder-therapist-in-san-antonio-tx/), the telehealth page, the group therapy page, the ADHD-and-eating page, the anxiety-and-weight page, and several others. That is a significant content event. At 4 days since launch, these pages are in the indexing-warmup window -- Google has had almost no time to re-crawl and re-evaluate them. No GSC signal yet is expected and not concerning. What matters is that these were high-priority pages and the cluster is now internally consistent, which is the right precondition for ranking improvement.

The /video-lightbox-test/ page published Sep 18 has 2 GA4 sessions and no GSC impressions -- that is normal for a 4-day-old page, but the slug and target keyword ("video lightbox test") suggest this is a technical test rather than a content asset. If that's correct, it doesn't need to be tracked for SEO efficacy. Confirm whether this page should be noindexed to avoid it diluting crawl budget. The homepage (/) and /start/ were also modified Sep 18 -- the homepage carries 340,084 GSC impressions and 1,049 GA4 sessions in the 30-day rollup, making it the site's single most visible asset. Any changes there warrant close monitoring over the next 2 weeks for CTR shifts.

The 30-day rollup shows 1,023,470 GSC impressions and 5,280 clicks attributed across the launch portfolio -- a 0.52% average CTR, which is low for a local healthcare site where you'd typically expect 2--4%+ from branded and near-me queries. Zero pages currently ranking in the top 10 in tracked keywords is a notable gap given the volume of content live. This suggests the cluster is generating impressions at lower positions (likely pages 2--4) and the next lever is moving individual pages from position 15--30 into the top 10, not publishing more new pages.

No new backlinks were earned within this window. The site currently holds 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links total, with 5 coming from .edu sources -- that .edu presence is a real asset. Backlink velocity is effectively zero right now, which is a meaningful constraint on how fast the eating-disorder cluster will climb in competitive local search.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit the GSC data connection.** Zero impressions across 7 consecutive days is a pipeline problem, not an organic performance problem. Before next week's rollup, verify that the GSC property is correctly linked to GA4, that the Search Console property covers the right domain variant (www vs non-www vs root), and that there are no property-level permission issues. Until this is resolved, organic search performance is effectively blind.

2. **Give the Sep 18 eating-disorder cluster edits 10--14 more days before evaluating.** Don't make additional changes to those 13 pages yet -- let Google re-crawl and re-index them cleanly. Schedule a focused efficacy check around Oct 2--5 specifically looking at position movement for the core pages: /eating-disorder-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/, /eating-disorder-therapist-in-san-antonio-tx/, and /telehealth-eating-disorder-treatment-in-texas/. Those are the highest-intent pages in the cluster.

3. **Decide what to do with /video-lightbox-test/.** If it's a dev/UX test, add a noindex tag this week. A publicly indexable page with "video lightbox test" as its target keyword consumes crawl budget and creates a minor credibility signal problem if a prospective client lands on it from search.

4. **Start a focused backlink outreach push.** The .edu links are a foundation, but 4 referring domains is a significant constraint. One well-placed backlink from a San Antonio health system, a local therapist directory, or a UTSA-adjacent health resource could meaningfully shift authority for the eating-disorder cluster. Identify 3--5 specific targets this week and draft a short pitch before new page production resumes.

5. **Investigate the late-week traffic softness.** Sep 19 and Sep 20 both flagged anomalously low pageviews (62 and 45, versus a median of ~98--103). This could be normal weekend variation, a tagging issue, or early signal of a visibility drop. Pull a channel-level breakdown for those two days specifically -- if Organic Search dropped sharply while Direct held, that's a crawl/ranking signal worth acting on quickly.
