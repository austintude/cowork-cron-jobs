# Weekly briefing -- 2026-09-07

### The window in one sentence

The Aug 30 -- Sep 5 window was heavily distorted by two near-complete traffic blackouts on Aug 31 and Sep 1, making raw totals misleading -- but once normal traffic resumed on Sep 2, the site accelerated meaningfully through the back half of the week. The underlying signal, stripped of the blackout days, suggests the site is in a healthy range, not a declining one.

---

### Volume + composition

Over the 7-day window, the site recorded 124 sessions, 115 users, and 340 pageviews -- averaging 17.7 sessions and 16.4 users per day. Those averages are dragged down significantly by Aug 31 and Sep 1, which each logged only 1 session and 2 pageviews. Excluding those two days, the remaining five days averaged roughly 22 sessions and 72 pageviews per day, which is closer to what the 28-day median suggests is normal. GSC impressions and clicks are both reading zero across the full window, which is almost certainly a data pipeline or property configuration issue rather than a real search collapse -- that needs a direct check in Search Console.

Channel composition for the window shows Organic Search (58 sessions) and Direct (57 sessions) in a near-even split, which is a meaningful departure from BCC's expected baseline of roughly 60% Direct and 25% Organic. Organic Search running at ~47% of all sessions -- nearly double its expected share -- is the most interesting signal in this window. That is not a fluke of one day; it held across multiple days in the back half. Referral (2 sessions), Email (6 sessions), and Organic Social (1 session) were all low but within normal ranges for a week without a deliberate email push. The elevated Organic share either reflects a real ranking improvement pulling new traffic, or it reflects that Direct traffic was suppressed (possibly by the blackout period affecting direct/returning visitors disproportionately). Worth separating those hypotheses in the coming week.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves comparison shows a stark acceleration: 23 sessions in the first half (Aug 30 -- Sep 1) versus 101 sessions in the second half (Sep 2 -- Sep 5), a 339% increase. Pageviews moved the same direction -- 60 in the first half versus 280 in the second. That looks dramatic on paper, but it is largely explained by the blackout on Aug 31 and Sep 1 collapsing first-half totals to near zero.

What is genuinely worth noting is that even after accounting for the blackout distortion, the back half of this window performed well above median. Sep 3 hit 31 sessions and 106 pageviews (a MAJOR anomaly on the upside), and Sep 4 followed with 30 sessions and 89 pageviews. Sep 5 pulled back to 19 sessions, which registered as below-median, but that pattern -- a mid-week peak followed by a Friday dip -- is typical for this site. The honest read is that the window is not accelerating in a clean trend sense, but the underlying traffic level once the blackout clears looks steady-to-positive rather than declining.

### Launches + efficacy

Every launch captured in this window is a git commit -- daily pulse entries and a handful of SEO pulse commits. There are no new content pages, no new service pages, and no new blog posts recorded in the launches ledger for this period. The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 7 pages launched and 32 pages modified over the broader month, but none of those appear to have landed specifically in this 7-day window. The only non-pulse commit of note is a log entry from Sep 1 recording a blocked blog redate drip run -- "staging12 logged out, 4th consecutive" -- which signals that an automated content workflow has been failing repeatedly and may be contributing to a content publication slowdown.

The 30-day rollup attributes 312,033 GSC impressions and 1,429 clicks to tracked launches, with 394 GA4 sessions attributed and one page currently ranking in the top 10. That is a real signal in an otherwise quiet window: the content catalog is generating search visibility, but it is concentrated. With zero pages in the top 3 and only one in the top 10, the bulk of those 312K impressions are coming from positions 11 and below -- meaning the site is being seen but not often clicked. That is a CTR problem, not an impressions problem.

No new backlinks were earned in this window based on the ledger data. The existing profile shows 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, and notably 5 edu links -- a strong ratio for a site this size. The edu link count is an asset worth building on.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Diagnose the GSC data gap before anything else.** Seven consecutive days of zero impressions in GA4 -- while the 30-day rollup attributes over 312K impressions -- points to a property connection or date-range issue in the data pipeline, not a real search collapse. Confirming this is a reporting artifact (not a real signal) should happen before drawing any conclusions about organic performance this week.

2. **Investigate the blocked redate drip workflow.** The Sep 1 commit logs a 4th consecutive blocked run on the blog redate automation. If that process is responsible for keeping content fresh and date-visible for Google, four consecutive failures likely means content that should be circulating is sitting stale. Identify whether this is a session/auth issue on staging12 and resolve it before resuming.

3. **Audit what is ranking just outside the top 10.** With 312K impressions attributed but only one page in the top 10, there is likely a cluster of pages sitting at positions 11-20 that are generating impressions without clicks. Pulling a GSC position report filtered to positions 8-20 and sorting by impressions would surface the highest-leverage pages for a title tag or meta description refresh -- small changes that can move CTR without requiring new content.

4. **Develop a plan to pursue a second edu-domain backlink.** BCC has 5 edu links from 4 referring domains -- a strong foundation. One targeted outreach pitch to a UTSA, UTHSC, or UT Health San Antonio resource page (student wellness, weight health resources, behavioral health) could add a high-authority link that compounds the existing profile. Research the right contact and page target this week; don't send cold yet.

5. **Plan the next substantive content launch.** The launches ledger for this window contains only automated commits -- no new pages, no modified cornerstone content. The 30-day pace of 7 pages and 32 modifications is healthy, but this specific week shows a production pause. Identify the next page in the queue and scope it so Daniel has a clear target for the week ahead.
