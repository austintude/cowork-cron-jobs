# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-23

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending August 21, sessions jumped 332% from the first half to the second half (160 vs. 691), almost entirely because of a massive one-day traffic spike on August 16 that drove 476 sessions -- more than the entire first week combined. Strip that anomaly out and the underlying baseline is still modestly above prior norms, which is a small positive, but the spike itself needs to be explained before it can be celebrated.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 851 total sessions, 824 users, and 2,033 pageviews across 14 days, averaging about 61 sessions and 145 pageviews per day. Those averages are inflated by August 16. A more honest baseline -- excluding that day -- is roughly 29 sessions and 79 pageviews per day, which is consistent with BCC's recent norms. GSC impressions and clicks came in at zero for every day of the window, which is a data-feed issue worth flagging separately rather than a sign the site stopped ranking.

Channel composition was significantly skewed from BCC's baseline. Direct accounted for 648 of 851 sessions (76%), well above the expected 60%. Organic Search landed at 153 sessions (18%), slightly below the 25% baseline. Referral came in at 26 sessions (3%), below the 10% norm. Organic Social contributed 13 sessions and AI Assistant sent 3, both small but worth tracking as emerging channels.

The outsized Direct share almost certainly traces back to the August 16 spike. When Direct surges that sharply and session duration collapses simultaneously (the anomaly log shows avg session duration dropped 81.7% that day, from a median of 122 seconds to about 22 seconds), the most likely explanation is an untagged email blast, a mass SMS send, or a social post that drove a large volume of brief visits -- not organic discovery. The short session durations suggest many of those visitors landed and left quickly, which means they may not have been people with genuine intent to explore the site.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

On paper, the trend looks explosive: 160 sessions in the first half (Aug 8-14) versus 691 in the second half (Aug 15-21), a 332% increase. In practice, one day -- August 16 with 476 sessions -- accounts for the entire gap. Pull that day out and the second half totals approximately 215 sessions across six days, which is a daily average of about 36, compared to a first-half average of 23. That underlying improvement is real and consistent with the anomaly data showing Aug 17-21 running above the 28-day median. So the picture is: one large unexplained spike surrounded by a modest, genuine lift in baseline activity.

The session duration data reinforces this reading. August 17 and 18 showed above-median engagement (211 and 179 seconds respectively), while August 19, 20, and 21 showed below-median duration. That oscillation suggests a mix of traffic quality -- some visitors exploring with intent, others arriving briefly and leaving. The window is not cleanly accelerating; it is uneven, with a legitimate but small upward baseline signal underneath a noisy anomaly.

### Launches + efficacy

The substantive launch worth noting in this window is DRB-1146: three psych-eval BOFU pages (post IDs 6530-6532) rebuilt and published to staging on August 21. These pages are 2 days old as of this briefing -- far too early to measure organic performance -- but they represent meaningful infrastructure work. Psych-eval content is directly tied to the consultation-to-appointment funnel, and BOFU pages in that category have a clear conversion purpose. The priority after the staging-to-production push clears should be requesting GSC indexing and confirming canonical URLs are clean.

Everything else logged in the launches ledger for this window is git-commit housekeeping -- daily pulse logs, SEO snapshots, and marketing pulse files. These are operational records, not content launches, and they carry no organic signal of their own. The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 553,973 GSC impressions and 2,352 clicks attributed across the broader site, with 1,338 GA4 sessions attributed to launched content. That is a meaningful pool of organic activity, but no specific page from recent launches is surfacing in the top_launches data with confirmed rankings or impression counts -- likely a data-attribution lag rather than a performance problem.

Backlinks stand at 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links total, with 5 of those being .edu links. No new backlinks were logged within this specific window. The .edu link count is a genuine asset for a medical-adjacent practice -- those are difficult to earn and carry real authority weight.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Identify the August 16 traffic source before next week ends.** Check your email platform send logs, any scheduled social posts from August 15-16, and SMS campaign records. If that spike came from an untagged send, tag it properly and document what drove 476 sessions -- because if it worked once, it should be repeatable and measurable.

2. **Prioritize the DRB-1146 psych-eval pages (6530-6532) for production push and indexing.** These are BOFU pages with direct appointment-funnel value. Once they clear preflight and go live on production, submit them to GSC manually and monitor for indexing within 7-10 days. If they show zero impressions after three weeks, the meta and title structure likely needs revision.

3. **Investigate the GSC data feed.** Zero impressions and zero clicks for every single day of a 14-day window is almost certainly a reporting or authentication gap, not a ranking collapse -- the 30-day efficacy rollup still shows 553K impressions attributed. Resolving this blind spot is a prerequisite for meaningful trend analysis going forward.

4. **Audit the session duration pattern across Aug 19-21.** Three consecutive days of below-median engagement (81, 72, and 26 seconds) after two high-engagement days suggests a content or traffic-source mismatch. Pull the landing page report for those days and check whether a specific page or channel is generating quick exits -- that points to either a page quality issue or misaligned audience targeting.

5. **Map the .edu backlink sources and look for one more pitch opportunity.** Five .edu links across 4 referring domains is a strong foundation for a practice this size. Identify which institution is sending the most authoritative link, review what content earned it, and look for a second institution in the San Antonio or broader Texas health-education space to approach with a similar content hook. Backlink growth at this level tends to compound slowly -- planting the next outreach now sets up a win in the next 60-90 days.
