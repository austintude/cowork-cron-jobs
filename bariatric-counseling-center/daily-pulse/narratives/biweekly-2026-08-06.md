# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-06

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days from July 22 through August 4, sessions trended up 13.8% (first half vs. second half), driven primarily by Organic Search -- which ran well above BCC's typical baseline -- with a notable traffic spike on July 30 that suggests an external referral or untagged campaign push. A large batch of 20 page modifications shipped on August 5 (the day after the window closed), so their real efficacy story begins now.

### Volume + composition

The window produced 295 total sessions from 268 users, generating 2,257 pageviews across 14 days. That works out to roughly 21 sessions and 161 pageviews per day on average -- though the pageview average is heavily distorted by two outlier days (July 23 and July 24 logged 592 and 952 pageviews respectively, likely from a crawl, an internal review session, or an untagged bulk content preview). Strip those two days out and the per-day pageview pace looks much closer to 55-70, which is more consistent with BCC's typical browsing patterns. GSC impressions and clicks both read as zero for the entire window, which is almost certainly a data pipeline issue -- it should be investigated before drawing any conclusions about search visibility.

Channel composition this window diverged meaningfully from BCC's baseline. Organic Search led at 149 sessions (roughly 51% of total), which is roughly double the expected ~25% share. Direct came in at 95 sessions (32%), well below the usual 60% anchor. Referral added 37 sessions (13%), and Organic Social contributed 11. The elevated Organic Search share is the most strategically interesting signal here -- it suggests that prior content work is gaining ground in search rankings, even without confirmed GSC data. The underperformance of Direct relative to baseline could mean fewer repeat visitors this period, or it could reflect measurement issues if email or social campaigns were running untagged.

### Trend (first half vs. second half)

The window is modestly accelerating. First-half sessions totaled 138 (average 19.7/day), second-half sessions totaled 157 (average 22.4/day) -- a 13.8% lift. That's not dramatic, but the direction is positive and the momentum going into August 4 looks encouraging: July 30 was the window's strongest single day at 40 sessions (roughly double the 28-day median), and August 4 closed at 27 sessions with average session duration spiking 46% above the median, which suggests people who visited that day were actually reading.

The concerning part of the second half is the two-day drop on August 1 and August 2, where sessions fell to 10 and 12 respectively -- both flagged as CRITICAL anomalies, down 50% and 40% from the 28-day median. That dip immediately following July 30's peak looks like a natural regression after a one-time traffic event rather than a structural decline, but it's worth watching. If the first week of August continues to hover in the low teens on weekdays, that pattern would warrant closer attention.

Of the 10 pages surfaced in the top-launches data (all August 5 modifications, all with 1 day of data), two are showing early signals worth noting. The /registered-dietitian-in-san-antonio-tx/ page already has 90 GSC impressions and a 6.7% CTR after just one day -- that is an unusually fast index and a healthy click-through rate for day one, suggesting Google likely had a prior version of this page indexed and updated its understanding quickly. The /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ page is showing 1,557 GSC impressions with zero clicks -- high impression volume with no clicks strongly suggests the snippet or title is not matching searcher intent, and that page warrants a title/meta description review soon. /weight-management-therapist-in-san-antonio-tx/ and /weight-management-program-in-san-antonio-tx/ show 16 and 56 impressions respectively with zero clicks, which is too early to read but follows a similar pattern to watch. Getting people to actually click is the gap to close.

No new backlinks were earned within the 14-day window itself, though the backlink profile currently shows 4 referring domains, 12 total inbound links, and 5 .edu links -- the .edu count is a meaningful asset for a clinical practice and worth protecting and growing.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Prioritize the /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ snippet.** It's showing 1,557 impressions with zero clicks after one day. That page is appearing in search results -- people just aren't choosing it. Rewrite the title tag and meta description to lead with what the person searching is looking for (support before or after bariatric surgery, not just the service name). This is the highest-leverage single fix available right now.

2. **Build an internal-linking pass across the 20 August 5 pages.** Twenty pages updating in a single day is a significant structural event for the site. Google will recrawl these, and the pages that already have relevant internal links pointing to them from high-authority pages will index and rank faster. Before the next content production cycle, map which existing high-traffic pages can link to these new or updated pages -- especially the eating disorder and bariatric clusters.

3. **Investigate the zero GSC data for the full 14-day window.** The aggregate shows 0 impressions and 0 clicks for every day from July 22 through August 4. That is almost certainly a data connection issue (API auth, property mismatch, or date-range lag) rather than a real drop to zero. Resolving this is a prerequisite for making any confident SEO decisions -- without GSC data, you're flying without instruments during the most active content period BCC has had.

4. **Trace the July 30 traffic spike to its source.** Forty sessions in one day with 119% above-median new users is a meaningful event. If it was a social post, an email, a referral mention, or a directory update, knowing which one tells you what to repeat. Pull the channel breakdown and landing pages for July 30 specifically and document what drove it.

5. **Protect and expand the .edu backlink profile.** Five .edu links across 4 referring domains is a real asset for a clinical practice in a competitive local health space. Identify which institutions are linking and whether there are natural outreach opportunities -- a guest resource, a local student mental health page, a university wellness directory -- that could add a second link from an existing .edu referrer or a new one. This is a slow-build play but one of the most durable things BCC can do for long-term domain authority.
