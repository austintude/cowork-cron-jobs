# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-26

### The window in one sentence

### Volume + composition

Over the 14-day window, the site recorded 850 sessions, 829 users, and 2,020 pageviews. The per-day averages of 60.7 sessions and 59.2 users look reasonable on paper, but they are pulled sharply upward by Aug 16, which alone contributed 476 sessions and 971 pageviews -- more than half the window's total. The true underlying daily run rate is closer to 25--40 sessions, which is a meaningful distinction when evaluating whether momentum is building.

GSC impressions and clicks both read zero across the entire window. This is almost certainly a data pipeline or API connection issue rather than a true signal -- the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 591,971 attributed impressions and 2,486 attributed clicks, so the search presence is real. That said, the missing GSC data means organic performance cannot be evaluated at the day level for this window, and that gap should be investigated.

Channel composition is heavily skewed toward Direct at 647 sessions (76% of total), well above the expected ~60% baseline. Organic Search contributed 151 sessions (18%), below the expected ~25%. The Aug 16 spike almost certainly explains the Direct imbalance -- a spike of that size concentrated in a single day typically suggests an untagged email blast, a social share that spread organically, or a direct link from a high-traffic source that didn't pass UTM parameters. Referral contributed 25 sessions, Organic Social 16, Email only 5, and AI Assistant 3. The AI Assistant channel worth noting -- that's a newer attribution pathway and worth watching over the coming weeks to see if it grows.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves comparison shows a -70.4% drop in sessions from the first half (656 sessions, Aug 11--17) to the second half (194 sessions, Aug 18--24). That number looks alarming, but the Aug 16 spike (476 sessions) sits entirely in the first half and accounts for nearly the entire gap. Remove that outlier and first-half sessions total roughly 180 across six remaining days -- which puts the two halves at near parity, around 28--30 sessions/day each.

What is worth paying attention to, though, is the tail end of the window. Aug 22 and 23 logged only 15 and 12 sessions respectively, both flagged as CRITICAL or MAJOR downward anomalies against the 28-day median. Aug 24 recovered to 27 sessions with a 36% pageviews lift. The anomaly pattern in the second half -- high new-user days Aug 19--20 followed by sharp pullbacks Aug 21--23 -- suggests the site may have caught a brief wave of new interest (possibly from content being indexed or shared) that then settled. The trend heading into the final days of the window is soft. Not a crisis, but not momentum either.

### Launches + efficacy

The most significant content shipped in this window is a cluster of three bariatric psych eval pages published Aug 21: `/what-happens-in-a-bariatric-psych-eval/`, `/bariatric-psychological-evaluation-near-me/`, and `/bariatric-psych-eval-in-texas/`. These are strategically well-targeted -- "bariatric psychological evaluation near me" is a high-intent local query, and the Texas page captures a geographic modifier that could capture statewide telehealth traffic. All three are only 3--5 days old relative to this briefing date, so zero impressions or sessions attributed is expected. These pages need 2--3 weeks minimum before any ranking signal appears.

Also published Aug 22 was the blog post `/self-improvement/mental-health/how-adhd-impacts-eating-habits-and-weight-management/`, targeting "how adhd impacts eating habits and weight management." Three posts were modified Aug 21 -- the bariatric surgery mental health checklist, the emotional eating vs. disordered eating post, and the emotional eating coping post. Modifications to established content can trigger re-crawls and ranking adjustments, so those are worth watching in the next GSC pull.

The 30-day efficacy rollup provides the broader context: 8 pages launched, 87 pages modified, 591,971 GSC impressions attributed, 2,486 clicks, and only 1 page ranking in the top 10 with zero in top 3. That top-10-only-one result against 591,971 impressions is the most important number in this data set. The site is getting seen but not clicked at scale -- a CTR or snippet issue across a large portion of the content library. Backlink totals show 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, and notably 5 .edu links, which is a meaningful authority signal for a site of this size.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Investigate the Aug 16 spike source before next week ends.** 476 sessions in a single day is not random. Check GA4's channel breakdown for that specific date, look for any email sends, social posts, or mentions that day, and determine whether this was a one-time event or something repeatable. If it was an email, the list size and engagement rate are worth knowing. If it was social, identify which post and replicate the format.

2. **Audit title tags and meta descriptions across the content library, starting with pages that have high impressions but low clicks in GSC.** The 30-day rollup -- 591,971 impressions, only 2,486 clicks, one top-10 ranking -- suggests a systemic CTR problem, not just a few underperforming pages. A targeted snippet audit across the top 20--30 impression-generating pages is likely to move the needle more than publishing additional new content right now.

3. **Build internal links from the three new psych eval pages to each other and to the highest-authority existing content on the site.** These pages launched cold -- no internal linking structure around them. Connecting `/bariatric-psychological-evaluation-near-me/` and `/bariatric-psych-eval-in-texas/` to the "what happens in a psych eval" page, and linking all three back to the surgery mental health checklist, strengthens topical authority for the cluster and helps Google understand the relationship between pages.

4. **Diagnose the GSC data gap.** Zero impressions and clicks across 14 consecutive days in the aggregate is not credible given the 30-day rollup data. This is likely a broken API connection or property configuration issue. Resolve it before the next rollup window or trend analysis will remain unreliable.

5. **Develop a second outreach target for .edu backlinks.** The site already has 5 .edu links across 4 referring domains, which is a strong foundation. Identify which institution(s) those links come from and whether a second placement at the same or a related institution is achievable -- .edu referrers carry disproportionate authority weight, and the model is clearly working.
