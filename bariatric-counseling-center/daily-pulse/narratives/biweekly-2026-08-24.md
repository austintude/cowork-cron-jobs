# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-24

### The window in one sentence

The 14-day period ending August 22 was defined almost entirely by a single anomalous traffic event on August 16 -- 476 sessions and 971 pageviews in one day against a baseline of roughly 25 sessions per day -- that inflated the second-half numbers dramatically. Strip that event out and the underlying site is running at a modest but steady pace, with no GSC data available to confirm whether organic search is contributing anything meaningful.

---

### Volume + composition

Over the 14 days from August 9 through August 22, the site recorded 851 sessions, 827 users, and 2,031 pageviews. The per-day averages of 60.8 sessions and 59.1 users look reasonable on paper, but they are artifacts of the August 16 spike. The true day-to-day baseline across most of this window was 11 to 44 sessions, with a median closer to 27. GSC impressions and clicks came in at zero across all 14 days, which is either a data pipeline issue or a sign that organic search is contributing nothing detectable -- that needs to be resolved before any meaningful SEO judgment can be made.

Channel composition skewed heavily toward Direct at 652 sessions (76.6% of total), well above BCC's expected ~60% baseline. Organic Search delivered 148 sessions (17.4%), below the expected ~25%. Referral accounted for 27 sessions (3.2%), also below the expected ~10%. Organic Social added 13 sessions and AI Assistant contributed 3 -- small but worth noting as a new channel showing up consistently. The outsized Direct share combined with the August 16 spike pattern strongly suggests an untagged email send or a social post with no UTM parameters drove that day's traffic. A 22-second average session duration on August 16 (down 81.7% from the 28-day median) supports this: people arrived, glanced, and left -- typical behavior for link-click traffic from a message that didn't set clear expectations about what they'd find.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

On paper, this window looks like a strong acceleration: first half (August 9-15) produced 169 sessions and 510 pageviews, while the second half (August 16-22) produced 682 sessions and 1,521 pageviews -- a 303.6% session increase. That number is not a trend. It is one day (August 16) accounting for 476 of those 682 second-half sessions.

If you remove August 16, the second half delivered 206 sessions across six remaining days -- about 34 per day -- which is a modest, real improvement over the first half's 24-per-day pace. That underlying 40% lift is worth noting: August 17-20 ran consistently above the 28-day median on users, new users, and sessions (ranging from +75% to +122% on new users alone). So there is a genuine small uptick in the second half, likely tied to whatever drove August 16 spilling residual interest into the days that followed. The window ends weak -- August 22 was down 38.8% on sessions versus the 28-day median -- so momentum did not hold into the close of the period.

---

### Launches + efficacy

The substantive work logged in this window was DRB-1146: three psych-eval bottom-of-funnel pages (post IDs 6530-6532) rebuilt and published to staging on August 21, with a preflight diff confirming prod vs. staging was clean and four previously stuck missed-schedule posts republished. This is meaningful work -- psych-eval content sits at a high-intent moment in the patient journey, and rebuilding those pages suggests either a quality or technical issue was identified and addressed. However, because these pages were logged as staging activity as of August 21, it is not yet clear whether they are live on production. They show zero GSC impressions and zero GA4 sessions, which is expected if they haven't pushed to prod yet, but needs confirmation. If they are live, three days is too early to expect ranking signals.

The remainder of the commits in this window are daily pulse logs, SEO snapshot commits, and marketing pulse entries -- routine documentation rather than publishable content. The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 3 pages launched and 105 pages modified across the broader period, with 570,848 GSC impressions and 2,422 clicks attributed to that work -- but zero top-3 or top-10 rankings recorded. That impression volume against flat ranking data suggests the site has pages surfacing in search at positions too low to reliably generate clicks, rather than pages with no visibility at all. No backlinks were earned in this window; the site currently holds 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, and notably 5 .edu links -- that .edu count is an asset worth protecting and expanding.

---

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Identify and tag the August 16 traffic source.** Check whether an email was sent, a social post went out, or a team member shared a link around that date without UTM parameters. If it was an email, that list is active and responsive -- it should be tagged and used intentionally going forward. If it was organic, that's a different and more interesting story that needs to be traced in GA4 source/medium detail.

2. **Resolve the GSC data gap before drawing any SEO conclusions.** Fourteen consecutive days of zero impressions and zero clicks in GSC is not a traffic story -- it is a data story. Verify that the GSC property is connected correctly and that the date range is returning data. Until that is fixed, the 30-day efficacy rollup's impression numbers cannot be reconciled with the 14-day window and no organic search trend analysis is possible.

3. **Confirm the DRB-1146 psych-eval pages are live on production.** If pages 6530-6532 are still on staging, prioritize the prod push and request GSC indexing immediately. These are BOFU pages -- they have the clearest path to conversion -- and every day they sit on staging is a day they can't rank or be found.

4. **Audit the 105 modified pages from the 30-day window against ranking position data.** The gap between high impression volume and zero top-10 rankings suggests a positioning or content-quality issue across a large portion of the modified inventory. Spend next week pulling the top 20 pages by impression count from GSC and checking whether meta titles, headers, and intent alignment match what searchers are actually looking for. That's a faster path to ranking movement than publishing new pages.

5. **Develop a second .edu backlink target.** Five .edu links on a four-domain referring portfolio is a meaningful signal, and .edu links carry authority that is hard to earn in health content. Identify which institution or program generated those links, map what content attracted them, and build a short list of similar academic programs -- bariatric surgery fellowships, nutrition science departments, health psychology programs -- to pitch as a relationship or resource link target for the coming month.
