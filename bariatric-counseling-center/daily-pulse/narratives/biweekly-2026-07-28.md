# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-07-28

### Volume + composition

The window produced 250 total sessions, 224 users, and 2,164 pageviews across 14 days -- averaging 17.9 sessions and 154.6 pageviews per day. At face value, those session and user numbers are unremarkable for BCC's scale, but the pageview average is heavily distorted by two outlier days: July 23 (592 pageviews) and July 24 (952 pageviews). Strip those two days out and the per-day pageview average drops to roughly 45 -- below baseline. GSC shows zero impressions and zero clicks for every day in the window, which is either a data pipeline issue or something more serious worth investigating immediately.

Channel composition came in meaningfully off from BCC's expected baseline. Direct led at 97 sessions (38.8%), followed by Organic Search at 85 (34.0%), Referral at 47 (18.8%), Organic Social at 10 (4.0%), and Email, Unassigned, and AI Assistant accounting for the remainder. The Organic Search share running above its typical ~25% baseline is a modest positive signal -- it suggests the existing content library is generating some search-driven traffic even during a period with no confirmed new rankings. Referral at 18.8% is nearly double the expected ~10%, which is worth tracing: 47 referral sessions in two weeks is meaningful at this site's current traffic level, and identifying the referring source could reveal a link or partnership worth nurturing further.

The July 18-19 trough is the other notable composition story. Sessions dropped to 8 and then 2 on those days -- 59% and 90% below the 28-day median, respectively. This looks like a weekend pattern rather than a technical failure, but the YoY comparisons are also deeply negative (down 86% and 94% vs. the same days last year), which suggests either a seasonal regression or a channel that was active last year and has since gone quiet.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions are exactly flat: 125 in the first half, 125 in the second half, 0.0% trend. On a pure volume basis, nothing moved. But the pageview story is different in a way that matters. The second half generated 1,779 pageviews vs. 385 in the first half -- a 362% increase. That ratio tells you a small group of people in the second half were doing something very different than first-half visitors: they were going deep. Average session duration confirms this. July 23 logged a 447-second average (about 7.5 minutes), and July 24 came in at 250 seconds. Both days were preceded and followed by session durations that crashed below 30 seconds, which is bounce territory.

The honest read here is that the window is neither accelerating nor declining on the metric that matters most for new patient acquisition (sessions). The pageview spike is real but concentrated in one or two days and likely tied to specific page edits reviewed in detail below -- not a sustained audience growth signal. The pattern to watch is whether the second-week session duration behavior (more time on site, deeper browsing) holds into the coming week or collapses back to the shallow-visit pattern that dominated July 18-22.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Investigate the GSC data gap before anything else.** Zero impressions and zero clicks for 14 consecutive days is either a Search Console API disconnection, a property verification lapse, or a genuine indexing problem. This needs to be confirmed or ruled out before content decisions can be made with any confidence. If GSC is broken, every efficacy number in this briefing is incomplete.

2. **Trace the 47 referral sessions.** Referral running at nearly double baseline is the strongest positive signal in this window. Identify which domains are sending that traffic (check GA4's Traffic Acquisition report, source/medium breakdown). If it's a single referring domain doing real work, that's a partnership worth deepening -- a second link request, a co-authored piece, or a resource mention from their side.

3. **Build internal links across the July 23 service page cluster.** The six updated service pages (/therapy-for-eating-disorders-in-san-antonio-tx/, /binge-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/, /emotional-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/, and the others) are all targeting San Antonio-specific queries that likely share searcher intent. Right now they almost certainly exist as islands. Connecting them with deliberate internal links -- including links to and from the new mental health checklist -- will help Google understand the topical cluster and improve crawl equity across all of them.

4. **Plan a second .edu outreach push.** The site has 5 .edu links, which is above average for a regional counseling practice and suggests at least one academic institution has already found the content credible. Identify which institution(s) those links come from and draft a low-friction pitch for a second link from the same domain or a peer institution (UTSA Health, UT Health San Antonio, or similar). A second link from an already-linking .edu carries less lift than a new domain, but it's faster to secure.

5. **Set a 14-day ranking checkpoint for the mental health checklist page.** The target keyword is long-tail and the page is one day old. Put a calendar reminder for August 10 to check GSC for the slug "preparing-for-bariatric-surgery-a-mental-health-checklist" specifically. If it shows impressions but low CTR by then, the meta title and description need revision. If it shows no impressions at all by August 10, request indexing manually via GSC URL Inspection and check whether the page is blocked by robots.txt or a noindex tag.
