# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-11

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day window (Aug 27 -- Sep 9), sessions accelerated 81% from the first half to the second, with the final two days (Sep 8--9) posting the strongest traffic of the entire window. The surge appears driven by an untagged or direct-traffic event rather than organic search, since GSC data returned zero impressions across all 14 days -- a data gap that needs to be resolved before the pattern can be fully trusted.

---

### Volume + composition

Total volume for the window: 357 sessions, 332 users, 987 pageviews, 25.5 sessions per day on average. GSC impressions and clicks both read zero across every day in the window, which is almost certainly a reporting or connectivity issue rather than a true organic blackout -- the site has 609,934 impressions attributed in the 30-day efficacy rollup, so the pipeline is clearly broken for this pull. That data gap affects how confidently we can interpret anything channel-related.

What the channel breakdown does show: Organic Search and Direct tied at 149 sessions each (42% apiece), Email contributed 42 sessions (12%), Organic Social added 10, and Referral brought 6. This is a meaningful departure from BCC's expected baseline of ~60% Direct / ~25% Organic Search. Two things stand out: Direct is lower than expected and Organic Search is higher than expected, which suggests either some Direct traffic has been properly tagged this window (a good sign) or organic content is genuinely pulling more than usual. Email at 12% is also worth noting -- that's above the baseline "other" bucket and suggests a send or automated sequence drove real traffic.

The low Referral number (6 sessions) is consistent with a site that has only 4 referring domains and 12 total inbound links. That's a floor, not a ceiling, and it limits how much organic authority compounds over time.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is clearly accelerating. The first seven days (Aug 27 -- Sep 2) produced 127 sessions and 354 pageviews. The second seven days (Sep 3 -- Sep 9) produced 230 sessions and 633 pageviews -- an 81% jump in sessions and a 79% jump in pageviews. That is a strong directional signal, not noise.

The caveat is that Aug 31 and Sep 1 each logged only 1 session, which looks like a tracking or site outage rather than real traffic behavior. Those two days artificially depress the first-half total, which means the 81% swing overstates the true acceleration somewhat. Even accounting for that, the second half is meaningfully stronger. Sep 8 was the window's peak at 62 sessions and 173 pageviews -- more than double the 28-day median -- and Sep 9 held much of that gain at 46 sessions and 118 pageviews. Something happened around Sep 8 that drove a genuine spike, and identifying the source (email send, social post, untagged campaign) is the most useful thing to chase right now.

---

### Launches + efficacy

The only meaningful content event in this window is a homepage modification on Sep 10 (just outside the window's close). Everything else logged in the launches ledger is git-commit housekeeping -- daily and SEO pulse files, a tag consolidation that was prepped but not shipped. There were no new pages published in the Aug 27 -- Sep 9 window. That means the session gains in this window cannot be attributed to new content -- they came from somewhere else.

The 30-day efficacy rollup tells a more useful story. Over 30 days, 6 pages were launched and 20 were modified. Those pages have accumulated 609,934 GSC impressions and 3,030 clicks, with 1 page currently ranking in the top 10 and none in the top 3. The homepage alone accounts for 302,123 impressions and 920 GA4 sessions, which makes it by far the most trafficked asset. A 0.5% CTR on 302,123 impressions is low -- that's leaving roughly 1,500+ potential clicks per impression cycle on the table if CTR could be nudged to even 1%. The homepage title tag and meta description are worth scrutinizing.

No new backlinks were earned in this window. The current profile sits at 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links, with 5 of those being .edu links -- which is a quality signal worth building on. Zero competitor links is expected but worth tracking as the link-building effort matures.

---

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Fix the GSC pipeline before anything else.** Zero impressions across 14 days is not real -- it is a broken data connection. Until that is resolved, you cannot make reliable SEO decisions for this site. This is the single highest-leverage fix available right now.

2. **Identify what drove Sep 8.** Something sent 62 sessions in a single day -- more than double the median. Check whether an email went out, a social post was published, or a partner shared a link. If it was an email send, document the format and timing so it can be repeated. If it was untagged, get UTM parameters on whatever it was.

3. **Run a CTR audit on the homepage.** 302,123 impressions at 0.5% CTR suggests the title tag or meta description is not compelling enough to earn the click. A revised title that speaks directly to what people living in larger bodies are searching for -- not the practice name -- could meaningfully move sessions without any new content production.

4. **Build a second .edu backlink.** Five .edu links is a real asset for a specialty health practice. Identify which institution or program linked previously and pitch a second placement -- a resource page mention, a counselor profile, or a guest contribution. This is the link-building model that is already working.

5. **Ship a new content page before the next 14-day window closes.** No pages were launched in this entire window. The 30-day rollup shows only 6 pages total over a full month. At that pace, the organic footprint grows slowly. One focused content page -- a specific condition, question, or service cluster -- gives the next rollup something concrete to evaluate.
