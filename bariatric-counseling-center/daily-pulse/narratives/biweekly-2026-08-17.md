# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-17

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day window (Aug 2--15), sessions trended upward 19.9% (first half vs second half), driven primarily by Organic Search -- which is reversed from BCC's typical Direct-dominant baseline and suggests real search visibility movement. The Aug 13 batch of page modifications, which touched the eating disorder and food-relationship cluster, likely contributed to the acceleration visible in the second half.

---

### Volume + composition

Total volume for the window: 310 sessions, 277 users, 1,036 pageviews. Per-day averages were 22.1 sessions and 19.8 users. GSC impressions and clicks both show zero across all 14 days -- this is a data gap worth flagging, not a true zero. Either the GSC property is disconnected from the reporting pipeline or the attribution window hasn't updated. This makes it impossible to assess search ranking movement from impression data alone.

Channel composition is the most interesting data point in this window. Organic Search led at 147 sessions (47%), followed by Direct at 114 (37%), Referral at 34 (11%), and Organic Social at 8 (3%). That is a meaningful departure from BCC's established baseline of roughly 60% Direct / 25% Organic Search. Organic Search at nearly double its expected share suggests something is pulling people in from search -- whether that is a ranking improvement, a newly indexed page gaining clicks, or a GSC discrepancy that makes attribution look heavier on Organic than it is. Referral at 11% is also above baseline (typical is ~10%, but the absolute number of 34 sessions is real and worth noting given the backlink profile discussed below).

Direct traffic at 37% is below the historical norm, which is not alarming on its own -- it often means new visitors are finding BCC for the first time rather than returning directly. If the new-user counts hold (Aug 12 showed 32 new users, +113% vs the 28-day median), that directional read is supported.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is accelerating. First half (Aug 2--8) totaled 141 sessions. Second half (Aug 9--15) totaled 169 sessions -- a 19.9% increase. That improvement happened despite Aug 9 being the lowest-traffic day of the entire window (11 sessions, -42% vs median), which means the recovery and climb in the back half was sharp enough to absorb that dip and still post a meaningful gain.

The anomaly pattern reinforces this read. Aug 9 was a low outlier -- low sessions, low pageviews, but a 159% spike in average session duration, which suggests a very small number of highly engaged users rather than a traffic problem. Aug 10 then reversed completely: +116% pageviews, +52% sessions, +53% new users, all CRITICAL-severity upswings. Aug 12 followed with sessions at +100% vs median and new users at +113%. The final three days (Aug 13--15) settled into a steadier above-median range (24--27 sessions/day). That pattern -- a floor, a spike, then sustained elevation -- suggests the second half is not noise. It looks like a step-change upward, not a single anomalous day.

### Launches + efficacy

The defining event in this window was the Aug 13 page-modification batch: 18 pages touched in a single day, spanning the eating disorder cluster (/binge-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/, /binge-eating-disorder-treatment-san-antonio/, /compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/, /food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/, /food-addiction-treatment-in-texas/, /night-eating-syndrome-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/, /stop-the-cycle-of-binge-eating-you-dont-have-to-face-this-alone/), the post-bariatric support page (/post-bariatric-surgery-support/), multiple weight loss therapy variants, the testimonials page, and a hydration blog post. At only 4 days since launch at the time of reporting, no page in this batch has accumulated meaningful GSC impressions data -- the pipeline shows zeros, consistent with the GSC gap noted above. The two pages that recorded even a single GA4 session (/post-bariatric-surgery-support/ and /professional-weight-loss-therapy/) are technically the best performers in this batch so far, but one session each is not a signal -- it is just proof of indexing.

What is notable is the timing correlation: the traffic acceleration visible from Aug 10 onward predates the Aug 13 modifications, which rules out those changes as the cause of the second-half lift. Something else drove the Aug 10 and Aug 12 spikes -- likely Organic Search, given that channel's above-baseline share for the window. This is worth investigating. The /professional-weight-loss-therapy-2/ and /professional-weight-loss-therapy-fb/ URLs are variant pages that likely serve paid or social landing purposes and should not be expected to rank organically. Their presence in the batch is worth a quick audit to confirm they are not cannibalizing the primary /professional-weight-loss-therapy/ page.

On backlinks: the site currently has 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links total, 5 of which are .edu links. That .edu count is a real asset -- educational institution links carry authority that is disproportionate to the small domain count. No new backlinks were earned inside this 14-day window specifically, but the existing .edu links are the strongest organic authority signal BCC has and should inform outreach strategy going forward.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Fix the GSC data pipeline before making any search decisions.** Fourteen consecutive days of zero impressions and zero clicks from GSC is not real -- it is a reporting failure. Until that is resolved, you cannot assess whether the Aug 13 eating-disorder cluster modifications improved rankings, degraded them, or had no effect. This is the highest-priority diagnostic task for the coming week.

2. **Investigate what actually drove Aug 10 and Aug 12.** The window's two biggest traffic days happened before the Aug 13 modifications. Check GA4 landing page reports for those two dates to identify which pages were the actual entry points. If a specific page drove both spikes, that page is your strongest current organic performer and deserves priority attention -- internal linking, potential expansion, and a GSC rank check once the pipeline is restored.

3. **Audit the eating-disorder cluster for internal linking gaps.** Ten-plus pages in that cluster were modified on the same day, but without a linking structure connecting them, search engines and users treat them as isolated. Next week's strategic task is mapping which pages link to which, identifying the gaps, and building a coherent hub-and-spoke structure around the strongest URL (likely /binge-eating-disorder-treatment-san-antonio/ or /binge-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/ based on slug clarity).

4. **Develop a second .edu backlink pitch.** BCC's 5 .edu links are the site's clearest domain authority signal. Identify which institution(s) those links come from, understand why they linked (resource page, citation, program listing), and replicate that approach with one or two additional Texas-based institutions -- UTSA, UT Health San Antonio, or Texas A&M San Antonio are reasonable targets. One additional .edu link at this domain size has an outsized impact.

5. **Clarify the purpose and status of the variant pages.** /professional-weight-loss-therapy-2/ and /professional-weight-loss-therapy-fb/ suggest they are landing page variants, possibly for paid traffic. If they are active paid landing pages, they should be noindexed to prevent keyword cannibalization with the primary /professional-weight-loss-therapy/ page. If they are deprecated, they should redirect. This is a technical hygiene issue that becomes more urgent as the primary page accumulates any ranking value.
