# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-09

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending August 7, the site pulled 301 sessions at a 21.5/day average, with Organic Search now outpacing Direct traffic by a significant margin -- a meaningful inversion of BCC's historical baseline. The window ended stronger than it started, with August 4-7 showing consistent above-median performance after a soft patch August 1-2.

---

### Volume + composition

The 14-day window produced 301 sessions from 273 users, generating 954 pageviews. Per-day averages of 21.5 sessions and 19.5 users are modest but broadly consistent with the site's current scale. GSC data returned zero impressions and clicks across all 14 days, which almost certainly reflects a data pipeline gap rather than actual search invisibility -- the efficacy rollup shows 345,977 impressions and 1,577 clicks attributed over the last 30 days, so the GSC feed into this aggregate is broken and should be investigated before the next rollup.

Channel composition is the most notable story in this window. Organic Search delivered 162 sessions (54% of total), Direct delivered 100 sessions (33%), Referral 30 sessions (10%), and Organic Social 8 sessions (3%). That is a substantial inversion of BCC's baseline expectation of ~60% Direct / ~25% Organic Search. Organic Search driving the majority of sessions suggests real ranking traction is generating new traffic, not just return visitors navigating directly. This is not a fluke pattern to dismiss.

Referral at 10% aligns with baseline. The site has 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links total, including 5 from .edu domains -- that's a meaningful foundation for a clinical site. Organic Social at 3% and Email nearly absent (1 session) suggest those channels are not currently contributing, which is worth noting if social card work like the August 7 commit is meant to drive traffic.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Session counts declined 17.6% from the first half (165 sessions, July 25-31) to the second half (136 sessions, August 1-7). On its face that's a concerning number. But the daily series tells a more nuanced story: the first half was pulled up by an outlier day on July 30 (40 sessions, nearly double the window average), and the second half started with two genuinely soft days on August 1 (10 sessions) and August 2 (12 sessions) that flagged as CRITICAL anomalies versus the 28-day median. Both of those dips appear to be a weekend trough -- the pattern recovered sharply, with August 4 at 27, August 5 at 23, and August 7 at 27.

Pageviews tell the opposite story: the second half generated 505 pageviews versus 449 in the first half -- a 12.5% increase. That means fewer sessions in the second half were producing more pageviews per session, which points to improving content engagement, not declining interest. Session duration data supports this: August 5 logged an average session duration of 408 seconds (240% above the 28-day median of 120 seconds), and August 4 and August 7 both showed above-median duration as well. The trend is better characterized as choppy-with-acceleration in engagement than as declining.

### Launches + efficacy

The significant production activity in this window happened on August 6, when 13 pages and posts were modified in what appears to be a coordinated content refresh -- including the homepage, the About page, four service pages, several blog posts, and two staff profiles. That is a large batch of changes in a single day, and it's too early (3 days post-launch) to draw conclusions about SERP impact. But the early signals on two of those pages are worth noting.

The "Understanding Weight Loss Medications" page (/understanding-weight-loss-medications-wegovy-ozempic-zepbound-and-mounjaro/) is already the most active non-homepage URL in the top-launches data: 30,914 GSC impressions, 94 clicks, and 42 GA4 sessions attributed. Its CTR is 0.3%, which is low for a page accumulating that many impressions -- this suggests the title or meta description is not pulling clicks relative to how often the page is appearing in search results. That's a fixable problem. The homepage (/) shows 113,304 impressions and 607 GA4 sessions with a 0.6% CTR, which is similarly low for a brand homepage. The "Bariatric Surgery Counseling" page (/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/) logged 1,701 impressions and zero clicks -- zero -- which is a clear signal that the snippet is not working at all and needs immediate attention.

The "Coordinate with Medical Providers" page (/ourprogram/coordinate-with-medical-providers/) is showing 2,774 impressions and 0.4% CTR with only 3 GA4 sessions, which puts it in a similar bucket to the surgery counseling page: visible but not drawing clicks. The backlink picture is stable at 4 referring domains and 5 .edu links, but no new backlinks were noted as earned in this window.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit and fix the GSC data pipeline.** The aggregate is reporting zero impressions across all 14 days while the 30-day rollup shows nearly 346,000 impressions. That discrepancy means trend analysis is flying partially blind. Resolve the feed before the next rollup window closes.

2. **Rewrite the meta descriptions for the surgery counseling page and the medications page.** The surgery counseling page (/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/) has impressions but zero clicks -- that's a snippet problem, not a ranking problem. The medications page is accumulating 30,000+ impressions at 0.3% CTR. Both pages are leaving sessions on the table right now. Prioritize these over new page production this week.

3. **Investigate the July 30 and August 5 spikes to identify the traffic source.** July 30 hit 40 sessions (nearly double average) and August 5 logged 162 pageviews with dramatically elevated session duration. These don't look like organic search wins -- they look like a referral event, an untagged email, or a social share that drove engaged visitors. Identifying the source would tell you what channel is actually working and whether it's repeatable.

4. **Develop a hypothesis for why Organic Search is outperforming Direct by this margin.** The 54% Organic / 33% Direct split is a significant departure from BCC's historical baseline. That's not a bad thing -- it likely means new content is ranking and reaching people who don't already know BCC. But before assuming this is a content win, it's worth checking whether there's been any change in how Direct is attributed (UTM gaps, campaign tracking issues) that might be routing branded traffic into Organic.

5. **Plan internal linking across the August 6 refresh pages.** Thirteen pages were modified in a single day, which is a strong batch of content work -- but if those pages aren't linking to each other and to the core service pages, the authority they generate stays siloed. A focused internal-linking pass this week, while the pages are still fresh in index crawls, is likely to compound whatever ranking lift the refresh produces.
