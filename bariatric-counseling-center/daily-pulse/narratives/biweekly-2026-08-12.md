# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-12

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day period from July 28 to August 10, the site held essentially flat on sessions (149 first half vs. 150 second half, a 0.7% difference) while pageviews accelerated meaningfully in the second half -- 580 vs. 407, a 42% jump -- suggesting that returning or deeper-browsing visitors are reading more per visit, not that raw traffic is growing. The big story of this window is the August 10 wave of page modifications across 13 service and content pages, which is too fresh to evaluate but sets up the next two weeks as a critical measurement period.

### Volume + composition

Over 14 days, the site logged 299 sessions, 267 users, and 987 pageviews. Per-day averages land at 21.4 sessions and 19.1 users -- a quiet but stable baseline for a specialty counseling practice. GSC impressions and clicks both show zero in the aggregate daily series, which is almost certainly a data pipeline issue (the efficacy rollup shows 226,690 impressions and 1,045 clicks attributed over 30 days, so the search presence is real -- the GSC feed into this window's aggregate is simply not populating correctly). That needs to be flagged as a reporting infrastructure problem before it creates confusion in future briefings.

Channel composition this window flipped BCC's expected baseline in a notable way. Organic Search led with 155 sessions (52% of total), compared to the expected ~25%. Direct came in at 102 sessions (34%), well below the typical ~60%. Referral held close to baseline at 32 sessions (11%). Organic Social and Email were minimal at 8 and 1 sessions respectively. An Organic Search share this high -- double the baseline -- suggests either a genuine ranking improvement on existing pages, or that Direct traffic (often from staff, known patients, or untagged email/social links) was suppressed during this period. Worth checking whether any internal or staff browsing filters were active and whether the Direct share recovers to normal levels next week.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions were dead flat: 149 in the first half (July 28 -- August 3), 150 in the second half (August 4 -- August 10). That 0.7% difference is not a trend -- it is a plateau. However, pageviews tell a different story. The first half generated 407 pageviews; the second half generated 580. That is a 42% increase in content consumption with essentially the same number of visitors. The session duration anomalies reinforce this -- August 5 showed average session duration at 408 seconds (240% above the 28-day median), and August 9 and August 10 also flagged well above normal. People who visited in the second half were spending significantly more time on the site.

This pattern -- flat sessions, rising pageviews, rising dwell time -- is not bad news, but it is not growth either. It suggests the site is engaging the people who do arrive, but is not yet pulling in meaningfully more of them week over week. The plateau on sessions is worth monitoring closely over the next 14 days, particularly as the August 10 page modifications begin to get indexed and potentially generate new search entry points.

### Launches + efficacy

The defining event of this window was August 10, when 13 pages were modified and pushed live. These include core service pages (/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/, /bariatric-surgery-psychological-evaluation-in-san-antonio-tx/, /nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/), newer content pages (/what-is-a-bariatric-therapist/, /bariatric-counseling-near-me/, /bariatric-therapist-near-me/), and utility pages like /testimonials/. At two days post-launch, this group is far too fresh for meaningful performance conclusions -- but the early GSC impression data is worth noting.

The high-impression pages at two days out are /nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ (3,854 impressions, 0 clicks), /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ (1,826 impressions, 0 clicks), /bariatric-surgery-psychological-evaluation-in-san-antonio-tx/ (1,460 impressions, 6 clicks, 0.4% CTR), and /testimonials/ (1,429 impressions, 0 clicks). The zero-click pattern across four pages that together are generating over 8,500 impressions is the signal that demands attention in the coming week. High impressions with no clicks at this scale almost always points to a title tag or meta description that is not compelling enough to earn the click, or a SERP position that is deep enough that users are not getting there. The /registered-dietitian-in-san-antonio-tx/ page is the notable exception -- 90 impressions and 6 clicks puts it at a 6.7% CTR, which is a legitimately good number and suggests that page's snippet is working. That one is a model worth studying.

No backlinks were earned within the window itself based on the ledger, though the site's backlink profile sits at 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, and 5 EDU links. The EDU link count is a meaningful asset for a specialty health practice -- those carry authority weight disproportionate to their number.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit title tags and meta descriptions for the four zero-click pages.** /nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/, /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/, /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/, and /testimonials/ are generating thousands of impressions with zero clicks. The /registered-dietitian-in-san-antonio-tx/ page at 6.7% CTR is your control case -- compare its snippet format, length, and specificity against the zero-click pages and rewrite accordingly. This is likely the highest-leverage single action in the coming week.

2. **Investigate the GSC data pipeline.** The aggregate daily GSC impressions and clicks for this window are all zeros, while the 30-day rollup shows 226,690 impressions. That gap is a reporting infrastructure problem, not a traffic problem -- but it will undermine your ability to track momentum if it persists. Confirm whether the GSC feed is scoped correctly to the window dates and that the property connection is pulling correctly.

3. **Build internal links into the August 10 cluster before Google's next crawl.** Thirteen pages modified on the same day likely share thematic territory (bariatric therapy, nutritional counseling, holistic approaches). Link them to each other and to the highest-authority pages on the site now, while Googlebot is still revisiting them post-modification. Internal linking at this stage can accelerate indexation of the updated content and distribute authority across the cluster.

4. **Develop a hypothesis for the Direct traffic suppression.** Direct traffic dropped to 34% of sessions against a ~60% baseline. Before attributing that to anything meaningful, check whether any regular staff or internal users were traveling, whether a known email send or social post was tagged properly (which would pull those sessions into Email or Social rather than Direct), or whether a link shared in a newsletter went untagged and inflated Organic. Understanding the cause tells you whether the Organic Search share is a real win or an artifact.

5. **Use the 5 EDU backlinks as a starting point for a referral outreach list.** BCC has earned EDU links, which is unusual and valuable for a local specialty practice. Identify which institutions those links come from, check whether those same institutions have health, counseling, or nutrition resource pages that could support a second link, and draft a brief outreach sequence. One more EDU link from an existing relationship is a more efficient path than cold outreach to a new domain.
