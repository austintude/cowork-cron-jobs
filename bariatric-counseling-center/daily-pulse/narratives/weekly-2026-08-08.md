# Weekly briefing -- 2026-08-08

### The window in one sentence

Over the 7-day period July 31 through August 6, sessions trended sharply upward -- 81% more in the second half of the window than the first -- driven by a major pageview surge on August 4-5 that coincided with deep site-wide content updates pushed on August 6. GSC data is entirely absent for this window, which is the single biggest gap in understanding whether that traffic surge has any organic search component.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 135 sessions, 122 users, and 494 pageviews across 7 days, averaging 19.3 sessions and 17.4 users per day. Those averages are pulled down by a soft first half (July 31 through August 2), so the raw numbers undersell what actually happened in the back half. The pageview total is heavily skewed by August 4 (110 pageviews) and especially August 5 (162 pageviews), which together account for 55% of the week's total pageviews. GSC impressions and clicks are showing zero across all 7 days -- this is almost certainly a data pipeline issue, not a real signal, but it needs to be confirmed before next week's briefing.

Channel composition this week flipped from BCC's baseline in a meaningful way. Organic Search led with 67 sessions (roughly 50% of total), followed by Direct at 49 sessions (36%), Referral at 13 (10%), and Organic Social at 5 (4%). Organic Search running 25 points above its typical 25% share is notable -- if GSC were reporting, you would expect to see ranking activity underneath it. Direct running below its usual 60% baseline suggests this was not primarily an email or social push driving traffic; people came in through search in unusually high proportions. The Referral count (13 sessions, ~10%) is in line with baseline and does not appear to be a single-source spike.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is clearly accelerating. The first half (July 31 through August 2) produced just 48 sessions and 122 pageviews across 3 days. The second half (August 3 through August 6) produced 87 sessions and 372 pageviews across 4 days -- an 81.2% session increase and a 205% pageview increase in proportional daily terms. That is not a flat week wearing a good disguise; something genuinely shifted around August 3-4.

The session duration anomalies reinforce this. August 2 and 3 showed session duration running 40-49% below the 28-day median, suggesting quick bounces or low-intent visits. August 4 flipped that completely, with duration up 46%. August 5 went further -- average session duration hit 408 seconds, 240% above median, which is a genuinely unusual number. That suggests visitors on August 5 were reading deeply, not skimming. Whether that was a specific content page, a referral audience, or a returning-user pattern is worth pulling in GA4's page-level report to confirm.

### Launches + efficacy

August 6 was a significant publishing day -- 6 page or post modifications plus a cluster of supporting content went out together, including the homepage (/), the About page (/about/), the weight loss medications page (/understanding-weight-loss-medications-wegovy-ozempic-zepbound-and-mounjaro/), the bariatric surgery counseling page (/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/), the psychological evaluation page (/bariatric-surgery-psychological-evaluation-in-san-antonio-tx/), and the medical providers coordination page (/ourprogram/coordinate-with-medical-providers/). Staff profiles and several blog posts also touched. These were modifications, not new launches, so the 30-day efficacy data gives a clearer picture of their pre-existing trajectory.

The weight loss medications page is the most important page to watch coming out of this window. It carries 29,752 GSC impressions and 94 clicks (0.3% CTR) over the 30-day rollup period -- by far the highest impression volume of any tracked page except the homepage. That CTR is low for a page generating that many impressions, which suggests it is ranking but the title or meta description is not compelling people to click. The homepage itself shows 106,049 impressions and 657 clicks (0.6% CTR) over 30 days -- healthy volume, but a 0.6% CTR on 106K impressions leaves a meaningful number of uncaptured clicks. The /about/ page is performing well proportionally: 6,684 impressions and a 1.0% CTR, the strongest click rate in the tracked set.

The bariatric surgery counseling page (/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/) is the one worth flagging as underperforming. It has 1,658 impressions and 0 clicks. Zero clicks on 1,658 impressions after at least 2 days is a snippet problem -- the title or description is not matching what searchers expect to find. The psychological evaluation page has 1,083 impressions and 6 clicks (0.6% CTR), which is modest but at least functional. August 7 commits (social cards, task board, daily pulse) are infrastructure work with no direct traffic impact expected. No new backlinks are reflected in the ledger for this window; the domain sits at 4 referring domains and 12 total inbound links, including 5 .edu links.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Diagnose the August 4-5 pageview surge before it becomes a mystery.** Pull the GA4 page-level report for those two days and identify which pages drove the 110 and 162 pageview spikes. If it was the medications page or homepage, that confirms the August 6 content refresh had an anticipatory effect (or a related referral sent traffic). If it was something else entirely, that's a story worth following.

2. **Fix the GSC data gap.** Seven consecutive days of zero impressions and zero clicks in the pipeline is a data problem, not a ranking problem. Confirm whether this is a property authentication issue, a date-range lag in GSC's API, or a GA4 property mismatch. Without GSC data, you cannot evaluate whether the Organic Search surge this week is from ranking improvements or untagged sources.

3. **Address the /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ snippet.** Zero clicks on 1,658 impressions is a clear action item. Rewrite the meta title and description for that page -- it likely needs to lead with the specific benefit or audience (people preparing for bariatric surgery in San Antonio who need a counselor, not a general descriptor). Test a revised snippet before the next 30-day efficacy window closes.

4. **Prioritize the weight loss medications page CTR.** With 29,752 impressions and a 0.3% CTR, even moving that to 0.6% would roughly double clicks. This page likely ranks for GLP-1 medication terms (Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound, Mounjaro) where searchers have specific, high-intent questions. Rewriting the meta description to answer a specific question -- rather than describe the page -- is the lever to pull here.

5. **Develop a backlink strategy beyond current referring domains.** Four referring domains total and 12 inbound links is thin for a specialty clinic that needs local and clinical authority signals. The 5 .edu links are a genuine asset. Identify which .edu domains those are, and use that as a model to approach 2-3 similar institutions (community colleges with health programs, local university departments, hospital systems) for a second or third .edu link. This is a multi-week project, not a single-day task, and the window is the right moment to start planning it.
