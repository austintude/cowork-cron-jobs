# Weekly briefing -- 2026-08-14

### The window in one sentence

The Aug 6--12 window closed with a strong upward surge -- sessions jumped 57% from the first half to the second half of the week, with Aug 12 delivering the highest single-day total (37 sessions, 102 pageviews) in the tracked period. The bulk of that lift came from Organic Search, which is outperforming the channel baseline and suggests content published in recent weeks is beginning to earn search visibility.

---

### Volume + composition

Over the seven days ending Aug 12, the site recorded 154 sessions, 138 users, and 470 pageviews. Per-day averages were 22 sessions and 19.7 users -- modest numbers in absolute terms, but the trajectory within the window matters more than the totals (see Trend section below). GSC impressions and clicks both show zero for this window, which is almost certainly a data lag or integration gap rather than a true absence of search activity -- the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 393,317 GSC impressions attributed to recent launches, so the pipe is working.

Channel composition this week departed noticeably from BCC's baseline. Organic Search led with 74 sessions (48% of total), well above the expected ~25% share. Direct came in second at 57 sessions (37%), below its usual ~60% baseline. Referral held close to baseline at 18 sessions (12%). Organic Social and Email together contributed just 4 sessions, which is normal given BCC's limited social posting cadence. The Organic Search overrepresentation is the most meaningful signal here -- it is not a fluke, since it held across multiple days. It likely reflects content published in July and early August beginning to rank and pull clicks, even without confirmed GSC data for this specific window.

The suppressed Direct share is worth monitoring but is not alarming on its own. One likely explanation: when Organic Search volume rises, its proportional share of the mix naturally compresses Direct's percentage even if Direct volume stays stable. Confirm that by checking Direct raw session counts against prior weeks before treating the drop as a concern.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is clearly accelerating. The first half (Aug 6--8) produced 60 sessions and 166 pageviews across three days. The second half (Aug 9--12) produced 94 sessions and 304 pageviews across four days. Normalizing for the extra day, second-half daily averages (23.5 sessions, 76 pageviews) still outpace first-half averages (20 sessions, 55 pageviews) -- and the final day of the window, Aug 12, hit 37 sessions and 102 pageviews, both well above the 28-day median and flagged as CRITICAL on the upside. That is a meaningful close.

The session duration data cuts against a clean "everything is good" read, though. Aug 12's session duration dropped 58% below the 28-day median (52 seconds vs a median of ~125 seconds) even as sessions spiked. That pattern -- high volume, low engagement time -- often indicates a large share of new or bouncing visitors who landed on a page and left quickly. It could be paid traffic, a social share, or a referral burst. Worth checking the Aug 12 channel breakdown specifically to see what drove that day's spike before treating it as organic growth momentum.

### Launches + efficacy

All of the tracked launches hit on Aug 13 -- technically one day after this window closes -- so there is no in-window launch data to assess from a traffic standpoint. However, the Aug 13 publish batch is large enough (13 page/post modifications plus 4 sitemap touches) that it warrants close attention as the primary story going into next week. The batch covers three clusters: eating disorder treatment pages (binge eating, compulsive overeating, food addiction, night eating syndrome), weight loss therapy landing page variants (including fb- and -2 suffixed slugs that appear to be ad landing page tests), and supporting blog content (emotional eating, hydration, pre-bariatric binge eating screening).

Two pages in that batch are already showing early GSC activity despite being only one day old. `/food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/` earned 486 impressions and 6 clicks (1.2% CTR) within 24 hours. `/compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/` earned 920 impressions and 6 clicks (0.7% CTR) in the same timeframe. Those are genuinely strong out-of-the-gate numbers -- 920 impressions on day one suggests Google is surfacing that page for multiple queries immediately, likely because it is a refresh of an existing indexed URL rather than a brand-new slug. The CTR of 0.7% is low relative to what a well-positioned snippet should earn, which suggests the page is appearing but not yet ranking high enough to pull strong click rates. That is a natural early-stage pattern; what matters is whether impressions hold and grow over the next 14 days.

The remaining eight modified pages show zero impressions and zero sessions after one day -- also normal for pages not yet re-crawled. The landing page variants (`/professional-weight-loss-therapy-2/`, `/professional-weight-loss-therapy-fb/`, `/expert-weight-loss-therapy/`) read as ad destination tests rather than organic plays. They should not be evaluated on GSC metrics; instead, check conversion tracking to confirm they are properly tagged if paid campaigns are running to them. The 30-day rollup shows zero ranking pages at top-3 or top-10, which remains the core SEO gap -- the site is generating impressions but not yet locking in first-page positions.

No new backlinks were earned inside this specific window based on the ledger data. The overall backlink profile stands at 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, and 5 edu links -- the edu link count is a genuine asset and above average for a practice of this size.

1. **Track the eating-disorder cluster for ranking movement over the next 10 days.** `/compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/` and `/food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/` showed day-one impressions that signal Google indexed and is testing them immediately. Pull their GSC position data by next Friday. If either is sitting in positions 11--20, a targeted internal-linking push from the blog cluster (emotional eating, pre-bariatric screening posts) could nudge them into page one.

2. **Audit the Aug 12 traffic spike for source.** Sessions doubled the 28-day median that day, but session duration collapsed to 52 seconds. That combination suggests a channel or referral source delivering low-intent visitors. Identifying the source will tell you whether Aug 12 is a meaningful growth signal or noise to filter out of the trend line.

3. **Confirm ad landing page tagging before running traffic to the `-2` and `-fb` variants.** Three of the Aug 13 modified pages appear to be paid or social landing page variants. If campaigns are active or planned, verify UTM parameters and GA4 goal tracking are in place now -- before spend goes behind them -- so performance data is clean from day one.

4. **Leverage the edu backlink profile for outreach.** Five edu links is a strong foundation for a specialty practice. Identify which institutions those links come from and whether a follow-up relationship (guest post, resource page addition, co-branded content) could generate a second link from the same domain. A second link from a high-authority edu referrer is easier to earn than a first link from a new one.

5. **Set a 14-day GSC review date for the night eating syndrome and binge eating disorder pages.** `/night-eating-syndrome-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/` and `/binge-eating-disorder-treatment-san-antonio/` showed zero impressions at 24 hours. That is not unusual, but if they are still at zero by Aug 27, request manual indexing via Google Search Console and review whether the pages are blocked by a noindex tag or have thin content that is suppressing crawl priority.
