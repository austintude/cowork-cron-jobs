# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-07-29

### The window in one sentence

Over July 14--27, sessions grew 16% from first half to second half (117 vs. 136), but the real story is a dramatic pageview spike on July 23--24 tied directly to a same-day update of five core service pages -- not a traffic volume breakthrough but a clear signal that on-site engagement depth jumped sharply when that content went live. GSC data is entirely absent for this window, which is the most pressing unresolved question in the dataset.

---

### Volume + composition

Total sessions across 14 days came to 253, with a per-day average of 18.1. Users totaled 227 (16.2 per day) and pageviews hit 2,176 -- though that number is heavily skewed by two outlier days (July 23 and 24 alone account for 1,544 of those pageviews, or 71% of the window total). GSC impressions and clicks are zeroed out across every day in the window, which almost certainly reflects a data pipeline or connection issue rather than a genuine absence of search visibility. That needs to be resolved before any SEO assessment is meaningful.

Channel composition for the window: Direct led at 97 sessions (38%), Organic Search was close behind at 88 (35%), Referral contributed 49 (19%), Organic Social 8, Email 6, AI Assistant 4, and Unassigned 6. That Organic Search share -- 35% -- is notably above BCC's baseline expectation of around 25%, which suggests either a modest ranking improvement is pulling real searchers in, or that some Direct traffic is being underclassified. Referral at 19% is also above the ~10% baseline; that's worth watching, particularly given the backlink profile. The AI Assistant channel logging 4 sessions is a small but real number worth tracking as a separate trend over time.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions are technically accelerating -- 117 in the first half, 136 in the second, a 16.2% gain. But that headline number masks a more complicated picture. The first half included a near-complete traffic collapse on July 19 (2 sessions, 4 pageviews -- both flagged as CRITICAL anomalies, down ~90% vs. the 28-day median), which depressed the first-half total. Strip that day out and the halves look much more comparable in session volume.

Where the second half clearly and genuinely outperformed is pageviews: 379 in the first half versus 1,797 in the second -- a 374% difference. That is almost entirely explained by July 23--24, when session duration also spiked to 447 and 250 seconds respectively (both flagged CRITICAL-up). People who came to the site on those two days read significantly more pages and stayed significantly longer. That pattern is meaningful engagement, not just raw traffic. The days immediately following (July 25--26) snapped back to low pageview counts, which suggests the spike was event-driven rather than a baseline shift.

### Launches + efficacy

The only new page that published inside this window is "Preparing for Bariatric Surgery: A Mental Health Checklist" (/self-improvement/mental-health/preparing-for-bariatric-surgery-a-mental-health-checklist/), which went live July 27. At 2 days old, it has already logged 3 GA4 sessions -- a small but legitimate early signal. It has no GSC impressions yet, which is completely normal for the first one to two weeks. No ranking position is tracked yet. This page is on the right trajectory; check back at the two-week mark.

The more consequential action in this window was the July 23 batch modification of five existing service pages: the homepage (/), Therapy for Eating Disorders in San Antonio (/therapy-for-eating-disorders-in-san-antonio-tx/), Nutritional Counseling (/nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/), Holistic Weight Loss Counseling (/holistic-weight-loss-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/), and Body Confidence Counseling (/body-confidence-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/). These updates landed the same day as the 592-pageview spike and a 300% surge in average session duration. That correlation is strong enough to treat as a working hypothesis: whoever visited on July 23--24 found enough value in those updated pages to navigate deeply. Whether that traffic came from someone reviewing the changes internally, an external referral, or an early crawler reaction is unclear -- but the engagement depth is real.

On the backlink side, the site currently shows 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links total, and notably 5 EDU links. Zero new backlinks were logged as earned specifically within this 14-day window, but the EDU link count is a genuine asset -- those carry outsized authority and suggest an institutional relationship worth maintaining and building on.

The 30-day efficacy rollup reports 368,754 GSC impressions attributed to launches, with 1,583 clicks and 1,089 GA4 sessions -- but zero top-3 or top-10 rankings tracked. That combination (large impression volume, no tracked top-10 positions) suggests either the ranking tracker is not yet capturing the right keywords, or the site is generating impressions primarily from positions 11--30 where clicks are minimal.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Resolve the GSC data gap first.** Every day in this window shows zero GSC impressions -- that is almost certainly a connection or property configuration issue, not reality. Without GSC data, you cannot evaluate whether the July 23--24 pageview spike had any organic search component, and you cannot assess whether the five updated service pages are gaining or losing position. This is the highest-priority diagnostic task for the coming week.

2. **Audit what actually drove July 23--24.** Before attributing the pageview and session-duration spike to the service page updates, check GA4's page-level report to see which specific URLs were consumed on those two days, and check Referral sources for those sessions. If it was an internal review session or a specific referrer, that changes the interpretation entirely. If it genuinely was distributed across the updated service pages, that is strong evidence those edits improved content quality in a way users responded to.

3. **Build an internal linking structure connecting the new mental health checklist to the eating-disorder and body-confidence service pages.** The checklist page (/preparing-for-bariatric-surgery-a-mental-health-checklist/) lives in the self-improvement/mental-health section but its natural neighbors are the Therapy for Eating Disorders and Body Confidence pages -- which were just updated. Linking them bidirectionally now, while the checklist is still in its indexing-warmup phase, gives Google's crawlers a clearer content cluster signal from the start.

4. **Investigate the EDU backlink sources and identify a second institution to pitch.** Five EDU links on a site with only 4 referring domains is a meaningful concentration. Identify which institution(s) those links come from and whether there is a relationship there that could be expanded -- a second page linked, a resource cited, or a new institutional partner approached using the same model that generated the first EDU link.

5. **Establish keyword tracking for the five updated service pages.** The 30-day rollup shows zero top-10 rankings tracked, but the site is clearly generating GSC impressions at scale (368K attributed over 30 days). Pick two to three target keywords per updated service page and get them into your rank tracker so the next rollup can show whether the July 23 edits are moving positions, holding, or sliding.
