# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-20

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day window (Aug 5--18), sessions totaled 805 with a 375% surge from first half to second half -- almost entirely attributable to a single anomalous spike on August 16 that drove 476 sessions in one day, likely a bot sweep, a viral social share, or an untagged campaign send. Strip that day out and the underlying baseline is steady to modestly up, not dramatically changed.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 805 sessions from 775 users across 2,001 pageviews. Per-day averages sit at 57.5 sessions and 55.4 users, but those numbers are heavily distorted by August 16. On a typical day in this window, sessions ranged from 11 to 37 -- call it a real baseline of 18--30 sessions per day, which is consistent with BCC's historical norm.

GSC data returned zero impressions and zero clicks across all 14 days. This is almost certainly a data pipeline issue rather than a real signal -- the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 503,059 attributed impressions and 2,168 clicks, so the property is absolutely indexed and ranking. Worth flagging to investigate before the next briefing cycle.

Channel composition came in at roughly 75% Direct, 18% Organic Search, 4% Referral, and the remainder scattered across Organic Social, Email, Organic Video, and AI Assistant. That is meaningfully skewed from BCC's expected baseline of 60/25/10/5. Direct is running about 15 points hot -- a pattern that almost always means one of two things: untagged email or social traffic bleeding into Direct, or bot/scraper traffic that doesn't carry UTM parameters. Given the August 16 spike, the bot/scraper explanation deserves serious consideration. Organic Search is running light at 18% versus the expected 25%, which could reflect normal variation or a real softness in click-through from search -- hard to confirm without functioning GSC data.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

On paper, the second half of the window outperformed the first by 375% -- 665 sessions versus 140. In practice, that number should not be read as genuine acceleration. August 16 alone contributed 476 sessions, which is more than the entire first half of the window. If you remove that single day, the second half total falls to roughly 189 sessions, which is a more honest 35% increase over the first half. That softer number is still worth noting -- Aug 12--15 each showed sessions 30--45% above the 28-day median according to the anomaly data, suggesting a genuine mild lift in the days leading into and following the spike.

What that lift was driven by is not confirmed in the data. The page modifications to the binge eating page and the post-bariatric surgery support page on August 13 are the most plausible candidates -- those are content-type changes likely to attract return visits or shares. The underlying trend, ignoring August 16, reads as modestly positive. Not accelerating, but not flat either.

### Launches + efficacy

The only substantive content work that shipped inside this window was two page modifications on August 13: the binge eating page (/stop-the-cycle-of-binge-eating-you-dont-have-to-face-this-alone/) and the post-bariatric surgery support page (/post-bariatric-surgery-support/). Everything else in the ledger is git-commit pipeline activity -- daily and SEO pulse commits -- which are operational, not content launches. No new pages were published during this 14-day window.

With GSC data returning zero for this window, it's not possible to measure direct ranking or impression impact from the August 13 modifications yet. The 30-day efficacy rollup offers some context: across the prior 30 days, three pages launched, 118 were modified, and attributed impressions reached 503,059 with 2,168 clicks. No pages are currently ranking in the top 3 or top 10 tracked positions, which is the most significant gap in the current content program. The site is generating impressions at scale but not converting them into top-of-page placements.

On the backlink side, the site currently holds 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links, 5 of which are from .edu sources. That's a lean profile for a medical practice competing in a health-adjacent space, but the .edu concentration is a real asset -- those carry authority. No new backlinks were added in this window.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Investigate the August 16 spike before drawing any conclusions from it.** Check GA4's channel breakdown for that day specifically -- if Direct carried the vast majority of those 476 sessions with sub-30-second average session duration (the data shows avg session duration dropped 81.7%), this reads as bot traffic or a scraper run, not a real audience event. If it was a campaign send or a social post, find the source and tag it going forward so it routes to the correct channel.

2. **Resolve the GSC data gap.** Fourteen consecutive days of zero GSC impressions while the 30-day rollup shows half a million attributed impressions means the pipeline feeding GSC into this briefing is broken, not the site. Fix the connection before the next window closes -- without GSC you're flying without a ranking instrument.

3. **Audit the August 13 page modifications for internal linking.** The binge eating and post-bariatric pages are likely the most plausible source of the mild session lift observed Aug 12--15. If those pages were meaningfully updated, make sure they have strong internal links pointing to them from related pages -- that's the fastest way to surface them to people already navigating the site.

4. **Build a pitch for a second .edu backlink.** Five .edu links from four referring domains is a stronger foundation than most local practices have. Identify which .edu domain is already linking and find a second institution -- UTSA, UT Health San Antonio, or a dietetics program -- where BCC's counselors could contribute a resource, guest post, or citation that earns a second academic-domain link.

5. **Set a content publishing goal for the next two weeks.** No new pages shipped in this 14-day window, and the 30-day rollup shows only 3 new pages against 118 modifications. Modifications are useful maintenance but they rarely generate new impressions at scale the way new indexed pages do. Identify one net-new page to publish in the next 7--10 days -- ideally targeting a query cluster that has impressions in the 30-day rollup but no top-10 ranking yet.
