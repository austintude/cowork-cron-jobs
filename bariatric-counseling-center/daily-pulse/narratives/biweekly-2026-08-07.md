# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-07

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending August 5, sessions held steady at a 21.5 daily average with a modest 9% acceleration from the first week to the second, driven primarily by Organic Search -- which ran well above BCC's 25% baseline at 52% of all traffic. The July 30 spike (40 sessions, double the 28-day median) and a strong close on August 4-5 (pageviews up 96% and 189% respectively vs. median) suggest the site is building quiet momentum, though the August 1-2 valley and persistent GSC data gaps leave real questions unanswered.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 301 sessions, 272 users, and 2,374 pageviews across 14 days. Per-day averages sat at 21.5 sessions and 19.4 users -- neither remarkable nor alarming at face value. GSC data shows zero impressions and zero clicks for the entire window, which almost certainly reflects a data pipeline gap rather than actual search invisibility; the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 415,077 attributed impressions and 2,105 clicks, so the property is very much alive in search. That said, the missing GSC data for this specific window makes it impossible to confirm whether the traffic gains here came from ranking improvements or something else.

Channel composition is the most interesting story. Organic Search delivered 155 of 301 sessions (52%), which is more than double BCC's 25% baseline. Direct came in at 97 sessions (32%), below the expected 60%. Referral added 36 sessions (12%), slightly above baseline. Organic Social contributed 11 sessions, and AI Assistant and Email each contributed 1. The Organic Search dominance is notable -- it suggests either a ranking gain on existing content, or that Direct traffic is being suppressed (possibly untagged email or social clicks being miscategorized). The low Direct share is worth watching; if it stays low next week, it may indicate a drop in repeat or branded traffic rather than a category shift.

Pageviews show a split personality. July 23-24 produced 592 and 952 pageviews respectively -- numbers that are dramatically higher than the rest of the window -- and those two days alone account for roughly 65% of total pageviews. From July 25 forward, daily pageviews normalized to the 20-170 range. The July 23-24 anomaly may reflect a crawl, a bot visit pattern, or a single session that hit dozens of pages. This deserves a direct look in GA4 -- if one or two users drove those numbers, they should be filtered before drawing conclusions about real engagement.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions grew 9% from the first half (144 sessions, July 23-29) to the second half (157 sessions, July 30-August 5). That's a real but modest acceleration. The story inside that 9% is more textured: July 30 was a genuine spike at 40 sessions (105% above median), likely from an external source -- an email send, a social post, or a referral link -- but August 1-2 dropped sharply (10 and 12 sessions, both flagged as CRITICAL anomalies at -50% and -40% below median). The second half recovered cleanly on August 4-5, with pageviews on August 5 reaching 162 (189% above median) and average session duration jumping to 408 seconds (241% above median). That August 5 engagement signal is genuinely encouraging -- people were not just landing, they were reading.

So the window is best described as volatile but directionally accelerating. The July 30 spike and August 4-5 recovery pull the second half above the first, but the August 1-2 valley means momentum is not yet consistent. The average session duration pattern -- crashing on July 29 (-61%), bottoming out August 1-3, then surging on August 4-5 -- suggests that whatever drove the July 30 spike brought a lot of low-engagement visitors, while the August 4-5 traffic was smaller in volume but meaningfully more engaged.

### Launches + efficacy

Everything that shipped in this window launched on August 6, which is one day before this briefing covers. That means none of the August 6 modifications had time to influence the July 23-August 5 analytics window. The session and pageview data in this briefing reflects the state of the site before those changes went live. That's an important framing note: what performed during this window was older content, not the August 6 batch.

On the August 6 launches themselves, the 30-day attribution data gives us useful early signals even without window-specific GSC data. The weight loss medications page (/understanding-weight-loss-medications-wegovy-ozempic-zepbound-and-mounjaro/) is the standout -- 28,477 cumulative GSC impressions and 42 GA4 sessions, but only a 0.3% CTR. That is a page with significant search visibility that is not converting to clicks. The title or meta description is likely underselling the content. The homepage (/) continues to dominate by volume with 99,821 impressions and 629 clicks at 0.6% CTR -- that's the traffic anchor. The About page (/about/) has the strongest CTR of the tracked pages at 1.0%, which suggests its title and snippet are well-matched to searcher intent. The bariatric surgery counseling page (/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/) earned 1,609 impressions but zero clicks -- a title/snippet problem worth addressing immediately. The self-sabotage, goal-setting, and emotional eating posts show zero impressions and zero sessions at one day old, which is normal; those need 2-4 weeks before any conclusions are warranted.

Backlinks: the site currently holds 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links total, including 5 edu links. No new backlinks were recorded in this window. The edu link count is a genuine asset for a health-adjacent practice; those carry real authority weight. No competitor links in the profile is a clean signal. The referring domain count of 4 is slim for a practice trying to rank in a competitive local health market -- this is a longer-term gap that needs a deliberate outreach strategy.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit the weight loss medications page CTR.** The /understanding-weight-loss-medications-wegovy-ozempic-zepbound-and-mounjaro/ page has 28,477 impressions and a 0.3% CTR. That's a high-visibility page leaving clicks on the table. Spend next week rewriting the title tag and meta description -- test language that speaks directly to people researching GLP-1 medications, not just naming the drugs. A 0.3% to 1.0% CTR improvement on 28k impressions would meaningfully move session counts.

2. **Diagnose the July 23-24 pageview anomaly before drawing engagement conclusions.** Those two days account for roughly 1,544 of 2,374 total pageviews (65%) but sessions were only 17 and 28 respectively. Something -- likely a bot, a crawler, or a misconfigured tag -- inflated pageviews on those days. Filter those sessions in GA4 by user type and page depth, and apply a bot filter if warranted. Leaving this unexamined skews every pageview-based metric in the window.

3. **Investigate the Organic Search channel share.** Organic Search running at 52% of sessions vs. a 25% baseline is a significant deviation. Pull the specific queries and landing pages from GSC for this window (once the data pipeline is restored) to confirm whether this reflects real ranking gains, or whether Direct traffic is being miscategorized. If it's real, identify which pages are driving it and prioritize them for internal linking support.

4. **Build a backlink outreach plan for the next 30 days.** With only 4 referring domains, the site is link-thin for a practice competing in a local healthcare market. The 5 existing edu links suggest institutional credibility is already there. Identify 3-5 local or regional partners -- hospital systems, university health programs, bariatric surgery programs -- and pitch a resource link or co-authored content placement. One new high-authority referring domain in the next month would likely have more SEO impact than 10 new pages.

5. **Repair the GSC data gap before the next rollup.** Zero GSC impressions for 14 consecutive days is a reporting failure, not a traffic reality. Confirm that the Search Console property is verified, that the data connector is live, and that the date range being queried matches the GA4 property. Without GSC data, it's not possible to assess ranking movement or tie content changes to organic performance -- which is the core question this briefing is trying to answer.
