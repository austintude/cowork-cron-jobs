# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-10

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day window (Aug 27 -- Sep 9), sessions accelerated 81% from the first week to the second, driven by a significant two-day surge on Sep 8-9 that pushed users and pageviews well above the 28-day median. GSC data is completely absent for the entire period, which is the most important data gap to resolve before drawing conclusions about organic search health.

---

### Volume + composition

The site recorded 357 total sessions and 332 users across 14 days, averaging 25.5 sessions and 23.7 users per day. Total pageviews came in at 987, averaging roughly 70 per day. GSC impressions and clicks are zeroed out across every single day in the window -- this is almost certainly a data pipeline or property connection issue, not a genuine reflection of search performance, and it needs to be investigated and fixed before any organic search conclusions can be made.

Channel composition this window split almost exactly 42% Organic Search / 42% Direct / 12% Email / 3% Organic Social / 2% Referral. That is a meaningful departure from BCC's baseline (roughly 60% Direct / 25% Organic Search). The Organic Search share being this elevated -- tied with Direct -- is a positive signal if real, suggesting content is picking up search visibility. Email at 12% (42 sessions) is also notably above baseline and likely reflects a send or two during the window; that volume warrants checking what went out and whether it drove the Sep 8-9 spike. Direct being lower than baseline may indicate that the email attribution is pulling sessions that would otherwise fall into Direct, rather than representing a true drop in direct audience.

The Referral channel (6 sessions) is below BCC's typical ~10% baseline, and Unassigned at 4 sessions suggests a small number of untagged touchpoints. Neither of those is alarming at current volumes, but the Referral underperformance is worth noting given the site currently has 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links total.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves comparison tells a clear story: the first half (Aug 27 -- Sep 2) produced 127 sessions, while the second half (Sep 3 -- Sep 9) produced 230 sessions -- an 81% jump. Pageviews followed the same pattern, going from 354 in the first half to 633 in the second. On its face, this is an accelerating window, which is good news.

However, the trajectory deserves some scrutiny. The first half was dragged down significantly by Aug 31 and Sep 1, which each recorded just 1 session -- almost certainly a tracking or holiday-weekend data gap rather than real site abandonment. Strip those two days out and the first-half average is considerably less dramatic. The true acceleration story is the Sep 8-9 two-day surge (62 and 46 sessions respectively, both flagged as CRITICAL anomalies running 113-115% above the 28-day median). That event drove the second-half totals. If the surge was Email-driven (which the channel data suggests is likely), the underlying organic and direct trend is probably steadier than the 81% figure implies -- not declining, but not dramatically accelerating either.

---

### Launches + efficacy

All commits logged in this window are internal pulse and SEO/marketing briefing files -- daily operational documents, not public-facing content pages. Every single launch entry in the ledger is a git-commit type with zero attributed GSC impressions, zero GA4 sessions, and no SERP position data. These are the system's own daily briefings being tracked as "launches," which is expected behavior but means there is nothing in this window's launch ledger that has meaningful content efficacy to report on.

The 30-day efficacy rollup provides better signal: 6 actual pages have been launched in the past 30 days, alongside 19 page modifications and 101 commits. Those 6 pages have accumulated 307,811 GSC impressions and 1,494 GSC clicks in aggregate over their lifetime -- that is a real and substantive number. However, only 1 page is ranking in the top 10, and none are in the top 3. That ratio (6 pages, 307K impressions, 1 top-10 ranking) suggests the pages are surfacing in search but mostly in positions where click-through rates are low. The 1,494 clicks attributed against 307,811 impressions works out to roughly a 0.5% CTR, which is consistent with pages sitting in the 15-30 position range rather than positions 1-10.

On backlinks: the site has 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links total, with 5 of those being .edu links. No new backlinks are recorded as earned within this specific window. The .edu link count is disproportionately strong relative to the domain total and represents a real authority asset worth actively protecting and building on.

---

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Diagnose the GSC data outage first.** Zero impressions and zero clicks across 14 consecutive days is a broken connection, not a content problem. Reconnect the GSC property in GA4 and confirm the data feed is live before the next rollup. Without GSC data, organic search trend analysis is essentially blind.

2. **Identify what drove Sep 8-9 and document it as a repeatable pattern.** Sessions more than doubled the daily median on both days. The Email channel volume in this window (42 sessions, 12% share) is the most likely cause. If an email send went out around Sep 7-8, audit what content was linked, what the open/click rate looked like, and whether the audience segment can be replicated. If the source turns out to be something else -- a social mention, a referral spike -- that changes the play, but right now Email is the most probable explanation.

3. **Run a snippet and title-tag audit on the 6 pages launched in the last 30 days.** A 0.5% CTR against 307K impressions strongly suggests these pages are earning impressions but not compelling clicks. Review the title tags and meta descriptions for each page to ensure they are specific, direct, and written for the person searching -- not for the algorithm. Pages in positions 10-30 can often move up in clicks without any ranking improvement just by improving the snippet.

4. **Build a second .edu backlink proactively.** You currently have 5 .edu links across 4 referring domains. That is unusually strong for a site this size and suggests an existing relationship with at least one academic institution. Identify which institution(s) those links come from, then find the right contact at UTSA, UT Health San Antonio, or another local program to pitch a second link -- a resource mention, a citation on a bariatric or eating-disorder resource page, or a community partnership reference.

5. **Establish a clear content page tracking list separate from the pulse/commit ledger.** The launch ledger right now is dominated by internal briefing commits, which obscures visibility into actual public content performance. A simple separate log -- page slug, launch date, target keyword, current position, impressions, clicks -- would make the next rollup's efficacy section substantially more actionable and give you a clean view of which pages need attention after the 14-day mark.
