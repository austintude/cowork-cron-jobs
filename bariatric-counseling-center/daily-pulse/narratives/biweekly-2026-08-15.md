# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-15

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending August 13, sessions are up 18.5% (first half vs. second half), driven by a channel mix that flipped from BCC's typical Direct-first baseline to Organic Search holding the top spot -- a meaningful structural shift. The second half closed on back-to-back high-volume days (August 10 and August 12) that suggest something is beginning to gain traction organically, even though GSC data is currently returning zero for the entire window.

### Volume + composition

The window produced 295 sessions, 264 users, and 991 pageviews across 14 days -- averages of 21.1 sessions/day and 18.9 users/day. Those are modest numbers in absolute terms, but the pageviews-to-sessions ratio of 3.36 is healthy, suggesting people who do arrive are browsing multiple pages rather than bouncing off a single one. GSC impressions and clicks are reported as zero for every day in the window, which almost certainly reflects a data pipeline or property-connection issue rather than a genuine search visibility collapse -- the channel data (142 sessions from Organic Search) confirms Google is sending people; GSC just isn't recording it right now.

Channel composition is the most notable structural story in this window. Organic Search produced 142 sessions (48% of total), well above BCC's baseline expectation of roughly 25%. Direct came in at 110 sessions (37%), below the expected ~60%. Referral contributed 33 sessions (11%), which is modestly above baseline, and Organic Social added 7. The reduced Direct share isn't necessarily bad news -- it may simply reflect fewer untagged email or social sends this period, which would make the Organic Search lift look even more meaningful in comparison. That said, the elevated Referral number is worth noting: 33 sessions from referrals suggests at least one external source sent a meaningful cluster of visitors, which aligns with the site's current backlink profile (12 inbound links from 4 referring domains, including 5 .edu links).

### Trend (first half vs. second half)

The window is accelerating. First-half sessions totaled 135 (July 31 -- August 6); second-half sessions totaled 160 (August 7 -- August 13), an 18.5% gain. Pageviews were nearly flat across the two halves (494 vs. 497), which means the session growth in the second half wasn't accompanied by deeper browsing -- people came more often but at roughly the same average depth. The more telling detail is where the second-half growth is concentrated: August 10 (29 sessions, 118 pageviews, flagged CRITICAL-up on three metrics) and August 12 (37 sessions, 102 pageviews, flagged CRITICAL-up on new users at +113% vs. 28-day median). August 12 is the single highest-session day in the window.

There's a counter-signal worth naming honestly: August 12 also recorded a CRITICAL drop in average session duration (52 seconds vs. a 28-day median of 125 seconds). A day that brings in double the usual new users at half the usual engagement time often means a page is pulling in visitors who don't find what they expected -- potentially a landing page with a weak content-to-intent match. August 9 shows the inverse pattern: lowest sessions of the second half (11), but the longest average session duration in the entire window (316 seconds, +159% vs. median). A small number of highly engaged visitors on a low-traffic day suggests a specific piece of content is connecting deeply with the right person, even if volume is still low.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit the /food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/ page for CTR opportunity.** It's already pulling 527 impressions in two days with a 1.3% CTR. That impression count suggests Google is indexing and testing it at meaningful scale quickly. The CTR is low, which usually points to a title tag or meta description that isn't matching searcher intent clearly enough. Before assuming the page needs more content work, check what queries are triggering it in GSC and compare the current snippet to what competitors show for those terms.

2. **Investigate the August 12 session-duration collapse.** Thirty-seven sessions on a day with 52-second average duration is a red flag. Identify which pages drove that volume -- if it was a landing page (the therapy or landing variants modified August 13 were likely already live), check whether the page resolves correctly on mobile, loads at acceptable speed, and delivers content that matches the traffic source. This is a conversion-quality issue, not just an analytics curiosity.

3. **Resolve the GSC data pipeline.** Zero impressions across every day of the window, while Organic Search is simultaneously the top traffic channel, is a data integrity problem. This needs to be confirmed and fixed before the next rollup -- decisions about which pages to prioritize, which queries to target, and whether rankings are improving are all downstream of reliable GSC data.

4. **Prioritize internal linking across the August 13 eating disorder and food addiction cluster.** Twelve pages touching related topics went live or were updated in a single day. Without deliberate internal links connecting them to each other and to the site's higher-authority pages, they'll compete against each other in search rather than reinforce each other. A focused internal-linking pass this week -- before Google has fully processed the batch -- is likely to improve how the cluster ranks as a group.

5. **Map the .edu referrers and identify a second outreach candidate.** Five .edu inbound links from 4 referring domains is a real foundation. Identify which institution(s) are linking and whether there is a natural follow-up relationship -- a second resource page pitch, a guest contribution, or a co-citation opportunity with another San Antonio health institution. This is the highest-leverage link-building play available right now given what's already working.
