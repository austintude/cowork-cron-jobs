# Weekly briefing -- 2026-08-22

### The window in one sentence

The week of August 14-20 was dominated by a single-day traffic event on August 16 that inflated the 7-day totals significantly -- strip that day out and the underlying baseline is actually running above its 28-day median on most days. The more important story is what happened after August 16: sessions on the 17th through 20th held meaningfully above normal, which suggests something real may be pulling new visitors in.

---

### Volume + composition

The 7-day window produced 689 sessions, 678 users, and 1,548 pageviews at an average of 98.4 sessions per day. Those numbers are heavily skewed by August 16, which alone accounted for 476 sessions and 971 pageviews -- roughly 69% of the window's total sessions in a single day. GSC impressions and clicks are at zero across the entire window, which is a data gap to address (more on that below).

Channel composition is the clearest signal here. Direct traffic drove 575 of 689 sessions, or about 83% -- far above BCC's expected baseline of ~60%. Organic Search contributed 87 sessions (~13%), well below the expected ~25%. Referral came in at 9 sessions, Organic Social at 10, Email at 3, AI Assistant at 3, and Organic Video at 2. The extreme Direct skew on August 16 almost certainly reflects an untagged send -- an email blast, a social post, or a text/SMS campaign that fired that day without UTM parameters. The sessions that followed on August 17 were also elevated (51 sessions, still well above the ~19 median), which is consistent with a day-after tail from a campaign send.

The organic picture is quieter than expected. With 537,539 GSC impressions attributed in the 30-day rollup, there is clearly search visibility being built -- but it is not converting into sessions at the rate you would expect. That disconnect is worth watching.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves comparison shows a -69.3% drop from the first half (527 sessions across August 14-16) to the second half (162 sessions across August 17-20). On the surface that looks like a sharp decline, but the August 16 spike makes this comparison misleading. The more honest read is that the second half, averaging roughly 40-44 sessions per day on August 19-20, is running 70-90% above the 28-day median of ~22-24 sessions. That is sustained elevation, not a collapse.

What this actually looks like: a traffic event fired mid-week, brought a large number of new visitors in (477 new users on August 16 alone against a median of 15), and a residual lift carried into the back half of the week. Session duration on August 16 crashed to 22 seconds -- consistent with a mass untagged traffic dump where many visitors bounced quickly. By August 17 and 18, session duration recovered to 179-211 seconds, above the 122-second median, suggesting the visitors who stuck around were genuinely engaged. The week is best described as event-driven, with a promising post-event baseline.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Identify and tag the August 16 traffic source.** 83% Direct in a single week almost always means an untagged campaign fired. Pull email send logs, text/SMS records, and any social posts from August 15-16 and match timing. If this was a deliberate send, retroactive UTM tagging on future sends will let you measure these events properly. If it was unintentional or you cannot find the source, that is a different problem worth investigating.

2. **Investigate the GSC zero-data gap.** The window shows zero impressions and zero clicks across all 7 days in GA4's GSC integration, while the 30-day rollup shows 537K impressions attributed. This suggests a data pipeline issue -- likely a GSC property connection or date-range offset in how impressions are being pulled into the daily view. Resolving this is a prerequisite for accurate trend analysis going forward.

3. **Prioritize getting the psych-eval BOFU pages (6530-6532) indexed.** They shipped to staging August 21. Confirm they are live on production, then submit them directly to GSC for indexing. BOFU pages targeting people actively researching bariatric psychological evaluations are high-intent -- every day they sit unindexed is lost ground.

4. **Audit the 5 EDU backlinks for cluster relevance and build on the strongest one.** Five EDU links is a real asset and the model worth replicating. Identify which institution is linking, what page they are linking to, and whether there is a relationship or outreach path to a second link from that same domain or a comparable one. EDU authority links tied to bariatric or health content are difficult to earn and worth protecting.

5. **Run a ranking position audit against the 537K-impression content.** With zero pages in top 10 but significant impression volume, there is likely a cluster of pages sitting in positions 11-30 that need targeted attention -- internal linking, title tag refinement, or structured-data additions -- to break into click-generating territory. Identifying the top 10 impression-earning pages by average position would tell you exactly where to concentrate effort next week.
