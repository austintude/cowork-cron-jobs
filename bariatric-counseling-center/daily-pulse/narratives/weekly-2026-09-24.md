# Weekly briefing -- 2026-09-24

### The window in one sentence

The seven days ending September 22 were volatile but finished strong -- sessions jumped 64% from the first half of the window to the second, driven almost entirely by a single high-traffic day on September 22 that coincided with two new page launches. GSC data is showing zero impressions across the full window, which is the most important unresolved question heading into next week.

### Volume + composition

The window produced 193 sessions, 174 users, and 607 pageviews across seven days, with a per-day average of 27.6 sessions and 24.9 users. GSC shows zero impressions and zero clicks for the entire window -- that is either a data pipeline issue or a signal that Google has not yet indexed the site's recent additions. It warrants immediate verification before drawing any organic search conclusions.

Channel mix came in at roughly 51% Direct (99 sessions), 35% Organic Search (68 sessions), 8% Email (15 sessions), 5% Referral (9 sessions), and 3% Unassigned (6 sessions). That is a notable deviation from BCC's baseline of ~60% Direct / ~25% Organic. Organic Search running 10 points above baseline suggests either improved rankings on existing pages or returning visitors coming through search rather than bookmarks -- a positive signal worth watching. Direct running below baseline is less concerning on its own, but if it stays low it could reflect reduced email list activity or a drop in returning visitors. The Email channel at 8% is worth noting too -- that is higher than the ~0-2% you would expect if no campaigns were running, which suggests either a campaign fired in this window or some traffic is being miscategorized from an untagged source.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is clearly accelerating. The first three days (September 16-18) produced 73 sessions and 265 pageviews. The final four days (September 19-22) produced 120 sessions and 342 pageviews -- a 64% session lift and a 29% pageview lift. Nearly all of that momentum came from September 22 alone, which recorded 44 sessions and 150 pageviews, both flagged as CRITICAL anomalies relative to the 28-day median.

That makes this a "spike acceleration" rather than a smooth upward trend. Days September 19 and 20 were actually soft -- pageviews on September 20 were down 54% versus the 28-day median, and September 20 also produced an unusual average session duration of under 10 seconds (down 91% from median), which suggests a crawl, a bot pass, or a broken session. The underlying week-over-week story is real, but it is being carried by one day. The coming week will show whether September 22 was a genuine inflection point or a one-day event.

### Launches + efficacy

Two substantive pages shipped on September 22: `/san-antonio-weight-loss-clinic/` targeting the keyword "san antonio weight loss clinic," and `/texas-food-addiction-search-trends/` targeting "texas food addiction search trends." Both are two days old as of this briefing -- no GSC impressions yet, which is normal for that window. The September 22 traffic spike (44 sessions, 150 pageviews) is circumstantially linked to these launches, but likely reflects Direct or internal preview traffic rather than organic discovery. Give both pages 10-14 days before drawing ranking conclusions.

Also notable from the September 22 sitemap activity: `/author/tmccord845/` picked up 1,084 GSC impressions and 18 clicks (1.7% CTR) since its sitemap touch -- generating 7 GA4 sessions. For an author archive page, that is a meaningful signal. It suggests Google is serving this page for some informational queries and people are clicking through. This is worth monitoring; author pages that accumulate impressions can sometimes be structured to capture more traffic with a brief content addition (bio, linked posts, expertise signals).

Three core service pages were modified on September 19 -- `/nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/`, `/bariatric-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/`, and `/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/`. None appear in the top-launches efficacy data with ranked positions or impressions, but modifications to core service pages typically take 2-4 weeks to reflect in GSC rankings. Flag these for re-evaluation in the October 3-7 rollup.

The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 1,034,522 GSC impressions and 5,355 clicks attributed across all tracked launches -- a site-wide CTR of roughly 0.5%, which is low and suggests either keyword difficulty (impressions at low positions) or a systemic snippet/title issue across the portfolio. No pages are currently ranking in the top 10. That is the most significant strategic gap in the data.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Verify the GSC data pipeline.** Seven consecutive days of zero impressions is almost certainly a tracking or authentication issue, not a real signal. Confirm the GSC property is connected, the service account has not expired, and the correct domain property is selected. This blocks all organic analysis until resolved.

2. **Monitor the September 22 new pages for indexing.** Submit `/san-antonio-weight-loss-clinic/` and `/texas-food-addiction-search-trends/` for indexing via GSC's URL Inspection tool if you have not already. "San antonio weight loss clinic" is a high-value local keyword -- the sooner Google processes the page, the sooner ranking data becomes available. Check back in 10-14 days.

3. **Investigate the September 20 session-quality anomaly.** A 9-second average session duration is not human browsing behavior. Pull the September 20 sessions in GA4, filter by session duration under 10 seconds, and check the source/medium and landing page. If it is bot traffic, filter it out. If it is real users bouncing immediately on a specific page, that page has a problem.

4. **Develop a plan for the `/author/tmccord845/` page.** It is generating impressions on its own, which is unusual for an author archive. Adding a short bio with relevant credential language and linking to 3-5 of that author's strongest posts could improve CTR from its current 1.7% and strengthen E-E-A-T signals across the pages attributed to that author.

5. **Audit the 30-day portfolio for title and meta-description quality.** A 0.5% site-wide CTR on over one million impressions suggests the site is appearing in search but not compelling clicks. Before producing more new pages, spend a week reviewing title tags and meta descriptions on the top 20 impression-generating pages in GSC. Small CTR improvements at scale will move sessions faster than new content right now.
