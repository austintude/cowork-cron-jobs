# Weekly briefing -- 2026-08-09

### The window in one sentence

The week of August 1-7 opened in a hole -- sessions on Aug 1-3 ran 40-50% below the 28-day median -- then recovered sharply in the second half, closing with two days at 27 sessions each and pageviews spiking to 162 on August 5. The net picture is a week that was worse than it looked on paper, saved by a mid-week surge that likely traces to the August 6 content push rather than any organic ranking movement.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 136 total sessions, 120 users, and 505 pageviews across seven days, averaging 19.4 sessions and 17.1 users per day. Those averages sit close to the 28-day median of ~20 sessions/day, which sounds reassuring -- but the average is misleading here because the week was two very different halves stitched together.

GSC impressions and clicks both show zero for the entire window, which is almost certainly a data pipeline issue rather than a real collapse in search visibility. The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 345,977 attributed impressions and 1,577 clicks, and individual page records (the homepage alone shows 113,304 impressions) confirm the site is indexed and ranking. Flag this with whoever manages the GSC integration before treating any organic numbers as reliable this week.

Channel composition flipped from BCC's baseline in a notable way. Organic Search led at 68 sessions (50% of total), well above the expected ~25% share. Direct came in at 46 sessions (34%), significantly below the typical ~60%. Referral contributed 17 sessions (12.5%), above its ~10% baseline. This inversion of Direct and Organic Search is worth watching -- it could mean Direct traffic is being correctly attributed now rather than lumped as unassigned, or it could reflect a genuine shift in how people are finding the site. The low Email count (1 session) and minimal Organic Social (4 sessions) suggest no newsletter or social push drove the week's traffic.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves data tells a clear story: the first three days (Aug 1-3) generated only 41 sessions and 109 pageviews. The final four days (Aug 4-7) produced 95 sessions and 396 pageviews -- a 131.7% session increase, half over half. That is a sharp acceleration, not a gradual one. August 5 in particular was a standout, with 162 pageviews against a 28-day median of ~56, and average session duration spiking to 408 seconds, more than triple the median. People weren't just arriving -- they were reading.

The concern is that August 6 dropped back hard (45 pageviews, session duration down 40% vs median), suggesting the Aug 4-5 spike was an event rather than a new baseline. August 7 recovered to 27 sessions and 79 pageviews, which is a healthy number. The honest read is that this week accelerated, but the acceleration was concentrated and volatile rather than steady. Whether it sustains into the current week is the open question.

### Launches + efficacy

August 6 was the major content push day for this window: four page modifications and nine post modifications shipped, plus several git commits for infrastructure work (the task board, August social cards, and daily pulse automation). The infrastructure commits are internal tooling and won't move traffic directly, but the social cards could support distribution if they're being actively used.

Of the four page modifications, two are doing real work already. The weight loss medications page (/understanding-weight-loss-medications-wegovy-ozempic-zepbound-and-mounjaro/) shows 30,914 total GSC impressions and 94 clicks -- the strongest individual page signal in the dataset. A 0.3% CTR on that volume is low, which suggests the title or meta description may not be pulling people through even when the page is surfacing. The homepage (/) is the volume anchor with 113,304 impressions and 680 clicks (0.6% CTR), generating 607 attributed sessions over its tracking window. The Bariatric Surgery Counseling page (/bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/) is a concern: 1,701 impressions and zero clicks. That page is visible in search but getting passed over entirely -- title/snippet work is warranted there. The Coordinate with Medical Providers page (/ourprogram/coordinate-with-medical-providers/) has 2,774 impressions and only 11 clicks (0.4% CTR); similar CTR problem, smaller scale.

No new backlinks were earned specifically in this window, but the domain profile stands at 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links total, with 5 of those being .edu links. That's a meaningful asset -- .edu links carry authority weight disproportionate to their count. No competitor links are pointing to the site, which is a neutral finding.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Address the CTR gap on the surgery counseling page before adding more content.** /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ has 1,701 impressions and zero clicks -- that's a snippet problem, not a ranking problem. Rewrite the title tag and meta description this week, then monitor CTR over the following 10 days. A page that ranks but doesn't get clicked is costing real opportunity.

2. **Run a CTR audit across the weight loss medications page and the medical providers page.** Both are generating solid impression volume with sub-0.5% CTR. Pull the exact search queries driving those impressions in GSC and check whether the current titles match what people are actually searching. Small snippet changes on high-impression pages can produce outsized click gains.

3. **Investigate the Direct-to-Organic channel flip before making channel mix assumptions.** Direct dropping from ~60% baseline to 34% while Organic Search jumped to 50% is unusual enough to warrant a verification pass -- check whether any UTM-tagged campaigns went dark this week, or whether a recent GA4 configuration change affected attribution. Understanding this will determine whether the shift is real or a measurement artifact.

4. **Resolve the GSC data pipeline gap.** Seven consecutive days of zero impressions/clicks in the aggregate feed, while individual page records show six-figure impression totals, points to a connection or query issue in the reporting layer. This needs to be confirmed and fixed -- if the GSC feed goes dark, the weekly and monthly trend data loses its most important organic signal.

5. **Develop a second .edu outreach target using the existing .edu link profile as proof of concept.** With 5 .edu links already in place, there's a credible pitch to make to similar institutions. Identify one or two San Antonio-area universities or community colleges with health, nutrition, or psychology programs and explore whether a resource link, guest contribution, or provider partnership mention is realistic. This is a strategic play that won't pay off this week but starts a pipeline.
