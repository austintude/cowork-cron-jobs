# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-05

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending August 3, sessions ticked up a modest 9.6% (first week vs second week), with Organic Search leading all channels at 49% of traffic -- a meaningful departure from BCC's typical Direct-heavy baseline. The window's headline story is a strong mid-window surge (July 29--31) followed by a sharp weekend pullback that softened what could have been a clearly positive trend.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 285 total sessions, 260 users, and 2,204 pageviews across 14 days. Per-day averages were 20.4 sessions and 18.6 users -- roughly in line with recent medians, nothing dramatic on its own. GSC impressions and clicks are showing zero across the entire window, which is a data collection or connection issue and not a real signal. Do not read that as organic visibility collapsing; pull GSC directly to verify.

Channel composition is the most notable story here. Organic Search drove 140 sessions (49%), which is nearly double BCC's expected ~25% baseline share. Direct came in at 97 sessions (34%), well below the typical ~60% share. Referral contributed 35 sessions (12%), slightly above baseline. Organic Social added 11, and AI Assistant contributed 2. This shift away from Direct toward Organic Search is a positive structural signal -- it suggests content is being found rather than the site being revisited by people who already know BCC. That said, when Direct drops this far below baseline, it's worth checking whether any email sends or social posts that typically drive Direct traffic were paused or delayed during this window.

Pageviews have an anomaly worth flagging separately: July 23 and July 24 produced 592 and 952 pageviews respectively against sessions of 17 and 28 on those days. That math implies roughly 35--55 pageviews per session, which is not organic browsing behavior. This looks like a crawl event, an internal testing session, or a monitoring tool. It inflated the first-half pageview total significantly (1,797 vs 407 in the second half) and should be filtered before any pageviews-per-session calculations are made for those days.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions grew 9.6% from the first half (136) to the second half (149), which reads as a slight positive. But the shape of that second half matters more than the aggregate. July 29 brought 23 sessions, July 30 spiked to 40 (105% above the 28-day median -- the window's single biggest day), and July 31 held at 26. Then the final three days of the window -- August 1, 2, and 3 -- dropped to 10, 12, and 19. That's a mid-window surge followed by a significant weekend/early-week pullback.

The July 30 spike coincided with new-user volume nearly doubling (34 new users, +119% vs median) and a YoY same-day pageview gain of +110.7%, which means this wasn't just noise. Something brought people in -- likely either an external link, a social share, or a piece of content getting picked up. The subsequent drop on August 1--2 is consistent with a weekend pattern rather than a structural problem, but the session duration data is worth watching: average session duration was elevated on July 26 (347 seconds, +171% vs median) and July 27, then collapsed to 47 seconds on July 29 and continued low through early August. That flip from deep engagement to shallow sessions suggests the character of traffic changed -- possibly from returning, intentional visitors to newer, less committed ones.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Investigate the GSC data gap.** The entire 14-day window shows zero impressions and zero clicks in the aggregated feed, while the 30-day rollup shows over 500K impressions. This is either a pipeline issue, a property mismatch, or a date-range misconfiguration. Until this is resolved, you're flying partially blind on organic performance. Confirm the GSC property connection and date range alignment before next week's rollup.

2. **Audit the July 30 traffic source.** That day produced 40 sessions (+105% vs median) and 34 new users (+119% vs median) with a year-over-year gain. Something specific drove it. Dig into GA4's session source/medium breakdown for July 30 to identify whether this came from a referral, a social share, or a Direct/untagged link. If it was a referral, that domain is worth a relationship.

3. **Prioritize internal linking into the two new emotional eating pages.** Both the "Emotional First Aid Kit" post and the "Emotional Eating vs. Disordered Eating" page are new, live, and in a topical cluster that appears to be attracting organic attention. Linking to them from existing, already-indexed pages in the eating-disorders and mental-health sections will help Google understand their context and pass authority faster than waiting for external links.

4. **Address the rankings gap in the 30-day rollup.** Zero pages in the top 10 despite 500K+ impressions is the central strategic problem. This suggests content is targeting terms where BCC is landing in positions 11--30 -- visible enough to generate impressions, not competitive enough to generate clicks. Spend time this week pulling the actual position distribution from GSC to identify which pages are closest to page one (positions 11--15) and treat those as the highest-leverage optimization targets.

5. **Evaluate the `.edu` backlink profile for replication opportunities.** Five `.edu` links across 4 referring domains is a strong start for a bariatric counseling center. Identify which institutions those links came from and whether there are parallel programs -- student health centers, nutrition departments, social work programs -- at other Texas universities where BCC's content would be a natural citation. One warm outreach per month to a relevant .edu contact is a low-effort, high-authority play.
