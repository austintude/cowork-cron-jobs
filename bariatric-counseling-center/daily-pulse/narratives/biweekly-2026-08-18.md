# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-18

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day window (Aug 3--16), sessions surged 353% from first half to second half, but nearly all of that movement is explained by a single anomalous day on Aug 16 -- 476 sessions in one day against a baseline of roughly 20. Strip that day out and the underlying trend is a modest, real improvement in the second half, not a breakthrough.

---

### Volume + composition

Total sessions across the window came to 774, with a daily average of 55.3 -- but that average is heavily distorted by Aug 16. On a typical day in this window, the site was pulling 18--29 sessions, users were running close to sessions (742 total, ~53/day), and pageviews totaled 1,974. GSC impressions and clicks are both reading zero across every day in the window, which is a data pipeline issue to flag -- it does not mean the site has zero organic visibility.

Channel mix was heavily skewed toward Direct at 579 sessions (74.8%), with Organic Search at 145 (18.7%) and Referral at 34 (4.4%). That Direct share is meaningfully higher than BCC's ~60% baseline, which suggests either untagged traffic from an email or social send, or that Aug 16's traffic spike (discussed below) came through a channel that wasn't properly tagged. Organic Search is slightly below the ~25% baseline but not alarmingly so. Referral at 4.4% is softer than the ~10% baseline, suggesting the site isn't pulling much link-driven traffic right now. Email and Organic Social combined account for fewer than 15 sessions over two weeks, which is worth noting if either channel is actively being used.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The headline number is a 352.9% increase from first half (140 sessions, Aug 3--9) to second half (634 sessions, Aug 10--16). That sounds like a major acceleration -- but it is almost entirely driven by Aug 16, which recorded 476 sessions and 971 pageviews against an avg session duration of just 22 seconds. That combination -- massive volume, near-zero dwell time -- is the signature of a bot crawl, a shared link that got picked up by an aggregator, or a social post that went briefly viral to people who bounced immediately. It does not represent meaningful audience engagement.

If you set Aug 16 aside, the second half (Aug 10--15) averaged about 26 sessions/day versus the first half's 20/day. That is a real, modest improvement -- roughly 30% -- consistent with the page modifications on Aug 13 beginning to generate some return traffic. That is the actual story of this window: a quiet, steady lift in the second half, not a breakthrough. The Aug 16 spike needs a source identified before it gets credited as progress.

---

### Launches + efficacy

The most substantive activity in this window happened on Aug 13, when a batch of page modifications shipped across nine URLs. The pages fall into two clear clusters: the binge-eating and food-relationship cluster (/stop-the-cycle-of-binge-eating-you-dont-have-to-face-this-alone/, /binge-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/, /food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/, /compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/) and the weight-loss therapy cluster (/professional-weight-loss-therapy/, /professional-weight-loss-therapy-2/, /professional-weight-loss-therapy-fb/, /expert-weight-loss-therapy/, /post-bariatric-surgery-support/). These are modifications, not new pages, so they are likely refinements to pages that already have some history with Google -- which means ranking movement, if it comes, should be faster than for fresh content.

The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 472,163 GSC impressions and 2,057 clicks attributed across the broader content portfolio, with 1,217 GA4 sessions. However, zero pages in the top-launches list carry a SERP position, and the ranking data shows nothing in the top 3 or top 10. That is a meaningful gap: the site is generating impressions but not converting them into rankings or click volume at scale. The Aug 13 modifications to the weight-loss therapy and binge-eating pages are the right kind of move to address this -- updating pages that already have crawl history is more likely to produce near-term ranking shifts than publishing new URLs. The binge-eating cluster pages in particular (/binge-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/ and /compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/) are targeting queries with real search intent and low competition from trauma-informed providers -- these deserve close monitoring over the next 14--21 days.

Everything else in the launches ledger is git-commit automation (daily pulse files, SEO snapshots) -- no new public pages shipped in this window. Backlinks currently sit at 4 referring domains, 12 total inbound links, and 5 .edu links. The .edu count is a genuine asset -- that is not easy to earn -- but 4 domains total is thin for a local healthcare provider trying to build authority.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Identify the Aug 16 traffic source before next week closes.** Check GA4 session source/medium for that day specifically. If it was an untagged email or social post, tag future sends so this volume gets attributed correctly. If it was bot traffic, filter it from reports going forward. Misreading a spike like this as organic growth leads to bad strategic decisions.

2. **Set up a GSC data check.** Every day in this window shows zero GSC impressions and clicks, which contradicts the 30-day rollup showing 472K impressions. This is a pipeline or property-connection issue. Accurate GSC data is foundational -- without it, you cannot tell whether the Aug 13 page modifications are moving rankings.

3. **Watch the Aug 13 binge-eating and food-relationship pages closely over the next 14 days.** These are the pages most likely to produce a measurable organic lift given their topic specificity and the trauma-informed angle that most competitors are not providing. If you are not already tracking /binge-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/ and /compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/ as priority URLs in GSC, add them now.

4. **Pursue a second or third referring domain, specifically through healthcare or .edu channels.** Four referring domains is the strategic constraint right now. One targeted outreach to a San Antonio-area hospital system, university health program, or NEDA-affiliated resource page -- the type that already links to similar providers -- is worth more than a month of new page production in terms of domain authority impact.

5. **Audit the duplicate and variant weight-loss therapy pages for consolidation signals.** The window shows four variants of essentially the same page (/professional-weight-loss-therapy/, /professional-weight-loss-therapy-2/, /professional-weight-loss-therapy-fb/, /expert-weight-loss-therapy/). If these are ad landing pages that were also modified for SEO, they may be competing with each other for the same queries. Worth investigating whether consolidating or canonicalizing them would concentrate ranking signals rather than splitting them.
