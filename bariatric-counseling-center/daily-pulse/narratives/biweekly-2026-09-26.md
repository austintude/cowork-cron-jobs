# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-26

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending September 24, the site logged 459 sessions at a 32.8/day average -- a solid baseline number, but the window is declining, with the second half running 22% below the first. The content pipeline is active and a handful of high-impression pages are showing real search visibility, but session volume and new user counts are softening heading into the final days.

---

### Volume + composition

The 14-day window produced 459 sessions, 417 users, and 1,394 pageviews, averaging 32.8 sessions and 29.8 users per day. GSC impressions and clicks show zero at the aggregate level for this window -- that's a data pipeline issue, not a signal that search visibility collapsed. The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 1.45 million attributed impressions and 7,340 clicks, which confirms organic search is very much alive. The zero values here likely reflect a GSC lag or a property-level reporting gap worth verifying in Search Console directly.

Channel composition came in at roughly 46% Direct (212 sessions), 39% Organic Search (177), 9% Email (43), 4% Referral (17), and a small Unassigned slice (19). That's a meaningful deviation from BCC's baseline of 60/25/10/5. Direct is running below baseline and Organic Search is running well above it -- which is good news for content investment. Email contributed 43 sessions, suggesting at least one send landed during this window. The elevated Organic share likely reflects content that has matured in rankings over recent months rather than any single new launch.

The anomaly log is dense -- 51 flagged events across 14 days, split between critical and major. The pattern worth noting is a cluster of high session-duration spikes (September 16, 18, 19, 23, 24) alongside low session-count days. Long time-on-site with low traffic volume typically means return visitors or highly engaged people reading deeply, not a broad audience issue. September 20 is the outlier that deserves attention: average session duration crashed 91% to roughly 9 seconds while pageviews fell 54% -- suggesting either a bot flush, a technical hiccup, or a day with almost no genuine engaged visitors.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is declining. The first seven days (September 11--17) produced 258 sessions at a 36.9/day average. The second seven days (September 18--24) produced 201 sessions at a 28.7/day average -- a 22.1% drop. Pageviews follow the same direction: 754 in the first half, 640 in the second. This is not a catastrophic slide, but it is a real one, and it is worth naming honestly.

September 11 opened with 64 sessions -- the window's peak -- which may reflect a carry-forward from something that fired just before the window opened (an email send, a social post, or an indexed page hitting rankings). From there, volume stepped down gradually through the middle of the window, briefly recovered on September 22 (44 sessions, flagged as a significant up-anomaly with 60.9% more new users than median), then fell again to 20 sessions on September 24. The September 22 spike is interesting -- it arrived late in the second half and did not sustain, but 37 new users in a single day is a real signal worth tracking. If a specific content piece or referral source drove that day, identifying it should be a priority.

---

### Launches + efficacy

Six content assets shipped inside or immediately adjacent to this window (September 23--25), covering a mix of service pages, program sub-pages, blog posts, and one staff profile. The two most significant by raw GSC impression volume are both just 1--2 days old at the time of this report, so performance judgments need to stay provisional.

The standout number is the hydration post (/healthy-living-2/why-hydration-is-essential-for-weight-loss-and-management/) -- modified September 24, it carries 340,096 cumulative GSC impressions and 1,693 clicks at a 0.5% CTR. That impression volume is substantial and indicates the page is already surfacing for a broad query set. The CTR of 0.5% is low relative to what a top-3 or top-5 ranking would typically yield, which suggests it is showing up in positions where clicks are rare (likely 6--20 range). A title or meta description refresh aimed at improving click-through is the lever here -- the impressions are already there. The gastroparesis post (/diet-and-nutrition/gut-health/gastroparesis-aka-stomach-paralysis/) shows 19,923 impressions and only 49 clicks (0.2% CTR), which is the same pattern: visible but not compelling enough to click.

The nutritional counseling service page (/nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/) is the most surprising number in the set -- 3,854 GSC impressions at one day old with zero clicks. That is a lot of early search exposure for a newly modified page, and the zero-click rate points directly at the title or snippet not matching what people actually want when they run those queries. The weight loss counselor page (/what-is-a-weight-loss-counselor/) landed at 1,072 impressions and 7 GA4 sessions in one day, with a 1.1% CTR -- better, and worth watching. The bariatric therapy page (/bariatric-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/) and the /start/ page are too early to read meaningfully. No new backlinks were earned inside this window per the ledger, though the site holds 4 referring domains and 5 edu links total, which is a thin but credible backlink foundation.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Investigate the nutritional counseling and hydration pages for CTR.** Both pages are generating real impression volume (3,854 and 340,096 respectively) but converting almost none of it to clicks. Pull their top queries in GSC, compare the title tags and meta descriptions to what the SERP actually shows for those queries, and test a revision. This is a higher-leverage play than publishing new content right now.

2. **Find out what drove September 22.** That day produced 44 sessions and 37 new users -- both well above median -- and it landed at the bottom of a low-traffic stretch. Identifying whether an email, a social post, a referral, or a ranked page drove that spike will tell you whether there is a repeatable channel to press on.

3. **Audit the GSC data pipeline for this window.** Aggregate impressions and clicks show zero for September 11--24 despite the 30-day rollup showing 1.45 million attributed impressions. That is almost certainly a reporting gap, not a real drop. Confirming the GSC connection is pulling correctly will restore confidence in the daily numbers.

4. **Build toward a second backlink from an institutional source.** The site's backlink profile is thin -- 4 referring domains, 12 total links. The 5 edu links are a real asset. Identifying which edu domain(s) are already linking and whether there are related departments, clinics, or programs at those institutions that could add a second link is a strategic play worth one focused outreach effort this week.

5. **Consider a content pause to let recent launches index and settle.** Six assets modified in two days (September 23--25) is a meaningful output burst. Letting those pages accumulate impressions and click data for 10--14 days before modifying them again will give cleaner signal on what is working. If production capacity is available, redirect it toward internal linking between the service pages and the high-impression blog content rather than new pages.
