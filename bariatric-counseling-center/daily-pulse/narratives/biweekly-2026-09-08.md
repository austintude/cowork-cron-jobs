# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-08

### The window in one sentence

The Aug 24 -- Sep 6 window delivered 320 sessions at a 22.9/day average, with Organic Search outpacing Direct for the first time in recent memory -- but a near-complete site outage on Aug 31 and Sep 1 collapsed the second half's numbers, making the -31.6% half-over-half decline a tracking artifact rather than a meaningful trend signal. The underlying picture, once those two days are set aside, is roughly flat with a channel composition shift worth paying attention to.

---

### Volume + composition

Over 14 days, the site generated 320 sessions, 299 users, and 902 pageviews. Per-day averages were 22.9 sessions and 21.4 users. GSC impressions and clicks both came back at zero for the entire window, which is almost certainly a data connection issue rather than a real search collapse -- that needs to be verified and resolved before any SEO conclusions can be drawn from this period.

The channel split is the most interesting story here. Organic Search led with 158 sessions (49.4%), followed by Direct at 133 (41.6%), with Organic Social at 11, Referral at 10, and Email at 8 accounting for the remainder. That is a meaningful departure from BCC's baseline expectation of ~60% Direct and ~25% Organic Search. Organic Search nearly doubling its share -- and leading the window -- suggests either a real ranking improvement starting to pull traffic or a reduction in Direct traffic (possibly lower email send volume or fewer direct patient touchpoints). Both explanations are worth investigating. Referral at 3% and Email at 2.5% are both running below baseline, which may simply reflect a quiet period for outbound communication.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves comparison shows a -31.6% drop from the first half (190 sessions, Aug 24-30) to the second half (130 sessions, Aug 31 -- Sep 6). On paper, that is a concerning decline. In practice, Aug 31 and Sep 1 each registered exactly 1 session and 2 pageviews -- numbers that match a site that was either down, blocked by a bot filter, or had tracking fully disabled. The git log from Sep 1 explicitly records a blocked blog redate drip run due to "staging12 logged out, 4th consecutive," which hints at a broader authentication or environment issue around that time. Those two days account for roughly 50 sessions of lost volume versus a normal two-day run.

Stripping Aug 31 and Sep 1, the remaining five days of the second half (Sep 2-6) averaged about 25.6 sessions per day, which is consistent with the first half's ~27.1/day average. The window is effectively flat -- not accelerating, not declining. That is honest and useful to know: the content and channel work is holding volume steady, but there is no growth signal yet.

---

### Launches + efficacy

All launches captured in this window are git commits -- daily pulse logs, SEO pulse logs, and a marketing pulse entry. None carry a public-facing URL, none show GSC impressions, and none show GA4 sessions attributed. These are operational and tracking commits, not content launches. That means there is no new page or post that shipped within this 14-day window for which we can measure early search traction.

The 30-day efficacy rollup tells a more substantive story. Across the prior 30 days, 7 pages were launched and 32 were modified, generating 317,261 GSC impressions and 1,463 clicks attributed to launched content -- with only 1 page currently ranking in the top 10 and none in the top 3. That top-10 page is the only measurable organic foothold right now, and the gap between 317K impressions and 1,463 clicks (a ~0.46% CTR) strongly suggests the site is appearing in search for relevant queries but not earning the click. That is a title tag and meta description problem more than a ranking problem.

No new backlinks were earned within this specific window. The overall backlink profile sits at 4 referring domains and 12 total inbound links, including 5 from .edu sources. That .edu count is a real asset -- edu links carry authority weight that most small health practices don't have. Zero competitor links is expected and fine.

---

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Resolve the GSC data gap before anything else.** Fourteen days of zero impressions is not plausible for a site showing 317K attributed impressions in the 30-day rollup. The connection between Google Search Console and the reporting pipeline is broken. Until this is fixed, you cannot make confident SEO decisions from the trend briefing.

2. **Investigate the Aug 31 -- Sep 1 site/tracking failure.** The git log flagged a staging12 authentication issue on Sep 1 (fourth consecutive blocked run). Determine whether this was isolated to the drip automation or whether it reflects a broader environment problem that took the site or GA4 tracking offline for 48 hours. If site traffic genuinely collapsed on those days, that needs a root-cause answer.

3. **Audit title tags and meta descriptions on the top-10 ranking page and any pages with high impressions in the 30-day window.** A 0.46% CTR across 317K impressions is low. The pages are visible -- they are just not earning clicks. Rewriting titles to be more specific, benefit-forward, and search-intent matched is likely to move CTR more than additional content production right now.

4. **Investigate why Organic Search is outpacing Direct.** This channel flip may be good news (rankings improving) or a signal that Direct touchpoints -- email, social, patient referrals -- have gone quiet. Pull the last 60 days of channel data and look for when the crossover started. If it correlates with a specific content launch, that page deserves more attention and internal linking.

5. **Develop a plan to build on the .edu backlink base.** Five edu links for a San Antonio bariatric practice is genuinely above average. Identify which institutions those links came from, what content earned them, and whether a direct outreach -- to a health science program, dietetics department, or student health office -- could produce a sixth. One more high-authority edu link this quarter would meaningfully strengthen the domain profile.
