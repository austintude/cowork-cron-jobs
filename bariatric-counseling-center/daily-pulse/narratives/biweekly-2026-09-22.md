# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-22

### The window in one sentence

The September 7--20 window shows a site that had a strong first week (peaking at 64 sessions on Sept 11) and then stepped down noticeably in the second half, with sessions declining 30% between the two halves. The most significant content event -- a bulk update to the eating disorder cluster across 14 pages on September 18 -- is too fresh to evaluate, but it lands in exactly the right direction.

---

### Volume + composition

Over the 14-day window, the site recorded 462 sessions, 424 users, and 1,308 pageviews. Per-day averages came in at 33 sessions and 30.3 users -- numbers that sit close to baseline, but the daily spread matters more than the average here. Three days (Sept 8, 9, and 11) drove a disproportionate share of first-half volume, while the second half settled into a quieter 21--39 session range.

Channel breakdown: Direct led at 210 sessions (45%), Organic Search followed at 162 (35%), Email at 69 (15%), and Referral at just 13 (3%). That mix is notably different from BCC's expected baseline of ~60% Direct / ~25% Organic. Organic Search running 10 points above baseline is the most interesting signal here -- it suggests content is earning search traffic at a higher-than-usual rate, even with GSC data currently showing zero (more on that below). Direct running 15 points below baseline, and Email contributing 15%, strongly suggests one or more email sends touched this window -- possibly newsletter or drip sequences hitting the Sept 8 and 11 spikes.

GSC impressions and clicks show zero across the entire window. This is a data pipeline issue, not a ranking collapse -- the 30-day efficacy rollup reports over 1 million impressions and 5,280 clicks attributed across the site's content. Do not read the zeroes as a sign the site disappeared from search; read them as a reporting gap that needs to be resolved before GSC trend analysis is meaningful.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves split tells a clear story: 272 sessions in the first half (Sept 7--13) versus 190 in the second half (Sept 14--20), a 30.1% decline. This is a declining window, and it should be named plainly. The first half was lifted by what looks like email-driven traffic on Sept 8 and 11 -- those days produced 62 and 64 sessions respectively, well above median. Without that lift, the first half would look much closer to the second.

The second half is not a collapse -- sessions held in the 21--39 range, which is within normal operating territory. What's notable is that session duration anomalies in the second half ran strongly positive: Sept 14, 15, 16, 18, and 19 all showed average session duration 50--260% above median. People who visited in the second half stayed considerably longer, even though fewer of them came. That pattern -- lower volume, higher engagement -- sometimes appears when content quality improves and the audience finding the site is more intentional. It's worth watching over the next two weeks to see if that engagement lifts CTR or return visits.

### Launches + efficacy

The biggest content event in this window was September 18: a coordinated update to 14 pages in the eating disorder cluster, plus a modification to the homepage and the /start/ page. The cluster pages include high-intent targets like /eating-disorder-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/, /eating-disorder-therapist-in-san-antonio-tx/, /eating-disorder-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/, /telehealth-eating-disorder-treatment-in-texas/, and /eating-disorder-group-therapy-san-antonio-tx/. These were all modified four days ago as of this briefing date. That is not enough time to draw conclusions -- Google typically takes 2--4 weeks to re-evaluate updated pages, and GSC data visibility is lagged further on top of that. What matters right now is that the updates shipped, they're comprehensive, and the cluster is positioned to compete.

The homepage modification (/), also from Sept 18, is the one launch with real data behind it: 340,084 GSC impressions and 1,049 GA4 sessions attributed over its full history. A 0.5% CTR on that impression volume is low -- the page is visible but not compelling enough in search results to earn clicks at a competitive rate. That CTR number is worth a dedicated look at title tags and meta descriptions.

The /video-lightbox-test/ page published Sept 18 has 2 sessions and no GSC impressions. This appears to be a development or UX test page rather than a content asset. It should either be noindexed or removed from public-facing production so it doesn't accumulate crawl budget or dilute the site's topical focus.

The launches_ledger shows 96 git commits in the 30-day window alongside 2 page publishes and 19 page modifications. That's a high commit-to-publish ratio -- lots of tooling and operational work relative to content output. That's not a criticism, but it's context: the site's SEO gains in the coming weeks will come from the September 18 cluster update, not from the commit volume.

No new backlinks were recorded in this window. Existing backlink profile: 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, 5 of which are .edu links. The .edu count is a genuine asset and suggests institutional credibility. Zero new links in a 14-day window is not alarming, but link acquisition should be on the medium-term agenda.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit the GSC pipeline this week.** Zero impressions across 14 days is a data gap, not a ranking reality -- but until it's resolved, trend analysis is flying blind on the organic side. Confirm whether the GSC API connection is authenticated and pulling correctly, and identify which date the data cutoff actually reflects.

2. **Set a two-week check-in on the September 18 eating disorder cluster.** These 14 pages need a structured review around October 2--7 to see whether impressions moved, positions shifted, or any pages picked up clicks. Create a simple tracking sheet now so the comparison is ready when the data arrives.

3. **Investigate the homepage CTR problem.** 340,000 impressions at 0.5% CTR means the page is appearing in search but not earning clicks. Pull the actual title tag and meta description live in search results and compare against two or three direct competitors. A single well-tested title tag change on a page with that impression volume could meaningfully shift organic sessions.

4. **Noindex or delete /video-lightbox-test/.** It's a test page, it's publicly indexed, and it has no content value. Leaving it live creates minor crawl budget and topical dilution risk. This is a five-minute fix worth making before the next content push.

5. **Identify the email send that drove the Sept 8 and 11 spikes, and replicate it.** Email drove roughly 15% of sessions this window -- well above its typical share. If there was a newsletter or drip email that went out around Sept 7--10, that send should be documented and the content or subject line studied. Email is clearly moving people to the site; understanding what triggered those two high-traffic days is worth more than another new page right now.
