# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-25

### The window in one sentence

The 14-day window (Aug 10--23) is dominated by a single massive traffic event on Aug 16 that inflated the period totals, and once that spike is stripped away, the underlying baseline is declining -- sessions dropped 65.6% from first half to second half. The operative question right now is not "are we growing?" but "what is driving the end-of-window dropoff, and is it a weekend artifact or a real trend?"

---

### Volume + composition

Over the 14 days, the site recorded 852 sessions, 828 users, and 2,046 pageviews, averaging about 61 sessions and 59 users per day. GSC data is returning zeroes across the entire window, which is a data feed problem -- not a ranking collapse -- and needs to be verified before any SEO conclusions can be drawn from this period.

Channel composition broke heavily toward Direct at 652 sessions (76.5%), well above the expected 60% baseline. Organic Search came in at 148 sessions (17.4%), which is modestly above the ~25% baseline in absolute-share terms but suggests organic volume is lower than normal in raw numbers. Referral (27 sessions), Organic Social (14), Email (4), AI Assistant (3), and Organic Video (2) round out the mix. The AI Assistant channel is worth noting as a small but real and growing source -- worth watching over future windows even though it is negligible right now.

The outsized Direct share is almost certainly explained by the Aug 16 spike. That single day produced 476 sessions and 971 pageviews -- more than half the window's total session volume in one day. A Direct traffic event of that magnitude almost always points to an untagged email blast, an SMS campaign, or a social post that was not UTM-tagged before it went out. If a send or post went out on Aug 15--16, that is the likely source. If nothing was intentionally sent, it is worth checking for any bot or scraper activity in GA4's raw session data, though the session duration data for Aug 17 (average 211 seconds, up 75%) suggests real human engagement carried over the day after -- which argues more toward a genuine untagged promotion than spam.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves data tells a clear and concerning story: first half (Aug 10--16) totaled 634 sessions; second half (Aug 17--23) totaled 218 sessions -- a 65.6% drop. The problem is that Aug 16 is doing nearly all the work in the first half. Remove that one day and the first half comes in around 158 sessions, which makes the second half look roughly comparable in baseline terms, averaging about 31 sessions per day versus a pre-spike average of roughly 26 per day. So the underlying trend is not catastrophic, but it is not accelerating either.

What is genuinely concerning is the hard tail-off at the very end of the window. Aug 22 and Aug 23 came in at 15 and 12 sessions respectively -- both flagged as critical or major anomalies against the 28-day median. Both are weekend days, which typically soften volume, but the YoY comparisons for Aug 22 (-73.7% sessions vs same day last year) and Aug 23 (only -7.7% -- a more normal Saturday) are inconsistent enough to suggest Aug 22 was a real down day, not just a weekend pattern. The session duration data from Aug 21--23 is also flagging as critically low (Aug 21 was -78.8% against median), suggesting the visitors who did arrive were not engaging deeply. This combination -- fewer people arriving, spending less time -- warrants attention heading into the next week.

### Launches + efficacy

The only substantive page-level work that shipped inside this window was DRB-1146, logged on Aug 21: three psych-eval BOFU pages (post IDs 6530, 6531, 6532) rebuilt and published on staging, then pushed to prod. These are too new to have GSC impressions or ranked positions yet, and given the GSC data feed is returning zeroes across the board this period, there is no way to evaluate their early traction from this briefing alone. Watch for first impressions in the next 7--14 days. Because these are BOFU pages tied to the psychological evaluation requirement for bariatric surgery, they carry high conversion value -- if they rank even modestly for queries like "psych eval for weight loss surgery San Antonio," a single patient inquiry justifies the production cost many times over.

The remainder of the window's logged activity is daily pulse commits, weekly SEO snapshots, and marketing pulse files -- operational cadence work, not net-new indexable content. The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 3 pages launched, 583,525 GSC impressions attributed across the broader portfolio, and 2,483 GSC clicks -- but zero rankings in the top 10. That last figure is the most important number in the efficacy data and the most concerning. With over half a million impressions in 30 days, the site clearly has search visibility, but if nothing is converting that visibility into top-10 positions, the existing content is likely living in positions 11--30 -- visible enough to rack up impressions, not prominent enough to pull consistent clicks. This is a snippet and position optimization problem, not a content volume problem.

No new backlinks were earned inside this specific 14-day window based on the ledger data. The domain currently shows 4 referring domains and 12 total inbound links, with a notable 5 edu-domain links. Those edu links are high-authority and worth building on.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Identify the Aug 16 traffic source before the week is out.** Pull GA4's session source/medium detail for Aug 16 and filter for Direct. If a large share shows landing pages that point to a specific service page or post, that is your clue. If an email or social post went out untagged, retroactively document it and add UTM parameters to future sends. This matters because if a promotion drove 400+ sessions in one day, repeating it -- tagged -- could give you a reliable traffic lever.

2. **Prioritize GSC data feed diagnosis over new content production.** Zero impressions and zero clicks across 14 full days is not a ranking problem -- it is a reporting problem. Until the GSC feed is restored, you are flying blind on what is actually ranking and what is not. This is upstream of all SEO decision-making for the site.

3. **Run a position-band audit on the 30-day GSC data (once the feed is restored).** The gap between 583K impressions and zero top-10 rankings over 30 days strongly suggests a cluster of pages sitting in positions 11--20. Pull a position filter for 11--20 in GSC, sort by impressions, and identify the top 5--10 pages. Those are your highest-leverage optimization targets -- existing content that is almost ranking, where a title tag refinement or a targeted internal link could move the needle without starting from scratch.

4. **Set up tracking for the psych-eval BOFU cluster (posts 6530--6532) as a named content group in GA4.** These pages need their own lens so you can see engagement separate from the broader site baseline. If they are getting organic traffic but low time-on-page, that points to a content depth issue. If they are getting impressions but low CTR, that points to a title/meta description issue. You cannot diagnose either without the tracking in place first.

5. **Explore a second outreach to an edu-domain partner.** BCC already has 5 edu links, which is genuinely strong for a regional clinical practice. One well-placed outreach to a UTSA, UT Health San Antonio, or Texas A&M health program contact -- referencing an existing relationship or resource page -- could add a sixth. Edu links in the bariatric/surgical counseling space carry outsized authority and are hard to replicate. One new edu referring domain likely has more long-term SEO value than a month of new page production at the current volume.
