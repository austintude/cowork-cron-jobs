# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-23

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day window (Sept 8--21), BCC averaged 33.9 sessions per day across 474 total sessions, but traffic fell sharply in the second half -- sessions dropped 34% week over week -- driven by a pullback across nearly all channels after an Email-fueled spike in the first half. The good news is that session duration is trending up even as raw volume falls, suggesting the people who are showing up are more engaged than usual.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 474 sessions, 435 users, and 1,352 pageviews at a daily average of 33.9 sessions and 31.1 users. Those numbers sit modestly above the 28-day median, but the average is being propped up by a strong first half. GSC impressions and clicks show zero across all 14 days, which almost certainly reflects a data connection or property configuration issue rather than an actual absence of organic visibility -- the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 1,033,946 attributed GSC impressions and 5,332 clicks, so the pipeline exists and is pulling data elsewhere. That discrepancy needs to be diagnosed before this briefing can say anything meaningful about organic search momentum.

Channel composition this window: Direct led with 212 sessions (45%), Organic Search came in at 169 (36%), and Email delivered 71 (15%). That is a notable departure from BCC's baseline of ~60% Direct / ~25% Organic / ~10% Referral. Organic Search punched above its expected share, which is a quiet positive -- it suggests the content catalog is earning clicks from Google even without a clearly ranked breakout page. Email at 15% is the channel story of the first half: the Sept 8--12 spike (62 and 64-session days) almost certainly traces to an email send. Referral (13 sessions, ~3%) and Organic Social (6 sessions, ~1%) remained well below baseline, which is normal for BCC but worth noting as an underutilized channel.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

This window is declining, and the numbers are clear about it. The first half (Sept 8--14) produced 286 sessions. The second half (Sept 15--21) produced 188 -- a 34.3% drop. Pageviews followed the same arc: 757 in the first half, 595 in the second. By the final four days of the window (Sept 18--21), daily sessions were flat in the low-to-mid 20s, which is below BCC's baseline median. This is not a catastrophic signal on its own -- a post-email-blast normalization pattern looks exactly like this -- but it does mean there is no organic floor building yet to absorb the traffic when campaigns go quiet. The one genuinely encouraging counter-signal: average session duration was elevated throughout the second half, including a 260% spike on Sept 18 and a 208% spike on Sept 16. Fewer people came, but the ones who did spent significantly more time on the site. That suggests content quality is not the problem.

That gap -- strong impression volume, no top-10 positions -- is the core strategic tension BCC is working through right now. ### What this means + what to do next

1. **Investigate the GSC zero-data issue in the 14-day aggregate.** The window shows 0 impressions for all 14 days, but the 30-day rollup shows over a million. Something is misconfigured in how this window pulls GSC data -- either a date-range offset, property mismatch, or filter error. Until this is resolved, the trend briefing cannot track organic search momentum accurately, which is the most important growth lever BCC has right now.

2. **Build internal links into `/san-antonio-weight-loss-clinic/` from the existing core service pages modified Sept 19.** Those four pages (`bariatric-therapy`, `bariatric-surgery-counseling`, `nutritional-counseling`, `bariatric-counseling-near-me`) were just refreshed and are already in Google's recrawl queue. Adding contextual links to the new clinic page now, while those pages are being re-indexed, compounds the signal efficiently.

3. **Assess whether BCC has an organic floor or is session-dependent on email sends.** The 34% drop in the second half likely reflects post-email normalization. Next week is a good window to look at what Organic Search alone delivers on days with no email activity -- that baseline number tells you how much SEO is actually doing on its own.

4. **Noindex or remove `/video-lightbox-test/` if it has not been addressed already.** A publicly crawlable test page is a minor but unnecessary liability. This is a five-minute WordPress task, not a strategic one -- but it is worth flagging in case it slipped through.

5. **Plan a structured push to earn a fifth referring domain before end of Q3.** With 4 referring domains total and no competitor links, BCC's backlink profile is thin. The .edu links are valuable, but Google wants to see domain diversity. A San Antonio health system, a local non-profit, or a professional association in the bariatric or behavioral health space would be the right targets -- and a well-sourced page like `/texas-food-addiction-search-trends/` is exactly the kind of content that gives an external site a reason to link.
