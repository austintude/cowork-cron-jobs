### Volume + composition

The 14-day window produced 456 sessions, 419 users, and 1,329 pageviews across 14 days. The daily session average of 32.6 is close to BCC's historical median, meaning this window was not a breakthrough -- it was roughly normal in aggregate. GSC impressions and clicks both reported as zero across the entire window, which is a data connection issue rather than a true performance reading. Do not interpret that as organic collapse; it means the GSC pull failed or the property is misconfigured for this date range and needs a manual check.

Channel composition tilted noticeably toward Direct (216 sessions, 47%) and Organic Search (170 sessions, 37%). BCC's baseline expects roughly 60% Direct and 25% Organic, so this window shows Organic punching above its weight while Direct came in below baseline. That Organic lift is worth paying attention to -- it suggests either an improving keyword footprint or that some of the service pages modified in the window are drawing search traffic. Email contributed 47 sessions (10%), which is close to baseline and suggests a send or two during the window without anything exceptional. Referral was thin at 15 sessions (3%), and Organic Social remains minimal at 6 sessions.

The anomaly count (25 critical, 13 major, 15 minor) is high for a two-week stretch, but most of the CRITICAL flags were session-duration spikes concentrated September 14--19, not volume spikes. A session duration anomaly of 208%--260% above median on low-volume days (September 16 was only 21 sessions) almost always means a small number of highly engaged visitors -- possibly staff, a referral from a trusted source, or someone doing extended research -- rather than a broad behavioral shift. Worth monitoring but not alarming.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is declining. The first seven days (September 9--15) produced 263 sessions; the second seven (September 16--22) produced 193 sessions -- a drop of 26.6%. Pageviews followed the same shape: 722 in the first half versus 607 in the second. The opening days were elevated by a clear cluster of strong performance September 11--12, when sessions hit 64 and 45 respectively and new-user counts ran 50--67% above median. After that cluster, the site settled into a quieter plateau through September 16--21, ranging from 21 to 27 sessions per day.

The good news is that September 22 recovered sharply -- 44 sessions and 150 pageviews, both above median -- on the same day the two new pages published. It is too early to attribute that spike to the new pages specifically (it may be coincidental or tied to an email or social post), but the rebound at least means the window did not close on a declining note. If the September 22 session level holds or builds in the current week, the declining trend label will need to be revisited.
