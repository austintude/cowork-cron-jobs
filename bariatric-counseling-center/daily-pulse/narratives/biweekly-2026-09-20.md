The notable departure from BCC's expected baseline (~60% Direct / ~25% Organic) is that Direct is running lower than usual while Organic Search is punching above its typical share. That is generally good news -- it suggests pages are generating search-driven traffic on their own, not just pulling in existing-audience visits. Email at 14% is a meaningful contributor this window and worth tracking -- if a specific send drove that, confirm it was properly tagged so it doesn't blur Direct in future windows.

Referral at 3% is below the expected ~10% baseline. That's worth noting but not alarming given that referral traffic can be lumpy and seasonal.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions in the first half (Sep 5--11) totaled 240; the second half (Sep 12--18) came in at 219 -- a -8.8% decline. On a raw numbers basis, the window is slightly declining. That said, the framing matters: the first half was inflated by a sharp spike on Sep 8 (62 sessions) and Sep 11 (64 sessions, +117% above median), following a near-collapse on Sep 10 (7 sessions, -76% below median). That Sep 10 drop and Sep 11 recovery pattern is almost certainly a single-day anomaly -- a tracking blip, a brief site issue, or a delayed batch session count -- rather than a real behavioral trend. Strip that distortion out and the two halves look closer to flat.

Pageviews tell a slightly different story: first half produced 600, second half produced 707, a +18% gain. People who did land in the second half looked at more pages per visit, and session duration anomalies in Sep 14--18 (avg session duration running 89--260% above median) reinforce that the audience arriving later in the window was more engaged, not less. The headline is: session volume is flat to slightly soft, but engagement quality appears to be improving. That combination suggests the content is doing its job when people find it -- the gap to close is top-of-funnel reach.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Establish a weekly ranking check for the Sep 18 eating disorder cluster.** Nineteen pages were modified in a single day. That's a meaningful content event, but without GSC data flowing cleanly, you won't be able to tell which pages responded positively and which didn't. Set a calendar reminder to pull GSC performance for these specific URLs seven and fourteen days from Sep 18 and compare position movement. /adhd-and-emotional-eating-in-san-antonio-tx/ is the first one to watch.

2. **Diagnose the GSC data gap before the next rollup.** The window shows zero impressions across all 14 days in the aggregate feed, while the 30-day efficacy rollup shows over one million attributed impressions. That discrepancy suggests the pipeline pulling daily GSC data is either misconfigured, hitting a quota limit, or pulling from the wrong property. Resolving this is a prerequisite for accurate trend analysis going forward -- right now you are flying without search visibility data.

3. **Investigate the Sep 10 collapse.** A single-day drop to 7 sessions (-76% below median) followed by a bounce to 64 sessions the next day is the kind of pattern that usually points to a tracking tag firing issue, a brief outage, or a caching problem rather than real user behavior. Check GA4's real-time logs and server uptime records for Sep 10 to confirm it was a data artifact and not a recurring fragility in the tracking setup.

4. **Audit the Email channel tagging.** Email drove 66 sessions (14% of the window) but runs well above its expected share relative to BCC's baseline. If email sends are not consistently UTM-tagged, some of that traffic may be leaking into Direct and inflating that channel on other windows -- or suppressing it here. A consistent tagging standard for email sends will make the channel data trustworthy over time.

5. **Investigate the ranking concentration question.** With 1M+ impressions in 30 days but only 1 page in the top 3 and 1 in the top 10, it's worth pulling a GSC query report to understand what's actually driving those impressions. If the bulk of visibility is coming from branded queries (people searching "Bariatric Counseling Center" by name), then the content program's organic reach into new audiences may be lower than the raw impression number suggests. Understanding that split will clarify whether the eating disorder cluster is genuinely expanding reach or just adding pages to an already branded-dominant profile.
