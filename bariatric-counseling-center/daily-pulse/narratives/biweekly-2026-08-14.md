# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-14

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14-day window (July 30 -- August 12), site traffic held essentially flat at 311 sessions and 22.2 sessions per day, with no meaningful acceleration or decline between the two halves. The most important development happened just outside the window: a significant batch of 10+ page modifications shipped on August 13, including the eating disorder and food addiction cluster, and those pages are too new to evaluate yet -- but early GSC signals on two of them are worth watching.

---

### Volume + composition

The window produced 311 sessions, 281 users, and 1,037 pageviews across 14 days. Per-day averages work out to 22.2 sessions and 20.1 users. GSC impressions and clicks both show zero in the window-level rollup, which is a data pipeline issue to flag -- the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 393,317 impressions and 1,802 clicks attributed to the broader content set, so Google Search Console is clearly not broken at the property level. The zero values in the daily GSC series suggest either a reporting lag or a connection gap in the data pull for this specific date range.

Channel composition is the most notable story here. Organic Search drove 157 sessions (50.5%), Direct drove 110 sessions (35.4%), Referral contributed 33 sessions (10.6%), and Organic Social and Email together added 10 more. That organic figure is meaningfully above BCC's baseline expectation of roughly 25% organic -- it is running double that. Direct, meanwhile, is well below the expected ~60% baseline. This inversion suggests the site is becoming more dependent on search discovery than on returning or directly-navigating visitors, which is a healthy long-term signal but raises a question: is the Direct number low because branded awareness is underdeveloped, or because untagged email and social campaigns are being misattributed elsewhere? Worth investigating before drawing conclusions.

Referral at 10.6% is roughly in line with baseline expectations. The 4 referring domains and 12 total inbound links in the backlinks ledger -- including 5 from .edu sources -- represent a real asset. Five edu backlinks is not nothing for a counseling practice of this size.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves comparison tells a flat story: 157 sessions in the first half (July 30 -- August 5), 154 sessions in the second half (August 6 -- August 12), a -1.9% change. That is statistically indistinguishable from flat. Pageviews followed the same pattern -- 567 in the first half, 470 in the second -- a modest pullback that likely reflects the high-pageview outlier on August 5 (162 pageviews, +189% above the 28-day median) skewing the first half upward.

What the halves don't show is the volatility underneath the averages. Session counts ranged from 10 to 40 within the window, and several days triggered CRITICAL anomaly flags in both directions. August 5 was a legitimate high-engagement day; August 9 had sessions down 42% from median alongside an average session duration of 316 seconds (more than 5 minutes), which suggests a small number of people spending deep time on the site rather than a volume event. August 12 closed the window strong -- 37 sessions, 36 users, and new users up 113% versus the 28-day median -- but session duration collapsed to 52 seconds on the same day, which points toward a new-visitor discovery event (possibly an untagged social or email share) where people arrived and left quickly. This pattern is not alarming, but it does suggest BCC is occasionally reaching new audiences through channels that aren't properly tagged.

### Launches + efficacy

All confirmed launches in this window shipped on August 13 -- technically one day after the window closed -- so there is no in-window performance data to evaluate on any of them. That said, they are the strategic story for the coming period, so it is worth mapping what shipped.

Ten page modifications went out on August 13 covering the eating disorder and food addiction cluster: `/stop-the-cycle-of-binge-eating-you-dont-have-to-face-this-alone/`, `/binge-eating-disorder-treatment-san-antonio/`, `/binge-eating-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/`, `/food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/`, `/compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/`, `/night-eating-syndrome-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/`, and `/food-addiction-treatment-in-texas/`. Three blog posts were also modified: the emotional eating and coping skills post, the pre-bariatric surgery binge eating screening post, and the hydration post. Four sitemap touches went out alongside, which should prompt Googlebot to recrawl category pages for eating disorders, self-improvement, and weight loss counseling. Two of the cluster pages already have early GSC impression data after just one day: `/food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/` logged 486 impressions and `/compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/` logged 920 impressions -- with CTRs of 1.2% and 0.7% respectively. Those are promising first-day numbers. They suggest Google is indexing the pages quickly and serving them for relevant queries, even if click volume is not yet meaningful.

The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 0 pages ranking in the top 3 and 0 in the top 10, which is the honest state of the site's search authority right now. The 393,317 impressions and 1,802 clicks over 30 days mean the site is visible but converting impressions to clicks at less than 0.5% -- a strong signal that title tags, meta descriptions, or SERP snippet content need attention across the board before more page production makes sense.

No new backlinks are confirmed in this window beyond the aggregate totals (4 referring domains, 12 links, 5 edu). The edu backlinks in particular deserve attention as a strategic asset worth building on.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit title tags and meta descriptions on the August 13 cluster before the pages fully index.** The 30-day rollup shows a sitewide click-through rate problem: hundreds of thousands of impressions, sub-0.5% CTR. The two cluster pages with early impressions are already showing the same pattern at 0.7--1.2%. Improving the snippet copy on `/compulsive-overeating-counseling-san-antonio-tx/` and `/food-addiction-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/` now -- while Google is still in early indexing -- is likely to yield faster CTR improvement than waiting.

2. **Build internal links from the blog cluster to the new service pages.** The modified posts (emotional eating, pre-bariatric screening, hydration) and the category pages that received sitemap touches are natural linking surfaces into the new eating disorder service pages. This is a one-time internal linking pass that could meaningfully lift the cluster's authority signals before rankings stabilize.

3. **Investigate the Direct traffic drop and the August 12 short-session spike as a pair.** Direct is running at 35% versus a 60% baseline, and the August 12 anomaly (new users up 113%, session duration down 58%) suggests people are finding the site through an untagged channel. Identify what happened on August 12 -- a social post, an email, a mention somewhere -- and make sure it is properly tagged going forward. Understanding where that audience is coming from is more valuable than the session count itself.

4. **Pursue a second .edu backlink using the existing five as a proof-of-model.** Five edu backlinks for a counseling practice in San Antonio is a credibility signal worth extending. Research which institution or program those five came from, identify peer programs or health departments at UTSA, UT Health San Antonio, or Trinity University, and make a direct outreach pitch using the existing relationship as a reference point.

5. **Hold off on net-new page production for the next week and instead request GSC indexing for the August 13 batch.** With 10+ pages modified in a single day and category sitemaps touched, the priority now is making sure Google has fully crawled and indexed everything before adding more pages to the queue. Use Search Console's URL inspection tool to manually request indexing on any cluster pages still showing zero impressions after 7 days.
