# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-08-11

### The window in one sentence

The July 27 -- August 9 window held steady at roughly 21 sessions per day with Organic Search taking an unusually dominant share of traffic, but sessions declined 9.7% from the first half to the second half, suggesting the site is coasting rather than building momentum. The big content push -- 13 page modifications shipped on August 10, one day after this window closes -- has not yet had time to move the needle, so the next briefing will be the real test.

---

### Volume + composition

Over 14 days, the site logged 295 sessions, 265 users, and 939 pageviews, averaging 21.1 sessions and 18.9 users per day. GSC impressions and clicks came back as zero for this window, which almost certainly reflects a data pipeline gap rather than actual search silence -- the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 216,827 attributed impressions and 1,021 clicks, so Google is clearly surfacing these pages. That disconnect needs to be resolved before GSC trend data can be used meaningfully in these briefings.

Channel composition is notably inverted from BCC's expected baseline. Organic Search drove 159 of 295 sessions (54%), well above the usual ~25% share, while Direct came in at 96 sessions (33%) -- roughly half its typical 60% weight. Referral contributed 31 sessions (11%), close to baseline, and Organic Social and Email were negligible. A sustained organic-heavy composition like this is generally a positive structural signal: it suggests people are finding the site through search intent rather than being pushed there by staff or existing contacts. However, the suppressed Direct number could also indicate that regular visitors and staff-driven traffic were simply quieter during this stretch, which would inflate Organic's share without representing true search growth.

The July 30 spike (40 sessions, 118 pageviews) and the August 4-5 cluster (27 and 23 sessions, 110 and 162 pageviews) stand out as the two meaningful traffic events. August 5 in particular registered a critical anomaly -- pageviews 189% above the 28-day median and average session duration 241% above median -- which suggests a small number of highly engaged visitors explored the site in depth that day rather than a broad traffic event. That pattern is worth watching: deep engagement from a few visitors often signals someone in a referral pipeline doing research before contacting the practice.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

Sessions declined 9.7% from the first half (155 sessions, July 27 -- August 2) to the second half (140 sessions, August 3 -- 9). That is a mild softening, not a collapse, but the direction is worth naming honestly: the window is declining, not accelerating.

The more interesting story is pageviews, which moved the opposite direction -- 422 in the first half versus 517 in the second half, a 22% increase. Sessions went down while pageviews went up. That combination usually means the visitors who did arrive in the second half spent more time and looked at more pages per visit. The session duration anomalies on August 4-5 and August 9 (316 seconds average, 159% above median) support this read. The site is not drawing more people, but the people it is drawing are engaging more deeply. That is a meaningful distinction: it suggests content quality is landing, but discovery -- the top of the funnel -- needs attention.

---

### Launches + efficacy

All 13 page modifications captured in this window shipped on August 10, one day after the measurement window closes. That means none of their traffic impact is reflected in the 295-session total above. What we can assess are their early GSC signals, since impression data accrues quickly even before click behavior stabilizes.

The pages showing the most pre-click visibility after just one day: /nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ (3,854 impressions, 0 clicks), /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ (1,775 impressions, 0 clicks), /bariatric-therapist-in-san-antonio-tx/ (1,479 impressions, 18 clicks, 1.2% CTR), /testimonials/ (1,420 impressions, 0 clicks), and /bariatric-surgery-psychological-evaluation-in-san-antonio-tx/ (1,343 impressions, 6 clicks, 0.4% CTR). The nutritional counseling page in particular is already surfacing nearly 4,000 times with zero clicks -- that is a strong signal that Google has position on this page but the title or meta description is not converting impressions into visits. At that impression volume, even getting CTR to 2% would mean ~75 additional clicks per day.

Three pages are pulling actual sessions already: /bariatric-therapist-in-san-antonio-tx/ (5 sessions), /testimonials/ (6 sessions), and /bariatric-surgery-psychological-evaluation-in-san-antonio-tx/ (3 sessions). Given they are only 1 day old, those numbers are reasonable early signals. The pages that need attention are /bariatric-therapy-in-san-antonio-tx/ (8 impressions total -- effectively invisible so far), /virtual-emotional-eating-therapy-in-texas/ (58 impressions), and /holistic-approach-to-weight-loss/ (125 impressions, 0 clicks). Low impressions at day one can simply mean indexing lag, but they are worth monitoring through the week.

The 30-day efficacy rollup also notes 5 .edu backlinks in the site's total profile (4 referring domains, 12 inbound links total). No new backlinks were earned specifically within this 14-day window based on available data, but the .edu link presence is a meaningful authority signal that suggests prior outreach or press work has generated real results. That pattern is worth repeating.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit title tags and meta descriptions on the high-impression, zero-click pages.** /nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ (3,854 impressions, 0 clicks) and /bariatric-surgery-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ (1,775 impressions, 0 clicks) are already visible in search results but are not earning clicks. Spend next week rewriting their title tags and descriptions with clearer value propositions and local specificity. A 1-2% CTR improvement on those pages alone would likely move total organic sessions meaningfully.

2. **Investigate the GSC data gap in this pipeline.** The 14-day window shows zero impressions and zero clicks across all days despite the 30-day rollup showing 216,827 attributed impressions. Before drawing any trend conclusions about organic growth or decline, this needs a diagnosis -- whether it is a property connection issue, date-range mismatch, or something else in the data pull.

3. **Build internal links from high-traffic pages to the August 10 cluster.** The new bariatric therapy, nutritional counseling, and psychological evaluation pages need referral pathways from wherever current sessions are landing. If most organic traffic is entering through a handful of top pages, those pages should link directly into the new content so visitors encounter it naturally.

4. **Identify and pursue a second .edu or healthcare-institution backlink.** The existing .edu link presence (5 links across 4 referring domains) is the site's most credible external authority signal. The pattern is working. Research which institution those links came from and treat that as a model for a targeted outreach pitch to one additional San Antonio-area academic or hospital partner this month.

5. **Investigate the top-of-funnel gap suggested by the sessions-down, pageviews-up pattern.** The second half of this window had fewer visitors but higher engagement per visitor. That is a discovery problem, not a content quality problem. Next week is a good time to look at whether any awareness-level content -- blog posts, FAQ pages, condition-specific entry points -- is missing or underlinked, and to prioritize one piece that could draw new visitors rather than deepening engagement with people already on the site.
