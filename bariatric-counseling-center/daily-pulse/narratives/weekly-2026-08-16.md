Organic Search led the week at 75 sessions (47%), followed by Direct at 64 (40%), Referral at 16 (10%), and a small scatter of Organic Social, Unassigned, and Email making up the remainder. BCC's baseline is roughly 60% Direct / 25% Organic Search, so seeing Organic Search nearly double its typical share -- and overtake Direct -- is the most meaningful compositional signal this week. That said, GSC impressions and clicks are reporting as zero across every day in the window. That's a data gap, not a zero-traffic reality. Until GSC data is confirmed healthy, we can't attribute the Organic Search lift to specific queries or pages, which limits how actionable this signal is right now.

The Referral channel at 10% is roughly in line with baseline. The 16 referral sessions are worth watching -- if those trace back to the .edu backlinks in the current totals (5 edu links across 4 referring domains), that's a healthy signal worth building on.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is clearly accelerating. The first half (August 8--10, three days) produced 55 sessions and 184 pageviews. The second half (August 11--14, four days) produced 105 sessions and 301 pageviews. Even accounting for the extra day in the second half, the per-day session average jumps from 18.3 to 26.3 -- a meaningful step up. The halves data shows a 90.9% trend increase in sessions, which is strong directional movement.

The most notable driver of that acceleration was August 12, which logged 37 sessions, 36 users, 32 new users, and 102 pageviews -- all well above median, with new users running at +113% versus the 28-day baseline. The caveat is that average session duration on August 12 dropped 58% below median, suggesting the influx of new users may have been lower-intent traffic (quick bounces, ad clicks, or a social share landing on a single page). August 13 and 14 held above median without that duration dip, which is a healthier pattern. The week's trajectory going into August 15 is positive, but the August 12 spike needs a source identified before it can be called a win.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Diagnose the GSC data gap before drawing channel conclusions.** The Organic Search channel overtaking Direct is potentially significant -- it could mean rankings are improving across the eating-disorder or bariatric cluster -- but with GSC impressions reporting zero for the entire week, you can't confirm that. Next week, verify that the GSC property is correctly connected and pulling data. If it's a property mismatch or filter issue, fixing it should be the top priority, because every strategic call about content efficacy depends on it.

2. **Set a 14-day index check for the August 13 eating-disorder cluster.** The six locally targeted eating-disorder and food-addiction pages are the most strategically valuable content in this window. By August 27, they should be showing at least some GSC impressions if indexed. If they're still dark, submit them manually via Google Search Console URL inspection and check for crawl errors or canonical issues.

3. **Investigate the August 12 traffic spike by source.** Thirty-seven sessions, 32 new users, and 102 pageviews in a single day -- well above median -- followed by a session duration that collapsed 58% suggests a burst of low-engagement traffic. This likely came from an untagged social post, a paid ad, or an email without UTM parameters. Identifying the source will either confirm an organic win (good) or reveal untagged campaigns that are inflating your Organic Search and Direct numbers (needs fixing).

4. **Address the top-10 ranking gap as a strategic priority over the next month.** The 30-day rollup shows strong impression volume but zero pages in the top 10. That's a signal that content is visible but not yet competitive enough on the queries that matter most. Next week, pull the top 20 queries by impression volume from GSC (once data is confirmed clean), identify which pages are sitting at positions 11--20, and prioritize those for content depth and internal linking -- they're the closest to driving real traffic.

5. **Build on the .edu backlink base.** Five edu links across 4 referring domains is a stronger-than-average backlink foundation for a local counseling practice. Identify which institutions those links come from, then look for adjacent departments, resource pages, or faculty with related content (dietetics programs, social work programs, counseling psychology departments) that could generate a second link from the same domain or a new high-authority domain. One warm outreach email to an existing partner is more efficient than cold pitching new sources.
