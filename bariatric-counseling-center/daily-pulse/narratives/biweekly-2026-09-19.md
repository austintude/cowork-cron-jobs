# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-19

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending September 17, sessions accelerated 25% from the first week to the second (206 vs. 258), driven by a cluster of above-baseline days in the second half -- suggesting the site has genuine momentum heading into the wave of updates that shipped on September 18. GSC data is absent for this window, which is the single most important data gap to close before drawing conclusions about organic growth.

### Volume + composition

The window produced 464 sessions, 432 users, and 1,295 pageviews across 14 days, averaging 33.1 sessions and 30.9 users per day. Those per-day numbers sit modestly above the 28-day medians flagged in the anomaly data (median ~29-30 sessions), which means the window is performing at or slightly ahead of the site's established baseline -- not a breakout period, but not a regression either. GSC impressions and clicks are zeroed out across all 14 days, which is almost certainly a data pipeline issue rather than a genuine signal drop; the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 1,007,559 attributed GSC impressions, so organic visibility clearly exists. Getting GSC reporting restored is urgent before the next rollup.

Channel composition came in at roughly: Direct 212 sessions (46%), Organic Search 163 (35%), Email 69 (15%), Referral 11 (2%), Organic Social 7 (2%), Unassigned 7 (2%). Compared to BCC's expected baseline of ~60% Direct / ~25% Organic / ~10% Referral, two things stand out. Direct is running notably lower than baseline -- 46% vs. expected 60% -- which either means a previously untagged source is now being properly attributed, or direct audience behavior has genuinely softened. Organic Search is running higher than expected at 35% vs. the ~25% baseline, which is the more interesting signal and suggests pages are pulling more search traffic than they historically have. Email at 15% is well above the typical ~5% "other" bucket, indicating at least one campaign or newsletter send happened during this window that drove a meaningful traffic pulse -- worth confirming against any sends from the week of September 8-12.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is clearly accelerating. The first half (September 4-10) produced 206 sessions and 541 pageviews. The second half (September 11-17) produced 258 sessions and 754 pageviews -- a 25.2% session lift and a 39% pageview lift. That pageview-to-session ratio growing faster than sessions themselves suggests people in the second half were browsing more pages per visit, which is a quality signal, not just a volume signal.

The anomaly data tells the same story directionally, though with some noise. September 10 was a genuine outlier low -- only 7 sessions, down 76% from the median -- and it skews the first-half total downward. Even accounting for that, the second-half baseline is meaningfully higher. Session duration anomalies are a recurring flag in the second half (September 14, 15, and 16 all show average session duration 89-209% above median), which suggests a subset of visitors are spending real time with content -- likely the eating disorder and emotional eating pages, given the page types that have been updated.

### Launches + efficacy

All of the launches captured in the ledger shipped on September 18 -- one day after this window closes -- so none of them have measurable impact on the September 4-17 data. That said, the September 18 wave is significant enough to call out clearly: 17 page modifications and one new page publication went live in a single day. The modifications span the core eating disorder cluster (/eating-disorder-treatment-in-san-antonio-tx/, /eating-disorder-therapist-in-san-antonio-tx/, /eating-disorder-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/, /therapy-for-eating-disorders-in-san-antonio-tx/, /eating-disorder-group-therapy-san-antonio-tx/, /eating-disorder-iop-in-san-antonio-tx/) plus adjacent pages including emotional eating, ADHD and eating, telehealth treatment, the clinician referral page, and the home page. That is a coordinated cluster update, not a scatter-shot publishing day.

Of the top launches tracked, two show early GSC signals despite being only one day old. /adhd-and-emotional-eating-in-san-antonio-tx/ has 211 impressions and a 2.8% CTR with 6 clicks and 3 GA4 sessions -- that CTR is solid for a page this young and suggests the title and snippet are working. /counseling-for-anxiety-and-weight-in-san-antonio-tx/ has 101 impressions but zero clicks, which is a snippet or title issue worth looking at now before it settles into a ranking position with a weak CTR. The home page modification shows 334,214 cumulative GSC impressions and 1,026 GA4 sessions, but those numbers reflect the page's full history -- no meaningful day-one signal is readable there yet.

The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 1,007,559 GSC impressions, 5,186 clicks, and 3,153 GA4 sessions attributed across all tracked launches -- with only 1 page ranking in the top 3 and 1 in the top 10. That gap between impression volume and ranking concentration suggests BCC has broad visibility at positions 11-30 or lower on a large number of queries, but has not yet converted that visibility into top-of-page presence at scale. That is the strategic gap the September 18 cluster updates are likely targeting. Backlinks stand at 4 referring domains, 12 total inbound links, and 5 edu links -- a small but meaningful foundation, with edu links being the highest-authority signal in that set.
