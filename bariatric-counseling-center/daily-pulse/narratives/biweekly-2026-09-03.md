# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-03

### The window in one sentence

Over the 14 days ending September 1, the site ran at a functional baseline of roughly 24 sessions per day through August 30, then went nearly dark on August 31 and September 1 -- a pattern that looks like a tracking or publishing infrastructure problem, not an organic traffic drop. The more useful story this window is that Direct and Organic Search are running neck-and-neck at exactly 148 sessions each, which is a meaningful deviation from the expected channel mix.

---

### Volume + composition

The 14-day window produced 333 sessions, 315 users, and 950 pageviews across a clean 14-day run -- but those totals are artificially suppressed by the near-zero readings on August 31 (1 session, 2 pageviews) and September 1 (1 session, 2 pageviews). Strip those two days out and you get roughly 331 sessions across 12 active days, or about 27.6 sessions per day -- closer to baseline than the 23.8 average suggests. GSC impressions and clicks came in at zero across all 14 days, which is almost certainly a data pipeline issue and not a sign the site fell out of search results entirely; the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 677,020 attributed impressions and 2,917 clicks, so the GSC feed is not flowing into this window's reporting correctly.

Channel composition is the most interesting signal here. Direct and Organic Search tied at 148 sessions each -- 44.4% each -- against BCC's expected baseline of roughly 60% Direct and 25% Organic. That means Organic Search is running nearly double its usual share. Referral contributed 18 sessions (5.4%), Organic Social 14 (4.2%), Email 3, and AI Assistant 1. The elevated Organic Search share suggests either a ranking improvement has been pulling in new visitors, or some portion of what would normally tag as Direct has properly attributed to Organic. Either interpretation is good news. The low Email number (3 sessions) is worth noting -- if email sends happened during this window, they likely aren't tagged correctly and are folding into Direct or Unassigned.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is technically declining: the first half (August 19-25) produced 200 sessions, the second half (August 26 -- September 1) produced 133 -- a -33.5% drop. However, this number is misleading in isolation. The August 22-23 weekend dip and the August 31/September 1 near-zero days are dragging both halves in different ways. The first half was boosted by strong August 19-20 days (41 and 44 sessions respectively), while the second half was dragged down by what looks like a complete data collection or site access failure on the final two days of the window.

If you remove August 31 and September 1 from the second half, the remaining five days (August 26-30) averaged about 26.2 sessions per day -- roughly in line with the first half's underlying baseline once the weekend dip is factored out. The honest read: traffic was largely stable through the middle of this window, the "decline" is an artifact of the end-of-window disruption, and the session duration anomalies (August 25 at +1,370% vs. median, August 26 at -69.6%) suggest at least one unusually engaged session followed immediately by very shallow ones -- worth keeping an eye on but not a pattern yet.

---

### Launches + efficacy

Everything captured in the launches ledger for this window is operational infrastructure -- daily pulse commits, SEO snapshot commits, and a manifest correction -- rather than published content pages. The one commit that stands out is the August 27 tag stack replacement (commit f87f38b, "DRB-1149/1214: replace the whole tag stack with one Google tag"). This is almost certainly the cause of the August 31/September 1 tracking collapse. A full tag stack replacement is a high-risk operation, and the near-total disappearance of sessions and pageviews two to three days after it shipped is a timing correlation that needs to be ruled out before anything else. The staging12 login block noted in the September 1 commit ("4th consecutive") adds another layer -- it's possible the blog redate drip is stuck, which could be suppressing publish activity that would otherwise generate sessions.

On the 30-day efficacy side: 8 pages launched, 677,020 GSC impressions attributed, 2,917 clicks attributed, 1,427 GA4 sessions attributed, and 1 page ranking in the top 10 (none in the top 3 yet). The gap between 677K impressions and only 2,917 clicks points to a CTR problem -- pages are getting seen in search results but not being clicked, which typically means title tags or meta descriptions aren't compelling enough, or the pages are ranking for queries where BCC's result is showing below the fold. No new backlinks were earned during this window; the current profile stands at 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, and 5 edu links -- a thin but credible foundation.

---

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Diagnose the August 27 tag stack migration before anything else this week.** The correlation between the Google tag consolidation commit and the August 31/September 1 near-zero readings is too tight to ignore. Pull raw server logs for those two days and compare against GA4. If the tag is misfiring, every subsequent metric -- sessions, channel attribution, GSC connection -- is unreliable until it's fixed.

2. **Resolve the staging12 login block for the blog redate drip.** Four consecutive blocked runs means content that was meant to publish hasn't published. Audit what's queued, confirm whether any of those posts are now stuck in draft, and either fix the credential issue or manually publish what's backed up. Stalled content is a silent drag on the schedule.

3. **Investigate the CTR gap in the 30-day efficacy data.** 677K impressions producing only 2,917 clicks is a 0.43% CTR -- well below a healthy content site's 2-5% range. Pull GSC's top-impression queries for the pages in that rollup and audit their title tags and meta descriptions. The pages are ranking; they're just not getting chosen. This is a rewrite opportunity, not a new content opportunity.

4. **Dig into why Organic Search is matching Direct this window.** That's a meaningful deviation from BCC's typical channel mix. Identify which pages or queries drove the Organic lift -- if a specific content cluster is responsible, that's the cluster to expand and internally link more aggressively this quarter.

5. **Establish a backlink outreach target for this month.** Four referring domains is the full current profile. Even one high-authority addition would be meaningful. Look at who is already linking (particularly the edu links) and map out two or three adjacent institutions -- health programs, hospital networks, patient advocacy organizations in San Antonio -- where a genuine relationship or content partnership is realistic.
