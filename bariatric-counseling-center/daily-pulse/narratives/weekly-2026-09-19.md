# Weekly briefing -- 2026-09-19

### The window in one sentence

Over the seven days ending September 17, BCC averaged 36.9 sessions per day against a 28-day median of roughly 30 -- a modest but real lift -- driven by a strong open on September 11 that faded across the back half of the week. The window closed softer than it opened, which is worth watching, though session quality (time on site) stayed unusually high throughout.

---

### Volume + composition

Total sessions for the window came in at 258 across 7 days, with 238 unique users and 754 pageviews. That works out to 36.9 sessions per day and 107.7 pageviews per day. GSC impressions and clicks show zero for this window -- that is almost certainly a data pipeline issue rather than a true organic search blackout, since the 30-day efficacy rollup shows over 1 million impressions attributed to tracked launches. Treat GSC figures in this window as unreliable until the feed is confirmed healthy.

Channel composition tells an interesting story. Direct led at 123 sessions (47.7%), which is below BCC's 60% Direct baseline -- that is actually a good sign, not a bad one. Organic Search contributed 95 sessions (36.8%), well above the expected ~25%, and Email drove 28 sessions (10.9%), which likely represents a send or newsletter that hit early in the window. Referral was minimal at 6 sessions, and Organic Social was nearly flat at 5. The elevated Organic Search share relative to baseline suggests either a ranking improvement on an existing page or a content asset that moved into a clickable position -- this is worth tracing in GSC once the feed is restored.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves data shows a clear step-down: the first three days (September 11--13) produced 142 sessions, while the final four days (September 14--17) produced only 116 -- a -18.3% decline in sessions even with one more day in the second group. In real terms, the window opened hot and cooled steadily. September 11 alone accounted for 64 sessions, more than double the daily median, with anomaly flags confirming it was genuinely elevated across sessions, users, new users, and session duration. By September 16 and 17, sessions had dropped to 21 and 27 respectively, both below baseline.

The one counterpoint worth noting: pageviews actually held up better in the second half (398 vs 356 in the first half), and session duration stayed critically elevated through September 16 (peaking at 278 seconds average, more than triple the 28-day median of 90). That combination -- fewer people arriving, but those who do staying much longer -- suggests the audience quality is strong even when volume softens. This is not a crisis pattern. It looks more like the September 11 spike was event-driven (probably the Email send), and the underlying organic floor is stable or slightly growing.

### Launches + efficacy

All confirmed launches in this window shipped on September 18 -- technically one day after the measurement window closes (September 11--17) -- so none of the 17 page modifications or the one new page publish (``/video-lightbox-test/``) have measurable session data attributable to this trend window yet. The September 18 batch is large: the homepage (``/``), the ``/start/`` intake page, and 15 eating disorder and behavioral health pages were all modified in a single day. That is a significant content push and its impact will show in next week's rollup.

Among pages with pre-existing GSC history that are now modified, the most notable early signal is ``/adhd-and-emotional-eating-in-san-antonio-tx/``, which already shows 211 GSC impressions and a 2.8% CTR with 6 clicks on just one day of post-modification data. That click-through rate is notably strong for a page this early -- it suggests the title and snippet are connecting. ``/counseling-for-anxiety-and-weight-in-san-antonio-tx/`` shows 101 impressions but zero clicks, which is the opposite signal: Google is surfacing it but nobody is choosing it, suggesting the title or meta description may not be compelling enough to earn the click.

The 30-day efficacy rollup provides the broader context: 1,007,559 GSC impressions and 5,186 clicks attributed across tracked launches, with one page ranking in the top 3 and one in the top 10. Those numbers represent real search presence. The backlink profile -- 4 referring domains, 12 inbound links, 5 of which are from .edu domains -- is small but healthy in composition. Edu links carry authority. No new backlinks appear to have been earned specifically during this 7-day window.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit the GSC data feed before next week's rollup.** Seven consecutive days of zero impressions in the aggregate is not credible given the 30-day rollup showing over a million impressions. Identify whether this is a property ID mismatch, a date-range offset, or a connector failure. Without GSC data, ranking and click trend analysis is flying blind.

2. **Monitor the September 18 batch closely over the next 10--14 days.** Seventeen pages modified in a single day is a meaningful signal to Google's crawl budget. Watch for crawl errors, indexing delays, or ranking shifts on the affected URLs -- particularly the eating disorder cluster pages that already had established impressions before modification.

3. **Investigate the CTR gap on ``/counseling-for-anxiety-and-weight-in-san-antonio-tx/``.** 101 impressions with zero clicks one day after modification suggests the title tag or meta description is not resonating with search intent. Pull the query report for that URL in GSC, identify what people are actually searching when they see it, and revise the snippet accordingly.

4. **Trace the source of the September 11 spike.** The combination of doubled sessions, doubled new users, and a 89% jump in session duration on a single day points to a specific trigger -- most likely the Email channel (which showed 28 sessions for the full week, and many of those likely hit on the 11th). Confirm what sent that day and whether it can be replicated or sequenced.

5. **Build a second .edu backlink target list.** With 5 edu links already in the profile, there is a demonstrated pattern of institutional interest or citation. Identify which .edu domains are linking, understand the context, and find comparable programs -- counseling departments, nutrition programs, social work schools at Texas universities -- that could be pitched for a resource link or clinical referral mention.
