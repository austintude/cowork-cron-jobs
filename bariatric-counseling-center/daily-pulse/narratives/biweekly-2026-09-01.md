# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-09-01

### The window in one sentence

The Aug 17--30 window shows 408 total sessions at a 29-session daily average, with traffic essentially split between Direct and Organic Search -- but the window is decelerating, sessions fell 12.8% from the first half to the second, and GSC data is completely absent, which is the most pressing structural issue this period.

---

### Volume + composition

Over the 14-day window, the site generated 408 sessions from 388 users across 1,138 pageviews, at a daily average of 29.1 sessions and 27.7 users. Those are modest but not alarming numbers on their own. The more notable absence is GSC data: zero impressions, zero clicks across every single day in the window. That is not a traffic story -- that is a data pipeline story. Either GSC integration broke, the property lost verification, or there is a sampling/export issue. Nothing in this briefing about organic search health can be stated with confidence until that gets resolved.

Channel composition is the most interesting story in this window. Direct (188 sessions, 46%) and Organic Search (176 sessions, 43%) are running nearly even -- which is a significant shift from BCC's ~60% Direct / ~25% Organic baseline. If that Organic Search share is real, it suggests meaningful ranking traction. Organic Social and Referral each contributed 19 sessions (about 5% each), Email was minimal at 4, and there was a single AI Assistant session -- a small but worth-noting signal that BCC content may be surfacing in AI-generated answers. The near-parity between Direct and Organic warrants validation once GSC data is restored; the volume pattern is plausible but the zero-impression data makes it impossible to confirm what organic queries are driving it.

---

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The window is declining. First-half sessions totaled 218 (avg 31.1/day); second-half came in at 190 (avg 27.1/day), a 12.8% drop. Pageviews followed the same shape -- 589 in the first half versus 549 in the second. This is not a cliff, but it is a clear downward drift rather than growth.

The first half was dominated by a strong Aug 20 (44 sessions, 140 pageviews, +83% and +106% above the 28-day medians), followed by an immediate two-day crash on Aug 22--23 that hit well below baseline. That spike-and-drop pattern in the first half inflates the first-half totals somewhat -- the underlying trajectory in the second half actually looks more stable and predictable, settling into a 21--32 session range day over day. The practical read is that the window does not suggest sustained growth momentum right now. Traffic is holding near baseline but not building.

---

### Launches + efficacy

The launches captured in this window are almost entirely automated operational commits -- daily pulse logs, SEO snapshot records, and a manifest correction. None of these represent new public-facing content. The most substantive technical work in the window was a cluster of Aug 27 commits (DRB-1149/1214) replacing the full tag stack with a single Google tag. That is significant infrastructure work: a tag stack consolidation of that kind can affect attribution, event tracking, and conversion measurement -- and it likely contributed to the anomalous session-duration readings visible Aug 21--26, where average duration swung wildly between 25 seconds and 1,339 seconds on consecutive days. Those readings are not credible as behavioral signals; they are almost certainly measurement artifacts from the tag migration.

The 30-day efficacy rollup shows 8 pages launched and 63 pages modified, with 659,729 GSC impressions and 2,810 clicks attributed across the broader period -- but only one ranking in the top 10 and none in the top 3. That top-10 ranking is a meaningful milestone and worth identifying specifically: which page earned it, what query it ranks for, and whether it is generating click-through at a rate proportionate to its position. The top_launches ledger, as surfaced here, does not include any content pages with measurable SERP positions or GSC impression counts, which suggests the pages doing the organic heavy lifting are not the most recently launched ones -- they are older pages in the 30-day rollup window that have had time to mature.

On backlinks: BCC currently holds 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links, including 5 from .edu sources. No new backlinks were added during this window. Five .edu links is a real asset for domain authority in a healthcare-adjacent space -- but the referring domain count is low enough that one new high-authority link would move the needle noticeably.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Diagnose the GSC data gap before anything else.** Fourteen consecutive days of zero impressions in the pipeline is not a traffic problem -- it is a measurement problem. Check Search Console property verification, confirm the data connector credentials are valid, and determine whether the tag stack consolidation (DRB-1149/1214) disrupted the GSC integration. Until GSC is reporting, organic search health is effectively invisible.

2. **Audit the tag migration's impact on session-duration and conversion tracking.** The wild average-duration swings between Aug 21 and Aug 26 strongly suggest the new Google tag was not firing cleanly during rollout. Pull a pre/post comparison on key events (form submissions, phone clicks, appointment requests) from Aug 26 onward to verify the consolidated tag is capturing conversions accurately. This matters more than any content decision right now.

3. **Identify and invest in the page holding the one top-10 ranking.** The 30-day rollup shows exactly one page ranking in the top 10. That page is the closest thing BCC has to a proven organic asset right now. Strengthen it -- review the meta title and description for CTR optimization, add or tighten internal links pointing to it from related pages, and check whether schema markup is in place.

4. **Build a second .edu backlink into the near-term plan.** Five .edu links suggests at least one institutional relationship already exists. Identify which institution that is, research whether there is a second relevant contact at the same school or a comparable San Antonio-area health or counseling program, and draft a specific outreach rationale. A targeted pitch to one high-quality referrer is more likely to move authority than broad outreach.

5. **Investigate the organic channel share shift before treating it as a win.** If Organic Search is genuinely running at 43% of sessions versus a 25% baseline, that is a real story worth understanding -- which pages, which queries, which geographies. But given the GSC gap, this figure could also reflect misattribution from the tag migration. Resolve the data integrity questions first, then assess whether the organic share is structural or a measurement artifact.
