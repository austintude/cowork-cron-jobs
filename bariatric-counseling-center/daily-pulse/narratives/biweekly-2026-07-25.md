# Biweekly briefing -- 2026-07-25

### The window in one sentence

The 14-day window (July 10--23) was defined by a sharp mid-window spike on July 16--17 followed by a steep drop on July 18--19, with sessions declining 33.6% from first half to second half -- but July 23 produced an extraordinary pageviews anomaly (+1,049% vs. the 28-day median) driven by a same-day batch of 17+ page modifications, suggesting the content push that closed the window may reshape the trajectory heading into next week.

### Volume + composition

Total sessions for the 14-day window came in at 248, averaging 17.7 sessions per day and 16.1 users per day. Pageviews totaled 1,280, but that number is heavily skewed by July 23's 592-pageview day -- strip that outlier and the daily pageview average sits closer to 53, which is in line with the site's 28-day median. GSC impressions and clicks registered as zero across the entire window, which is a data gap to flag: this likely reflects a GSC integration or reporting lag rather than actual zero search visibility, given that the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 350,454 attributed GSC impressions and 1,469 clicks.

Channel composition for the window: Direct led at 113 sessions (46%), Organic Search at 68 (27%), Referral at 46 (19%), Organic Social at 9 (4%), Unassigned at 6 (2%), Email at 8 (3%), and AI Assistant at 3 (1%). Direct is running below BCC's expected ~60% baseline, while Referral is running meaningfully above the expected ~10%. That elevated Referral share is worth watching -- it likely connects to the 4 referring domains and 12 inbound links currently in the backlink profile, including 5 .edu links, which punch above their weight for trust signals. Organic Search at 27% is slightly above baseline and suggests the existing content library is holding its search presence even without new rankings to report.

### Trend (first half vs second half)

The halves data shows a clear decline: 149 sessions in the first half (July 10--16) vs. 99 in the second half (July 17--23), a drop of 33.6%. On sessions alone, this window is declining. The July 16 spike -- sessions up 52.6%, users up 75%, pageviews up 105.7%, and average session duration up a striking 339.5% vs. the 28-day median -- was real engagement, not a bot hit. Someone or some group spent meaningful time on the site that day. Then July 18--19 cratered, with sessions down 59% and 89.7% respectively. The most likely explanation is a one-time traffic source (an untagged email, a social share, or a direct referral) that peaked July 16--17 and exhausted itself. The second half then stabilized at roughly 14--17 sessions per day July 20--23, which is closer to baseline -- except July 23's pageview explosion, which came from 17 page modifications and likely includes significant crawl or preview activity alongside real user sessions. The honest read: the window is declining on sessions but the July 23 content push introduces enough new variables that the second-half number alone does not tell the full story.

### What this means + what to do next

1. **Audit the /nutritional-counseling-in-san-antonio-tx/ title tag and meta description this week.** It has 3,854 impressions and zero clicks -- that is not a ranking problem, it is a snippet problem. Pull the exact queries driving impressions in GSC, identify what intent those queries signal, and rewrite the title and description to match what someone searching those terms actually wants to find. This is the highest-leverage single page in the current launch batch.

2. **Investigate the July 16--17 traffic spike source before it goes cold.** Sessions and engagement spiked meaningfully on those two days and then collapsed. Check GA4's session source/medium breakdown for July 16 specifically. If it was an untagged email or a social share by a staff member or partner, identify it, document the format, and consider whether it can be repeated deliberately.

3. **Confirm GSC data is flowing correctly for the window.** Fourteen consecutive days of zero GSC impressions in the aggregate data -- while the 30-day efficacy rollup shows 350,454 impressions -- suggests a reporting pipeline gap rather than a real traffic collapse. Resolving this is important because the July 23 batch of modifications will need GSC data to evaluate properly within the next 10--14 days.

4. **Build internal links across the July 23 cluster before adding new pages.** Seventeen pages were modified in a single day, many targeting adjacent topics (binge eating therapy, binge eating treatment, eating disorder therapy, eating disorder treatment, compulsive overeating, food addiction). Without strong internal linking connecting these pages to each other and to the homepage, search engines will have difficulty understanding topical relationships and the cluster will underperform relative to its potential. Mapping and adding those links this week costs nothing and compounds over time.

5. **Monitor the homepage modification's effect on sessions and CTR over the next two weeks.** The homepage carries the most historical traffic of any page on the site. Any change to it -- positive or negative -- will show up in sessions and GSC CTR before it shows up anywhere else. Set a specific checkpoint for July 31 and August 7 to compare homepage sessions against the July baseline.
