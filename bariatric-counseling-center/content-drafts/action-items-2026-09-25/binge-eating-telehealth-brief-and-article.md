# Binge Eating Treatment by Telehealth in Texas When Weight Is Part of the Story

Yes, you can get structured binge eating treatment by telehealth anywhere in Texas, including if you have had bariatric surgery, take a weight loss medication, or have lost and regained weight more times than you can count. The Bariatric Counseling Center treats binge eating and weight as one connected problem, in person at our San Antonio office and by telehealth statewide.

That second part matters more than it sounds. Many people with binge eating also carry a long history with their weight, and they want a program that will talk about both honestly.

## When your binge eating and your weight are tangled together

A lot of eating disorder care is built to be weight-neutral, and for many people that is exactly right. But if you are living with obesity, preparing for or recovering from bariatric surgery, or working with a doctor on weight loss medications, you may want a team that can hold both conversations at once: the binge cycle and what drives it, and the medical and practical reality of your weight, without making the number on the scale the whole point.

The Bariatric Counseling Center is a behavioral health facility focused exclusively on the behavioral treatment of obesity. We help people who struggle with weight-loss, obesity, binge eating, and chronic over-eating understand and modify their relationship with food. The program can be standalone behavioral treatment, or in addition to bariatric surgery or weight loss medication from your own medical providers.

## Binge eating after bariatric surgery

Surgery changes how much the stomach can hold. It does not change why, when or how a person eats. For some people, binge eating that was there before surgery comes back afterward in a new shape: grazing through the afternoon and evening, leaning on soft or liquid foods that pass easily, or the same loss of control with a smaller amount of food.

When weight starts to return, many people blame themselves for "failing" the surgery. The surgery did not fail. The behavioral work was never part of the operation. Weight regain after bariatric surgery is common and treatable, and it responds to the same skills that treat binge eating: understanding the triggers, building other ways to cope, and eating regularly enough that the body stops pushing back.

If you are still before surgery, the same logic applies. A pre-surgical psychological evaluation is the moment to name binge eating, not hide it, because it can be treated before the operation.

Always check with your surgeon about anything physical, such as pain, vomiting or trouble keeping food down. Behavioral care works alongside your surgical team, not instead of it.

## Binge eating on or after weight loss medications

Weight loss medications can quiet appetite a great deal. What they do not do is change the reasons food became a way to cope. People with binge eating often notice that episodes shrink but do not stop, that the pattern moves to drinks or grazing, or that the old urges return when a dose is held or the prescription ends.

That return is common. In one large trial, people who stopped a weekly weight loss medication (semaglutide) regained about two-thirds of the weight they had lost within a year (Wilding et al., STEP 1 trial extension, Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism, 2022). [SARA TO CONFIRM use of this citation]

Behavioral treatment is how people build what the medication cannot: skills for cravings and emotions, regular eating routines, and a plan for the day the medication changes or stops. BCC does not prescribe, and we do not tell anyone to start, stay on or stop a medication. That is between you and your doctor. Our registered nurse coordinates with your prescriber so both plans point the same way.

## The lose-and-regain cycle

Some people have never had surgery or medication. They have had diets, many of them, and each one ended the same way. Strict rules, long gaps without eating and "starting over Monday" are some of the most reliable ways to set up the next binge. Breaking the cycle usually means less restriction, not more: regular meals, foods you actually enjoy, and a way to handle the evenings, the stress and the weekends without the old pattern.

## What telehealth treatment looks like at BCC

Care runs through our Intensive Outpatient Program. It lasts about three months and roughly 30 sessions, meeting 2 to 3 times per week, with morning, evening and Saturday tracks. You can attend onsite in San Antonio or join by telehealth from anywhere in Texas.

We provide group and individual therapy, dietary guidance, cooking classes and movement education. Behavioral Therapy & Counseling. Dietary Guidance & Support. Culinary Classes. Mindful Movement. One program, not four.

The staff is comprised of a clinical psychologist, licensed professional counselors, registered dietitians, movement therapists, and a registered nurse, as well as a professional chef. Therapy draws on approaches including CBT and CBT-E, DBT and RO-DBT, and ACT. With your consent, a partner or family member can be part of the work.

If a surgeon or doctor referred you, our team coordinates with them during treatment, and at discharge they receive a detailed summary of your progress.

## Is telehealth a good fit for you?

Telehealth works well for many adults with binge eating: you practice new skills in the same kitchen and the same evenings where the eating actually happens. You need a private space, a device with a camera and a steady connection.

BCC serves adults and is an outpatient program, not inpatient or residential care. If your physical health needs closer monitoring, talk with your doctor about the right level of care first. If you are in crisis, call or text 988.

## Insurance

We accept most health insurances (our claims are processed under patients' mental health benefits). Benefits vary by plan, so our admissions team verifies yours before your first session and tells you what to expect.

## Common questions

### Can I get binge eating treatment by telehealth if I live outside San Antonio?
Yes. BCC's program is available by telehealth to adults anywhere in Texas, with the same team that sees people at our San Antonio office.

### Will you tell me to stop my weight loss medication?
No. BCC does not prescribe, and decisions about starting, staying on or stopping a medication stay between you and your doctor. Our job is the behavioral side, and our nurse coordinates with your prescriber.

### Can you work with my surgeon or my doctor?
Yes. With your consent, our team coordinates care with the medical providers who referred you and sends them a detailed summary of your progress at discharge.
